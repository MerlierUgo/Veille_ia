## Résumé

Long-horizon LLM agents interact with their environments through complete assistant turns while generating each action token by token. We observe that critic values vary primarily across turns, motivating optimization anchored at turns with finer credit allocation among tokens. We propose Multiscale Policy Optimization (MPO), combining Multiscale Advantage Estimation (MAE) and turn-level importance sampling to align credit assignment and policy updates at the turn scale. Extensive experiments across search, tool-use, and automation tasks with 4B and 30B-A3B models demonstrate consistent gains over strong RL baselines.

### 1 Introduction

An LLM agent interacts with its environment through complete assistant turns, such as search queries, tool calls, and final responses, while its policy generates each turn token by token. Learning from a terminal task reward therefore involves two scale choices. Credit must connect the final outcome to interactions across the trajectory and distinguish the tokens within each interaction. Policy updates must also account for changes in complete-output probabilities while retaining those local distinctions. These choices motivate a policy-optimization method that uses both the interaction and generation scales.

Two common families of RL methods leave a gap between these scales. Critic-free, group-relative methods , including GRPO, DAPO, and GSPO [ 30 , 44 , 50 ] , derive one shared advantage per generated sequence from group-relative outcome rewards. Despite differences in their update objectives, this shared credit signal does not distinguish the contributions of individual turns or tokens within a rollout. Critic-based, token-level methods , including PPO, VC-PPO, VAPO, and SAO [ 29 , 45 , 46 , 9 ] , use a learned value function to estimate advantages along the token sequence. Their common formulation treats each generated token as an action, providing finer credit without explicitly separating within-turn generation from turn-level environment interaction. Agentic RL therefore calls for credit assignment that captures both the contribution of each complete interaction and the relative contributions of tokens within it.

> Figure 2: Critic values exhibit structure at the turn scale. A failed trajectory (a) and a successful trajectory (b) both exhibit relatively stable values within turns and pronounced changes across turn boundaries.


##### Observing credit structure across turns.

Figure 2 illustrates learned values that vary within turns and exhibit larger changes across some environment interactions. Across 2,048 trajectories and 16,323 turns from eight checkpoint batches of one search-agent run, differences between turn means account for a median of 89.5% of the critic’s token-weighted within-trajectory value variance. Between-turn variance exceeds within-turn variance in 93.8% of trajectories. This structure motivates propagating long-range credit across turns while retaining local token information. Appendix C provides additional statistics and detailed analyses of critic behavior in multi-turn agent interactions.


##### Multiscale Policy Optimization.

Treating complete assistant turns as macro actions, we establish a turn-level improvement bound and show that token surrogates incur an additional within-turn distribution-shift penalty for exact advantages under the same KL constraints. Guided by this analysis and the observed critic structure, we develop Multiscale Policy Optimization (MPO), combining multiscale credit assignment with turn-level importance weighting. Multiscale Advantage Estimation (MAE) uses turn-level GAE to propagate credit across interactions, then refines token credit through a centered local correction that preserves the turn mean. We derive this allocation as a constrained projection using a single critic. Turn-level importance weighting applies the complete-turn probability ratio while retaining each token’s MAE advantage in its policy gradient through a stop-gradient construction.

Our contributions are threefold:

- • We analyze policy improvement for LLM agents at turn and token scales, deriving a turn-level improvement bound and identifying the additional discrepancy that token-level surrogates introduce through within-turn distribution shift.

- • We propose MPO, which combines multiscale advantage estimation (MAE) with turn-level importance sampling to align credit assignment and policy updates with complete turns while preserving token-specific credit.

- • We demonstrate consistent performance gains over strong RL baselines across search, tool-use, and automation tasks with both 4B and 30B-A3B models.


### 2 Related Work


#### 2.1 Tool use and multi-turn agent learning

Early work connected language-model reasoning with actions and external tools [ 42 , 26 , 31 ] . Reflection, memory, planning, reusable skills, and multi-agent collaboration supported adaptation from experience and sustained interaction [ 33 , 20 , 36 , 39 , 8 ] , establishing core interaction patterns for agents operating over multiple turns.

Modern coding agents and execution harnesses integrate repository editing, code execution, testing, and browsing to resolve software issues and complete practical web and research tasks [ 41 , 49 , 1 , 37 , 34 ] . Coding agents also support algorithm discovery [ 19 ] . Agents also use execution feedback and accumulated experience to refine their tools, workflows, and memory [ 11 , 25 , 40 , 47 , 24 ] . These systems motivate optimizing agents across complete interaction turns and the tokens generated within each turn.


#### 2.2 RL in LLM Posttraining

Classical policy-gradient methods optimize expected return through reward- or advantage-weighted updates [ 38 , 35 ] . Advantage estimation and conservative, trust-region, or proximal updates provide foundations for stable policy optimization [ 14 , 27 , 28 , 29 ] .

Building on GRPO [ 30 ] , DeepSeek-R1 [ 7 ] demonstrated the potential of large-scale RL for reasoning. Subsequent methods improve importance weighting and trust-region control [ 44 , 50 , 5 , 21 ] , as well as critic learning and long-response optimization [ 45 , 46 , 3 , 6 ] . Risk-based objectives further address exploration and solution diversity [ 23 , 12 ] . Policy optimization also targets visually grounded causal reasoning [ 16 ] .

RL also supports multi-turn agents that learn through tool use and environment feedback [ 51 , 13 , 18 ] . Long horizons and sparse task rewards make credit assignment across decisions especially important [ 4 , 2 , 52 , 48 ] . Recent work further develops stable policy updates for asynchronous agent training [ 9 , 10 ] . MPO aligns advantage estimation and importance correction with complete turns, while retaining centered token-level credit within each turn.


### 3 Agentic RL Across Turns and Tokens


#### 3.1 Agentic RL with Terminal Reward

An LLM agent solves a task by alternating between assistant outputs and environment observations. Its interaction trajectory is

[Tableau] $$\tau=(o_{0},a_{0},o_{1},a_{1},\ldots,o_{T},a_{T}).$$ (1)

Here $o_{0}$ is the task prompt, $a_{t}$ is an assistant output such as a tool call or final response, and $o_{t+1}$ is the environment feedback following a nonterminal output. The trajectory ends after $a_{T}$ . Each observation and assistant output is a token sequence:

[Tableau] $$o_{t}=(o_{t,1},\ldots,o_{t,M_{t}}),\qquad a_{t}=(a_{t,1},\ldots,a_{t,L_{t}}),$$ (2)

Here $M_{t}$ and $L_{t}\geq 1$ are the respective sequence lengths, and $t=0,\ldots,T$ indexes assistant turns. Let $h_{t}=(o_{0},a_{0},\ldots,o_{t})$ denote the history at the start of turn $t$ , with $h_{0}=(o_{0})$ . The history before generating token $a_{t,i}$ is

[Tableau] $$h_{t,i}=(h_{t},a_{t,<i}),\qquad a_{t,<i}=(a_{t,1},\ldots,a_{t,i-1}).$$ (3)

The assistant policy factorizes autoregressively as $\pi_{\theta}(a_{t}\mid h_{t})=\prod_{i=1}^{L_{t}}\pi_{\theta}(a_{t,i}\mid h_{t,i})$ . Environment observations follow $o_{t+1}\sim P_{\mathrm{env}}(\cdot\mid h_{t},a_{t})$ and become part of the next assistant turn’s context. The task distribution and environment dynamics are independent of $\theta$ ; together with the policy, they induce the trajectory distribution $p_{\theta}$ .

The agent receives a single reward $R(\tau)$ when the interaction terminates, with no intermediate rewards. Its objective is $\mathcal{J}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim p_{\theta}}[R(\tau)]$ . Only the final assistant token of the entire trajectory receives this reward; every earlier token has zero reward:

[Tableau] $$r_{t,i}:=\begin{cases}R(\tau),&t=T,\ i=L_{T},\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}$$ (4)


#### 3.2 Turns as Macro Decisions

The environment receives complete assistant outputs as actions, while the policy generates each output autoregressively. We treat $a_{t}$ as the macro action and retain $h_{t}$ as its state: the history includes the time index and any remaining interaction or token budget. The turn-ending decision is included in $a_{t}$ . A fixed maximum token length $L_{\max}$ and any forced termination rule are shared by both policies. There is no environment interaction between tokens inside one assistant output. The macro transition is therefore $h_{t}\xrightarrow{a_{t}}h_{t+1}$ , with the same environment law defined above.

We use the original policy notation at both scales:

[Tableau] $$\pi_{\theta}(a_{t}\mid h_{t})=\prod_{i=1}^{L_{t}}\pi_{\theta}(a_{t,i}\mid h_{t,i}).$$ (5)

The complete-turn probability on the left is induced by the token probabilities on the right, using the same model parameters. This factorization lets us evaluate changes in either a complete output or an individual token distribution while retaining one policy. The objective remains

[Tableau] $$\mathcal{J}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim p_{\theta}}[R(\tau)].$$ (6)

When discussing two fixed policies, we also write $\mathcal{J}(\pi)$ and $\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})$ for this same objective.

Under a lossless token refinement, identical reward and stopping rules, and $\gamma=1$ , the macro and token descriptions have the same task return. Moreover,

[Tableau] $$\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(a_{t}\mid h_{t})=\sum_{i=1}^{L_{t}}\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(a_{t,i}\mid h_{t,i}).$$ (7)

The proof is given in Appendix A.1 .

Both descriptions assign the same probability and return to each complete interaction. Their common task objective allows us to compare surrogates built at different scales. The choice of advantage weights and the choice of importance ratios are separate parts of this construction; the following analysis makes the surrogate difference explicit. The theoretical analysis assumes a fixed turn horizon of $T+1$ .


### 4 Policy Improvement Across Turns and Tokens

We analyze how turn-level and token-level surrogates relate to the same task-return objective. Following the surrogate-and-distribution-shift analysis of TRPO [ 27 ] , we first establish a finite-horizon turn-level improvement bound, then identify the additional discrepancy introduced by token surrogates. These results motivate combining multiscale credit assignment with turn-level importance weighting in MPO. Full proofs and the extension to estimated turn advantages are provided in Appendix A .


#### 4.1 Turn-Level Policy Improvement

Fix an old policy $\pi$ and a candidate $\pi^{\prime}$ on the same turn process, with horizon $T+1$ , $\gamma=1$ , and identical initial-history and environment distributions. Define the time-indexed occupancy measure at turn $t$ as $d_{t}^{\pi}(h):=\Pr_{\pi}(h_{t}=h)$ for $t=0,\ldots,T$ , where the probability is taken over the initial task, assistant outputs sampled from $\pi$ , and environment responses. Thus $d_{t}^{\pi}$ is the distribution of histories at the start of turn $t$ . At each active history, define the exact old-policy values by

[Tableau] $\displaystyle V_{t}^{\pi}(h)$ $\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[R(\tau)\mid h_{t}=h],$ $\displaystyle Q_{t}^{\pi}(h,a)$ $\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[R(\tau)\mid h_{t}=h,a_{t}=a],$ $\displaystyle A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a)$ $\displaystyle=Q_{t}^{\pi}(h,a)-V_{t}^{\pi}(h).$ (8)

These expectations mean continuing $\pi$ from the supplied history; the values must also be defined at histories reached by $\pi^{\prime}$ . Set $V_{T+1}^{\pi}=0$ and all post-termination values to zero. Assume all expectations below are finite; bounded task rewards suffice. Common policy support is assumed for importance ratios, but is not needed for the performance-difference or TV inequalities themselves. The Bellman identity gives $\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a)=0$ .

In the finite-horizon setting above, the performance difference satisfies

[Tableau] $$\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}(\pi)=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{t}\sim d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}}\\ a_{t}\sim\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid h_{t})\end{subarray}}\!\left[A_{t}^{\pi}(h_{t},a_{t})\right].$$ (9)

Define the surrogate under the old-policy occupancy measure by $\mathcal{S}_{M}(\pi^{\prime};\pi):=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{d_{t}^{\pi},\pi^{\prime}}[A_{t}^{\pi}]$ . If $\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid h)$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\pi(\cdot\mid h)$ at old-policy histories, then $\mathcal{S}_{M}(\pi^{\prime};\pi)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\sum_{t=0}^{T}(\rho_{t}-1)A_{t}^{\pi}$ , where $\rho_{t}=\pi^{\prime}(a_{t}\mid h_{t})/\pi(a_{t}\mid h_{t})$ is the complete-turn importance ratio.

$\mathcal{S}_{M}$ is an improvement, so $\mathcal{S}_{M}(\pi;\pi)=0$ ; the corresponding absolute surrogate is $\mathcal{J}(\pi)+\mathcal{S}_{M}$ .

The proof is given in Appendix A.1 .

In the finite-horizon setting above, suppose $|A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a)|\leq A_{\max}$ at all admissible turn-start histories and complete outputs. Set

[Tableau] $$\alpha_{M}=\sup_{t,h}D_{\mathrm{TV}}(\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid h),\pi(\cdot\mid h)),$$

where $D_{\mathrm{TV}}(p,q)=\frac{1}{2}\|p-q\|_{1}$ . Here the supremum ranges over turn-start histories, and the distributions are over complete outputs $a_{t}$ . Then

[Tableau] $$\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}(\pi)\geq\mathcal{S}_{M}-2A_{\max}T(T+1)\alpha_{M}^{2}.$$ (10)

Thus a nonnegative right-hand side certifies nondecrease of the task return; a positive right-hand side certifies strict improvement.

Appendices A.1 – A.2 provide the full performance-difference derivation and proof of Theorem 1 .

The bound certifies improvement when the exact surrogate gain covers the distribution-shift penalty. Its surrogate combines a turn-level advantage with a complete-output importance ratio, connecting credit at interaction boundaries to changes in the probability of each turn.


#### 4.2 Additional Cost of Token Surrogates

The autoregressive parameterization supports the macro surrogate through $\rho_{t}=\prod_{i}\rho_{t,i}$ , where $\rho_{t,i}=\pi^{\prime}(a_{t,i}\mid h_{t,i})/\pi(a_{t,i}\mid h_{t,i})$ . The product ratio corrects the entire turn distribution. A separate token ratio corrects only the next-token distribution at an old-policy prefix, so replacing the turn surrogate with token terms changes the prefix distributions used to evaluate credit.

Let $A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}$ be the exact token advantage, and let $A_{\max}^{\mathrm{tok}}<\infty$ be a uniform bound on its absolute value over all admissible token prefixes and next tokens, so that $|A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(h_{t,i},a_{t,i})|\leq A_{\max}^{\mathrm{tok}}$ . Set $\alpha_{T}=\sup_{h}D_{\mathrm{TV}}(\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid h),\pi(\cdot\mid h))$ , where $h$ ranges over token prefixes and the distributions are over next tokens. Define

[Tableau] $$\mathcal{S}_{T}=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\sum_{t}\sum_{i}(\rho_{t,i}-1)A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}.$$

For $L_{t}\leq L_{\max}$ ,

[Tableau] $$|\mathcal{S}_{M}-\mathcal{S}_{T}|\leq 2(T+1)A_{\max}^{\mathrm{tok}}L_{\max}(L_{\max}-1)\alpha_{T}^{2}.$$ (11)

Consequently,

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}(\pi)\geq{}$ $\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{T}-2A_{\max}T(T+1)\alpha_{M}^{2}$ (12) $\displaystyle-2(T+1)A_{\max}^{\mathrm{tok}}L_{\max}(L_{\max}-1)\alpha_{T}^{2}.$

At a fixed macro state, the exact macro advantage telescopes into exact token advantages. The macro surrogate averages them over new-policy prefixes, whereas $\mathcal{S}_{T}$ uses old-policy prefixes. This within-turn distribution shift produces the additional term; Appendix A.3 gives the proof.

The policy-change budgets must also be compared at the same scale. By the KL chain rule, a uniform token KL cap $\delta$ , including the stopping decision, implies

[Tableau] $$D_{\mathrm{KL}}^{\max}(\pi\|\pi^{\prime})\leq L_{\max}\delta,\qquad\alpha_{M}^{2}\leq\frac{L_{\max}\delta}{2}.$$ (13)

For $R\in[0,1]$ , setting $\delta=\Delta/L_{\max}$ certifies a macro KL budget $\Delta$ ; the token bound then carries an additional penalty $(T+1)(L_{\max}-1)\Delta$ .


### 5 Multiscale Policy Optimization

As illustrated in Figure 1 , MPO combines two components motivated by the analysis above. Its advantage estimator, MAE, propagates credit across turns and allocates it among tokens within each turn (Sections 5.1 – 5.2 ). Its policy update uses complete-turn importance weights while preserving each token’s advantage in the gradient (Section 5.3 ).

The two MAE stages reuse the critic $V_{t,i}=V(h_{t,i})$ , whose old-policy rollout predictions remain fixed during advantage estimation. Environment tokens enter the history but are not policy actions. At turn boundaries, set

[Tableau] $$V_{t,L_{t}+1}:=V_{t+1,1}\quad(0\leq t\leq T),\qquad V_{T+1,1}:=0,$$ (14)

with zero values after true termination. Under the terminal reward placement in Eq. ( 4 ) and $\gamma=1$ , define

[Tableau] $$\delta_{t,i}=r_{t,i}+V_{t,i+1}-V_{t,i}.$$ (15)


#### 5.1 Turn-Level Credit Propagation

Summing TD residuals within a turn cancels all internal value predictions:

[Tableau] $$\delta_{t}^{\mathrm{turn}}=\sum_{i=1}^{L_{t}}\delta_{t,i}=\mathbf{1}\{t=T\}R(\tau)+V_{t+1,1}-V_{t,1}.$$ (16)

Applying GAE [ 28 ] on this macro time axis gives

[Tableau] $\displaystyle A_{t}^{\mathrm{turn}}$ $\displaystyle=\delta_{t}^{\mathrm{turn}}+\lambda A_{t+1}^{\mathrm{turn}}$ (17) $\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{T-t}\lambda^{\ell}\delta_{t+\ell}^{\mathrm{turn}},$

where $\lambda\in[0,1]$ and $A_{T+1}^{\mathrm{turn}}=0$ . This shared credit decays once per interaction rather than once per token. For fixed rewards and boundary values, it is independent of turn lengths and internal critic predictions. Appendix A.5 characterizes the corresponding estimation error using only boundary critic errors; the token-GAE comparison is in Appendix A.6 .


#### 5.2 Within-Turn Credit Allocation

To refine the relative token weights, form a local trace restricted to the current turn:

[Tableau] $$A_{t,i}^{\mathrm{intra}}=\sum_{j=i}^{L_{t}}\lambda_{\mathrm{intra}}^{j-i}\delta_{t,j}.$$ (18)

Only the advantage trace is cut at the boundary: the final TD residual still bootstraps from $V_{t+1,1}$ , and equals $R(\tau)-V_{T,L_{T}}$ at trajectory termination.

We combine the turn and local signals according to two design requirements. First, the turn estimate should determine the mean token credit. It propagates task-level credit across interactions using boundary values, so we use it as the shared credit for the complete output. Broadcasting this estimate gives the turn-level score contribution by Eq. ( 7 ); local refinement then adjusts individual token weights around that common level. Directly adding the local trace would also shift the mean by $\beta\overline{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{intra}}$ , where $\overline{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{intra}}=L_{t}^{-1}\sum_{i}A_{t,i}^{\mathrm{intra}}$ . Fixing the mean to $A_{t}^{\mathrm{turn}}$ keeps the local branch from changing this shared credit.

Second, the allocation should retain the relative differences in the local estimates, which provide information for distinguishing tokens within a turn. Broadcasting alone assigns identical weights to all tokens. We therefore seek weights that satisfy the prescribed turn mean while remaining as close as possible to the scaled local signals. Writing $\mathbf{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{intra}}$ for their vector, we express these requirements as a least-squares projection. The squared distance treats all token positions equally and penalizes changes to the local weights:

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{MAE}}$ $\displaystyle=\underset{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{L_{t}}}{\operatorname{argmin}}\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}-\beta\mathbf{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{intra}}\|_{2}^{2},$ (19) $\displaystyle\text{subject to}\quad\frac{1}{L_{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{L_{t}}w_{i}=A_{t}^{\mathrm{turn}},$

where $\beta\geq 0$ scales the local refinement. Solving this strictly convex problem yields the unique allocation

[Tableau] $$\boxed{A_{t,i}^{\mathrm{MAE}}=A_{t}^{\mathrm{turn}}+\beta\bigl(A_{t,i}^{\mathrm{intra}}-\overline{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{intra}}\bigr)}.$$ (20)

Before batch normalization, it satisfies

[Tableau] $$\frac{1}{L_{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{L_{t}}A_{t,i}^{\mathrm{MAE}}=A_{t}^{\mathrm{turn}}.$$ (21)

Centering fixes the average credit to the turn estimate while preserving pairwise differences in the scaled local signals. Setting $\beta=0$ recovers turn broadcasting.


#### 5.3 Turn-Level Importance Correction

Having assigned token credit, we combine it with importance correction at the turn scale. We normalize $A_{t,i}^{\mathrm{MAE}}$ once over valid assistant tokens in the rollout batch and hold the resulting weights $\widehat{A}_{t,i}^{\mathrm{MAE}}$ fixed during the update. Write $\pi_{\theta}$ for the candidate policy and $\pi$ for the fixed rollout policy. The token and complete-turn ratios are

[Tableau] $$\rho_{t,i}(\theta)=\frac{\pi_{\theta}(a_{t,i}\mid h_{t,i})}{\pi(a_{t,i}\mid h_{t,i})},\qquad\rho_{t}(\theta)=\prod_{i=1}^{L_{t}}\rho_{t,i}(\theta).$$ (22)

Multiplying all MAE advantages by the same differentiable $\rho_{t}$ would cancel the centered local correction in the unclipped sum. To retain token-specific credit under complete-turn importance weighting, we stop the gradient through the other token ratios:

[Tableau] $$\widetilde{\rho}_{t,i}(\theta)=\rho_{t,i}(\theta)\,\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\ j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{L_{t}}\rho_{t,j}(\theta)\right],$$ (23)

where $\operatorname{sg}$ preserves its argument in the forward pass and has zero gradient. Thus every token has the complete-turn weight $\widetilde{\rho}_{t,i}(\theta)=\rho_{t}(\theta)$ in the forward pass, while

[Tableau] $$\nabla_{\theta}\widetilde{\rho}_{t,i}(\theta)=\rho_{t}(\theta)\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(a_{t,i}\mid h_{t,i}).$$ (24)

This construction weights each token’s score gradient by the complete turn ratio and its own MAE advantage. The MPO update is defined by the clipped surrogate

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MPO}}(\theta)$ $\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{t,i}\Bigl[\min\bigl(\widetilde{\rho}_{t,i}(\theta)\widehat{A}_{t,i}^{\mathrm{MAE}},$ (25) $\displaystyle\operatorname{clip}\bigl(\widetilde{\rho}_{t,i}(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon\bigr)\widehat{A}_{t,i}^{\mathrm{MAE}}\bigr)\Bigr],$

where $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{t,i}$ is the empirical mean over all valid assistant tokens in the rollout batch and $\epsilon>0$ is the clipping threshold. Gradient ascent uses the stop-gradient rule in Eq. ( 23 ); the full turn ratio therefore controls the forward weighting and clipping, while each token’s MAE advantage controls its own score gradient.

Following VAPO’s decoupled GAE [ 46 ] , we set $\lambda_{\mathrm{critic}}=1$ , so the critic directly regresses the terminal return $R(\tau)$ .


### 6 Experiments


#### 6.1 Experimental Settings

We evaluate Qwen3-4B-Thinking and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct [ 22 ] in three agentic settings: Search, Claw-Eval, and Automation Bench. The main comparisons use separate training in each setting, with 300 steps for Search, 300 for Claw-Eval, and 200 for Automation Bench. We compare MPO with the initial checkpoint (Base), GRPO [ 30 ] , GSPO [ 50 ] , PPO [ 29 ] , VAPO [ 46 ] , and SAO [ 9 ] . To examine the update objective, MPO w/ token IS retains MAE but replaces complete-turn importance ratios with token-level ratios.


##### Evaluation protocol.

For the trained-model results in all tables, we select the best-performing checkpoint from each training run. We evaluate the selected checkpoint offline five times and report the mean of the five evaluation scores. The benchmark-specific rollout budgets and metrics described below apply to each evaluation repetition. For BrowseComp-Plus, we follow the held-out evaluation setup of Lu et al. [17] . For GAIA and HLE, we use WebThinker’s text-only evaluation subsets, drawn from their validation and test sets, respectively [ 15 ] . Base results use three rollouts per task. For trained Search models, pass@1 and pass@3 are estimated from a pool of 32 independent rollouts per task, using the same samples across evaluation budgets. We follow the original evaluation protocols for Claw-Eval [ 43 ] and Automation Bench [ 32 ] . Claw-Eval uses three independent trials, reporting pass@3 for success in at least one trial and pass 3 for success in all three. Automation Bench reports pass@1 using its end-state assertion checks. All score differences below are percentage points.

##### Performance across tasks and model scales.

Table 1 shows that MPO achieves the highest reported score on all nine metrics at both model sizes. Relative to the strongest non-MPO baseline in each column, MPO improves BrowseComp-Plus, GAIA, and HLE pass@1 by 4.33, 3.85, and 0.66 points on 4B, and by 3.59, 3.80, and 0.12 points on 30A3B. The gains extend to pass@3, including improvements of 5.11 and 5.90 points on GAIA at the two scales. MPO also improves both Claw-Eval metrics and Automation Bench performance. For example, Claw-Eval pass 3 increases by 0.95 and 1.12 points, showing that the improvements extend to success across repeated attempts.


##### Aligning advantage estimation and importance correction.

Replacing turn-level ratios with token-level ratios reduces every reported score. The Search pass@1 drops on BrowseComp-Plus, GAIA, and HLE are 2.36, 3.12, and 1.03 points for 4B, and 2.05, 2.19, and 0.58 points for 30A3B. With token IS, MAE still outperforms PPO on all six Search metrics at both scales, indicating that multiscale credit assignment contributes useful gains on its own. Complete-turn importance correction provides a further improvement. Together, these comparisons support aligning shared credit and probability correction with the turn-level interaction objective, while retaining local token distinctions in the policy gradient. ##### Critic fit under the same regression target.

PPO and MPO both train the critic against terminal returns with $\lambda_{\mathrm{critic}}=1$ , yet MPO exhibits higher explained variance on average during late training at both model scales. A plausible explanation is that turn-aligned policy updates change the visited histories and subsequent outcomes, making their return structure easier for the critic to capture. Better value estimates may in turn improve the actor’s credit signal. This interpretation concerns the interaction between policy learning and critic fitting: explained variance is measured on each policy’s own rollouts and also depends on return variance, so it does not isolate a causal improvement in critic accuracy.

##### Turn-scale propagation, $\lambda$ .

The intermediate setting $\lambda=0.80$ performs best among the tested values. Increasing it to 0.85 reduces BrowseComp-Plus, GAIA, and HLE pass@1 by 1.72, 2.21, and 1.05 points, respectively, and also reduces all three pass@3 scores. Moving closer to one thus hurts performance over this part of the tested range, consistent with a longer trace carrying more variability from distant interactions. Reducing $\lambda$ to 0.60 also lowers performance, suggesting that shortening the trace too much discards useful cross-turn credit. The results favor balancing these effects rather than maximizing the propagation range.


##### Within-turn trace, $\lambda_{\mathrm{intra}}$ .

Reducing $\lambda_{\mathrm{intra}}$ from 0.95 to 0.90 lowers pass@1 by 1.83, 1.55, and 1.77 points on BrowseComp-Plus, GAIA, and HLE, and lowers pass@3 on every benchmark. This shorter trace limits how far local TD information reaches within a generated turn. Setting $\lambda_{\mathrm{intra}}=1$ also reduces all six scores relative to 0.95, including a 0.41-point drop in BrowseComp-Plus pass@1. Thus, 0.95 performs best on all six metrics in this sweep.


##### Different decay rates for different time scales.

The two coefficients count different events: $\lambda$ decays once per interaction, whereas $\lambda_{\mathrm{intra}}$ decays once per generated token. A trajectory contains far fewer interactions than tokens, and each interaction itself requires many generated tokens. Using the same coefficient would therefore impose very different credit ranges. For example, the nominal geometric trace length $1/(1-\lambda)$ is five turns at $\lambda=0.80$ , while $\lambda_{\mathrm{intra}}=0.95$ gives twenty tokens; reducing the latter to 0.90 halves that local range. A smaller turn coefficient can therefore retain credit across several interactions, while a larger local coefficient avoids restricting allocation to only a few tokens. This difference motivates tuning the two scales separately and explains our choice of $\lambda<\lambda_{\mathrm{intra}}$ : multiscale policy optimization uses distinct propagation ranges for environment interaction and autoregressive generation.


##### Local refinement strength, $\beta$ .

Setting $\beta=0$ removes within-turn credit allocation from MAE, leaving only turn-level GAE: every token in a turn receives the same turn advantage. Even without within-turn allocation, this turn-level variant outperforms the standard PPO baseline with token-level GAE in Table 1 on all six Search metrics. Its pass@1 gains over PPO are 0.95, 2.49, and 0.66 percentage points on BrowseComp-Plus, GAIA, and HLE, respectively. However, removing local allocation still substantially reduces performance relative to full MPO (Table 3 ). Compared with $\beta=1.00$ , pass@1 drops by 6.28, 5.27, and 2.04 percentage points on BrowseComp-Plus, GAIA, and HLE, respectively, and pass@3 also decreases on all three benchmarks. These results show that allocating credit within a turn provides benefits beyond propagating credit across turns alone. Introducing local refinement with $\beta=0.50$ partially recovers the loss, while $\beta=1.00$ performs best on all six metrics. Increasing $\beta$ further to 1.50 and then 2.00 progressively reduces both pass@1 and pass@3 across all three benchmarks. Since the local correction is centered, these changes preserve the turn’s mean advantage while amplifying differences among its tokens. The results therefore support within-turn credit allocation with a moderate refinement strength; removing it or giving it excessive weight both hurt performance.


### 7 Conclusion

We introduced MPO to organize LLM-agent policy optimization across turns and tokens. Its MAE component combines cross-turn credit propagation with mean-preserving local allocation, and its update aligns complete-turn importance correction with token-specific gradients. Our analysis motivates these scale choices, while experiments show gains across Search and agent tasks and continued benefits from increased sampling. The ablations support treating interaction and generation as distinct credit horizons. Together, these findings motivate using both scales when optimizing long-horizon LLM agents.

### Appendix A Proofs of the Policy-Improvement Guarantees


#### A.1 Objective Consistency and the Macro PDL

Fix a complete interaction $\tau$ . Its probability is the initial-task probability, multiplied by the probabilities of the generated assistant tokens and the intervening environment responses. Grouping the assistant factors within each turn gives

[Tableau] $$p_{\theta}(\tau)=p(o_{0})\prod_{t=0}^{T}\prod_{i=1}^{L_{t}}\pi_{\theta}(a_{t,i}\mid h_{t,i})\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}P_{\mathrm{env}}(o_{t+1}\mid h_{t},a_{t})=p(o_{0})\prod_{t=0}^{T}\pi_{\theta}(a_{t}\mid h_{t})\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}P_{\mathrm{env}}(o_{t+1}\mid h_{t},a_{t}).$$

Thus the token and macro descriptions induce the same trajectory law. They also assign the same return to each trajectory: $\gamma=1$ , the reward is unchanged, and the same stopping rule determines termination. Their expected task returns are consequently equal.

For a fixed complete output of positive probability, the finite-product factorization gives

[Tableau] $$\log\pi_{\theta}(a_{t}\mid h_{t})=\sum_{i=1}^{L_{t}}\log\pi_{\theta}(a_{t,i}\mid h_{t,i}).$$

Differentiating this finite sum proves Eq. ( 7 ), wherever the token probabilities are differentiable. For variable-length outputs, the stopping decision is one of these factors; when differentiating a fixed output its length is fixed. Deterministic forced stopping contributes a factor of one and a zero score. ∎

For each supplied history, the values in Eq. ( 8 ) use the old policy for all subsequent decisions. They are defined throughout the histories relevant to either policy, not merely at the histories observed in a sampled batch. At termination let $h_{T+1}$ be an absorbing history with value zero. The reward law and the law of $h_{t+1}$ given $(h_{t},a_{t})$ are common to both policies. Consequently the exact values satisfy

[Tableau] $\displaystyle Q_{t}^{\pi}(h,a)$ $\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{L_{t}}r_{t,i}+V_{t+1}^{\pi}(h_{t+1})\,\middle|\,h_{t}=h,a_{t}=a\right],$ (26) $\displaystyle V_{t}^{\pi}(h)$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid h)Q_{t}^{\pi}(h,a),\qquad\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid h)A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a)=0.$ (27)

Here $\sum_{i}r_{t,i}$ is zero except at the actual terminal turn, where it equals $R(\tau)$ ; no intermediate reward is introduced. All expectations are assumed finite, as they are for bounded rewards.

We next relate these conditional identities to the full return. Define the one-turn temporal difference

[Tableau] $$\delta_{t}^{\pi}=\sum_{i=1}^{L_{t}}r_{t,i}+V_{t+1}^{\pi}(h_{t+1})-V_{t}^{\pi}(h_{t}).$$

Although actions are sampled under $\pi^{\prime}$ , conditioning on the current history and complete output fixes the environment’s next-step law. Equation ( 26 ) therefore gives

[Tableau] $$\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}[\delta_{t}^{\pi}\mid h_{t},a_{t}]=Q_{t}^{\pi}(h_{t},a_{t})-V_{t}^{\pi}(h_{t})=A_{t}^{\pi}(h_{t},a_{t}).$$ (28)

For each realized trajectory the value terms telescope, since $\gamma=1$ :

[Tableau] $$\sum_{t=0}^{T}\delta_{t}^{\pi}=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{L_{t}}r_{t,i}+V_{T+1}^{\pi}(h_{T+1})-V_{0}^{\pi}(h_{0})=R(\tau)-V_{0}^{\pi}(h_{0}).$$

Under a trajectory sampled from $\pi^{\prime}$ , the marginal law of $(h_{t},a_{t})$ is $d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}}(h_{t})\pi^{\prime}(a_{t}\mid h_{t})$ . Thus the conditional identity in Eq. ( 28 ) gives the following chain:

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}},\pi^{\prime}}[A_{t}^{\pi}]$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi^{\prime}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}[\delta_{t}^{\pi}\mid h_{t},a_{t}]\right]$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi^{\prime}}[\delta_{t}^{\pi}]$ $\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi^{\prime}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\delta_{t}^{\pi}\right]$ $\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi^{\prime}}[R(\tau)-V_{0}^{\pi}(h_{0})]$ $\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathbb{E}_{h_{0}\sim d_{0}^{\pi^{\prime}}}[V_{0}^{\pi}(h_{0})].$

The second equality is the tower property; the third exchanges a finite sum and expectation; the fourth uses the pathwise telescoping identity.

Both policies start from the same initial-history distribution, so $d_{0}^{\pi^{\prime}}=d_{0}^{\pi}$ . By the definition of the exact old-policy value, $V_{0}^{\pi}(h_{0})=\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi}[R(\tau)\mid h_{0}]$ . Hence

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{h_{0}\sim d_{0}^{\pi^{\prime}}}[V_{0}^{\pi}(h_{0})]$ $\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{h_{0}\sim d_{0}^{\pi}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi}[R(\tau)\mid h_{0}]\right]$ $\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi}[R(\tau)]=\mathcal{J}(\pi).$

Only the initial-history distributions are identified here; the full trajectory laws under $\pi^{\prime}$ and $\pi$ are not interchanged. Substituting this equality into the preceding chain yields

[Tableau] $$\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}},\pi^{\prime}}[A_{t}^{\pi}]=\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}(\pi).$$

This proves the PDL in Eq. ( 9 ); it does not replace any new-policy state distribution by an old-policy one.

It remains to establish the importance-ratio representation. Replacing only $d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}}$ by $d_{t}^{\pi}$ in the PDL defines $\mathcal{S}_{M}$ ; the resulting approximation error is retained below. For its ratio representation, common support gives, at a fixed $h$ ,

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid h)\left(\frac{\pi^{\prime}(a\mid h)}{\pi(a\mid h)}-1\right)A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a)$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\pi^{\prime}(a\mid h)A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a)-\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid h)A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a)$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\pi^{\prime}(a\mid h)A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a).$

Average over $d_{t}^{\pi}$ and sum over $t$ to obtain the importance-ratio representation in Lemma 2 . Absolute continuity of the new action law with respect to the old one suffices for this step; the preceding PDL and the TV argument below do not require it.

∎


#### A.2 Turn-Level Policy Improvement

Sums below are over complete outputs and token-sequence histories; the same argument uses integrals for general observation spaces.

We first bound the difference between the turn-start occupancy measures. Given a history $h$ and a complete output $a$ , the next observation is sampled from the same environment distribution $P_{\mathrm{env}}(o\mid h,a)$ under both policies. The next history is obtained by appending $a$ and $o$ to $h$ . Thus the joint probabilities of $(h,a,o)$ are $d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}}(h)\pi^{\prime}(a\mid h)P_{\mathrm{env}}(o\mid h,a)$ and $d_{t}^{\pi}(h)\pi(a\mid h)P_{\mathrm{env}}(o\mid h,a)$ , respectively. Summing these probabilities over triples that produce the same next history and applying the triangle inequality gives, for $0\leq t<T$ ,

[Tableau] $\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{TV}}(d_{t+1}^{\pi^{\prime}},d_{t+1}^{\pi})$ $\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{h,a,o}\left|d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}}(h)\pi^{\prime}(a\mid h)-d_{t}^{\pi}(h)\pi(a\mid h)\right|P_{\mathrm{env}}(o\mid h,a)$ $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{h,a}\left|d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}}(h)\pi^{\prime}(a\mid h)-d_{t}^{\pi}(h)\pi(a\mid h)\right|$ $\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{h,a}d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}}(h)|\pi^{\prime}(a\mid h)-\pi(a\mid h)|+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{h,a}|d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}}(h)-d_{t}^{\pi}(h)|\pi(a\mid h)$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{h}d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}}(h)D_{\mathrm{TV}}(\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid h),\pi(\cdot\mid h))+D_{\mathrm{TV}}(d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}},d_{t}^{\pi})$ $\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{M}+D_{\mathrm{TV}}(d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}},d_{t}^{\pi}).$ (29)

The first equality uses $\sum_{o}P_{\mathrm{env}}(o\mid h,a)=1$ . The second inequality adds and subtracts $d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}}(h)\pi(a\mid h)$ inside the absolute value. The resulting terms separate the change in the action distribution from the existing difference in occupancy measures. Since $d_{0}^{\pi^{\prime}}=d_{0}^{\pi}$ , induction yields

[Tableau] $$D_{\mathrm{TV}}(d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}},d_{t}^{\pi})\leq t\alpha_{M},\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.$$ (30)

In particular, the first turn contributes no occupancy-measure error.

Next, the exact old-policy advantage has zero mean under $\pi$ . Using Eq. ( 27 ), for every history $h$ ,

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid h)}[A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a)]\right|$ $\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{a}[\pi^{\prime}(a\mid h)-\pi(a\mid h)]A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a)\right|$ $\displaystyle\leq A_{\max}\sum_{a}|\pi^{\prime}(a\mid h)-\pi(a\mid h)|\leq 2A_{\max}\alpha_{M}.$ (31)

Finally, subtracting the surrogate from the performance-difference identity in Lemma 2 gives the exact relation

[Tableau] $$\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}(\pi)-\mathcal{S}_{M}=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\sum_{h}[d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}}(h)-d_{t}^{\pi}(h)]\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid h)}[A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a)].$$

Taking absolute values and applying Eqs. ( 31 ) and ( 30 ), in that order, gives

[Tableau] $\displaystyle|\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}(\pi)-\mathcal{S}_{M}|$ $\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T}\sum_{h}|d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}}(h)-d_{t}^{\pi}(h)|\left|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid h)}[A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a)]\right|$ $\displaystyle\leq 2A_{\max}\alpha_{M}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\sum_{h}|d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}}(h)-d_{t}^{\pi}(h)|$ $\displaystyle=4A_{\max}\alpha_{M}\sum_{t=0}^{T}D_{\mathrm{TV}}(d_{t}^{\pi^{\prime}},d_{t}^{\pi})$ $\displaystyle\leq 4A_{\max}\alpha_{M}^{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T}t=2A_{\max}T(T+1)\alpha_{M}^{2}.$ (32)

The equality uses $D_{\mathrm{TV}}(p,q)=\frac{1}{2}\|p-q\|_{1}$ , and the last step uses $\sum_{t=0}^{T}t=T(T+1)/2$ . Taking the lower side of this absolute-error bound gives Theorem 1 .

∎

For $T=0$ , the initial occupancy measures coincide and the remainder is zero. For $\alpha_{M}=0$ , both the surrogate gain and the remainder are zero. The condition in the theorem is sufficient for improvement; it is not necessary.


#### A.3 Within-Turn Surrogate Shift and KL Conversion

Fix a turn $t$ , its starting history $h_{t}=h$ , and a complete output $a_{t}$ . At an active token prefix $x$ , write $V^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[R(\tau)\mid h_{t,i}=x]$ for the expected return from continuing $\pi$ . Thus $V^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(h_{t,1})=V_{t}^{\pi}(h)$ . The exact token advantage is the expected return after fixing the next token, minus this value. For $i<L_{t}$ , appending $a_{t,i}$ deterministically gives $h_{t,i+1}$ , with no reward or environment interaction. Hence

[Tableau] $$A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(h_{t,i},a_{t,i})=V^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(h_{t,i+1})-V^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(h_{t,i}).$$

At the last token, the complete macro action has been specified, so

[Tableau] $\displaystyle A_{t,L_{t}}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(h_{t,L_{t}},a_{t,L_{t}})$ $\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{L_{t}}r_{t,i}+V_{t+1}^{\pi}(h_{t+1})\,\middle|\,h_{t},a_{t}\right]-V^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(h_{t,L_{t}})$ $\displaystyle=Q_{t}^{\pi}(h_{t},a_{t})-V^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(h_{t,L_{t}}).$

The conditional expectation averages over the environment response, and the second equality is Eq. ( 26 ). Summing over the tokens cancels each internal value:

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{L_{t}}A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(h_{t,i},a_{t,i})$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{L_{t}-1}\bigl[V^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(h_{t,i+1})-V^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(h_{t,i})\bigr]+Q_{t}^{\pi}(h_{t},a_{t})-V^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(h_{t,L_{t}})$ (33) $\displaystyle=Q_{t}^{\pi}(h_{t},a_{t})-V_{t}^{\pi}(h_{t})=A_{t}^{\pi}(h_{t},a_{t}).$

This identity holds for every complete output.

We next bound the difference between the within-turn occupancy measures. Pad a stopped output to $L_{\max}$ positions with deterministic zero-advantage actions. Extend the occupancy notation by writing $d_{t,i}^{\pi}(\cdot\mid h)$ for the distribution of the token prefix $h_{t,i}$ when the turn starts at $h$ and its tokens are sampled under $\pi$ ; define $d_{t,i}^{\pi^{\prime}}(\cdot\mid h)$ in the same way. These are conditional occupancy measures within a fixed turn: both start from the same $h$ , regardless of how that history was reached. In the sums below, $x$ denotes a token prefix and $a$ a single token.

Each next prefix is obtained by sampling one token and appending it. As in Eq. ( 29 ), adding and subtracting $d_{t,i}^{\pi^{\prime}}(x\mid h)\pi(a\mid x)$ gives

[Tableau] $\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(d_{t,i+1}^{\pi^{\prime}}(\cdot\mid h),d_{t,i+1}^{\pi}(\cdot\mid h)\bigr)$ $\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,a}\left|d_{t,i}^{\pi^{\prime}}(x\mid h)\pi^{\prime}(a\mid x)-d_{t,i}^{\pi}(x\mid h)\pi(a\mid x)\right|$ $\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,a}d_{t,i}^{\pi^{\prime}}(x\mid h)|\pi^{\prime}(a\mid x)-\pi(a\mid x)|$ $\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,a}|d_{t,i}^{\pi^{\prime}}(x\mid h)-d_{t,i}^{\pi}(x\mid h)|\pi(a\mid x)$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{x}d_{t,i}^{\pi^{\prime}}(x\mid h)D_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid x),\pi(\cdot\mid x)\bigr)$ $\displaystyle\quad+D_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(d_{t,i}^{\pi^{\prime}}(\cdot\mid h),d_{t,i}^{\pi}(\cdot\mid h)\bigr)$ $\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{T}+D_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(d_{t,i}^{\pi^{\prime}}(\cdot\mid h),d_{t,i}^{\pi}(\cdot\mid h)\bigr).$

The equality uses $\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid x)=1$ and the definition of total variation. At $i=1$ , both occupancy measures concentrate on $h$ , so their difference is zero. Iterating the bound $i-1$ times gives

[Tableau] $$D_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(d_{t,i}^{\pi^{\prime}}(\cdot\mid h),d_{t,i}^{\pi}(\cdot\mid h)\bigr)\leq(i-1)\alpha_{T}.$$ (34)

Padded positions have identical deterministic transitions and also satisfy the one-step bound.

We next bound the mean old-policy token advantage under $\pi^{\prime}$ . At each prefix $x$ , the exact advantage satisfies $\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid x)A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(x,a)=0$ ; this also holds at padded positions, where the advantage is zero. Consequently,

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid x)}[A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(x,a)]\right|$ $\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{a}[\pi^{\prime}(a\mid x)-\pi(a\mid x)]A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(x,a)\right|$ $\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a}|\pi^{\prime}(a\mid x)-\pi(a\mid x)||A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(x,a)|$ $\displaystyle\leq A_{\max}^{\mathrm{tok}}\sum_{a}|\pi^{\prime}(a\mid x)-\pi(a\mid x)|$ $\displaystyle=2A_{\max}^{\mathrm{tok}}D_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid x),\pi(\cdot\mid x)\bigr)$ $\displaystyle\leq 2A_{\max}^{\mathrm{tok}}\alpha_{T}.$ (35)

The first equality subtracts the zero mean under $\pi$ . The first inequality is the triangle inequality, and the second uses the uniform bound $|A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(x,a)|\leq A_{\max}^{\mathrm{tok}}$ . The next equality is the definition of total variation; the final inequality uses $\alpha_{T}$ as its supremum over all token prefixes.

Finally, express both surrogates using these conditional occupancy measures. By the definition of $\mathcal{S}_{M}$ and Eq. ( 33 ),

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{M}$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{h\sim d_{t}^{\pi}}\mathbb{E}_{a_{t}\sim\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid h)}[A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a_{t})]$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{h\sim d_{t}^{\pi}}\mathbb{E}_{a_{t}\sim\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid h)}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{L_{\max}}A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(h_{t,i},a_{t,i})\right]$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{h\sim d_{t}^{\pi}}\sum_{i=1}^{L_{\max}}\sum_{x}d_{t,i}^{\pi^{\prime}}(x\mid h)\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid x)}[A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(x,a)].$

The last equality conditions each summand on its prefix: under $\pi^{\prime}$ , the prefix has occupancy measure $d_{t,i}^{\pi^{\prime}}(\cdot\mid h)$ and the next token has distribution $\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid x)$ . For $\mathcal{S}_{T}$ , prefixes are sampled under $\pi$ . At a fixed prefix, the importance ratio changes only the distribution of the next token:

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot\mid x)}[(\rho_{t,i}-1)A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(x,a)]$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid x)\left(\frac{\pi^{\prime}(a\mid x)}{\pi(a\mid x)}-1\right)A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(x,a)$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{a}[\pi^{\prime}(a\mid x)-\pi(a\mid x)]A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(x,a)$ $\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid x)}[A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(x,a)].$

The ratio is well defined by the support assumption, and the last equality uses the zero old-policy mean. Conditioning $\mathcal{S}_{T}$ on the turn-start history and then on the token prefix gives

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{T}$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{h\sim d_{t}^{\pi}}\sum_{i=1}^{L_{\max}}\sum_{x}d_{t,i}^{\pi}(x\mid h)\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot\mid x)}[(\rho_{t,i}-1)A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(x,a)]$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{h\sim d_{t}^{\pi}}\sum_{i=1}^{L_{\max}}\sum_{x}d_{t,i}^{\pi}(x\mid h)\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid x)}[A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(x,a)].$

Both expressions average turn-start histories under $d_{t}^{\pi}$ ; only the conditional within-turn occupancy measures differ. Subtracting gives

[Tableau] $$\mathcal{S}_{M}-\mathcal{S}_{T}=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{h\sim d_{t}^{\pi}}\sum_{i=1}^{L_{\max}}\sum_{x}[d_{t,i}^{\pi^{\prime}}(x\mid h)-d_{t,i}^{\pi}(x\mid h)]\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid x)}[A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(x,a)].$$

Taking absolute values, applying Eq. ( 35 ), and then using Eq. ( 34 ) yields

[Tableau] $\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}_{M}-\mathcal{S}_{T}|$ $\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{h\sim d_{t}^{\pi}}\sum_{i=1}^{L_{\max}}\sum_{x}|d_{t,i}^{\pi^{\prime}}(x\mid h)-d_{t,i}^{\pi}(x\mid h)|\left|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid x)}[A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(x,a)]\right|$ $\displaystyle\leq 2A_{\max}^{\mathrm{tok}}\alpha_{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{h\sim d_{t}^{\pi}}\sum_{i=1}^{L_{\max}}\sum_{x}|d_{t,i}^{\pi^{\prime}}(x\mid h)-d_{t,i}^{\pi}(x\mid h)|$ $\displaystyle=4A_{\max}^{\mathrm{tok}}\alpha_{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{h\sim d_{t}^{\pi}}\sum_{i=1}^{L_{\max}}D_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(d_{t,i}^{\pi^{\prime}}(\cdot\mid h),d_{t,i}^{\pi}(\cdot\mid h)\bigr)$ $\displaystyle\leq 4A_{\max}^{\mathrm{tok}}\alpha_{T}^{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{L_{\max}}(i-1)$ $\displaystyle=2(T+1)A_{\max}^{\mathrm{tok}}L_{\max}(L_{\max}-1)\alpha_{T}^{2}.$

The first equality uses $\sum_{x}|p(x)-q(x)|=2D_{\mathrm{TV}}(p,q)$ ; the last uses $\sum_{i=1}^{L_{\max}}(i-1)=L_{\max}(L_{\max}-1)/2$ . This proves Eq. ( 11 ). Applying Theorem 1 and then this bound gives

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}(\pi)$ $\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{S}_{M}-2A_{\max}T(T+1)\alpha_{M}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{S}_{T}-|\mathcal{S}_{M}-\mathcal{S}_{T}|-2A_{\max}T(T+1)\alpha_{M}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{S}_{T}-2A_{\max}T(T+1)\alpha_{M}^{2}-2(T+1)A_{\max}^{\mathrm{tok}}L_{\max}(L_{\max}-1)\alpha_{T}^{2},$

which is Eq. ( 12 ). ∎

Fix $h$ and include the stopping decision in the complete output. Since $L(a)\leq L_{\max}$ , the log ratio is a bounded-length sum over generated tokens. Conditioning each term on its prefix gives

[Tableau] $\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi(\cdot\mid h)\|\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid h))$ $\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot\mid h)}\sum_{i=1}^{L(a)}\log\frac{\pi(a_{i}\mid h_{i})}{\pi^{\prime}(a_{i}\mid h_{i})}$ (36) $\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot\mid h)}\sum_{i=1}^{L(a)}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi(\cdot\mid h_{i})\|\pi^{\prime}(\cdot\mid h_{i}))\leq L_{\max}\delta.$

Here logarithms are natural and the assumed finite token KL cap makes the required absolute continuity explicit. Taking the supremum over $h$ and applying Pinsker’s inequality gives $\alpha_{M}^{2}\leq L_{\max}\delta/2$ ; at token prefixes it likewise gives $\alpha_{T}^{2}\leq\delta/2$ .

When $R\in[0,1]$ , all exact remaining-return values lie in $[0,1]$ , so $A_{\max}=A_{\max}^{\mathrm{tok}}=1$ is valid. Writing $L=L_{\max}$ and substituting the two TV bounds into the macro and token bounds gives

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}(\pi)$ $\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{S}_{M}-T(T+1)L\delta,$ (37) $\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}(\pi)$ $\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{S}_{T}-T(T+1)L\delta-(T+1)L(L-1)\delta.$ (38)

For $\delta=\Delta/L$ , the last penalty is $(T+1)(L-1)\Delta$ , as claimed in the main text. ∎


#### A.4 Arbitrary Estimators and Action-Independent Baselines

Let $\widehat{A}_{t}$ be any integrable scalar macro estimate computed on old-policy rollouts, with its construction fixed during the update. Assume all displayed importance-weighted expectations are finite. Define its conditional mean error and population estimated improvement by

[Tableau] $\displaystyle b_{t}(h,a)$ $\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\widehat{A}_{t}\mid h_{t}=h,a_{t}=a]-A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a),$ (39) $\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{M}$ $\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\sum_{t=0}^{T}(\rho_{t}-1)\widehat{A}_{t}.$ (40)

The expectation includes future rollout randomness. The hat denotes an estimated advantage, not an empirical average.

Under Theorem 1 , suppose state-only functions $c_{t}$ satisfy $|b_{t}(h,a)-c_{t}(h)|\leq\epsilon_{A,t}$ uniformly. Then

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}(\pi)\geq{}$ $\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{M}-2A_{\max}T(T+1)\alpha_{M}^{2}$ (41) $\displaystyle-2\alpha_{M}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\epsilon_{A,t}.$

In particular, a common bound $\epsilon_{A}$ gives an estimation penalty $2(T+1)\alpha_{M}\epsilon_{A}$ .

The estimator is computed under the old-policy rollout law, and $\rho_{t}-1$ is a function of $(h_{t},a_{t})$ . Conditioning on that pair gives

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{M}-\mathcal{S}_{M}$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[(\rho_{t}-1)(\widehat{A}_{t}-A_{t}^{\pi}(h_{t},a_{t}))]$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{h\sim d_{t}^{\pi}}\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid h)\left(\frac{\pi^{\prime}(a\mid h)}{\pi(a\mid h)}-1\right)b_{t}(h,a)$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{h\sim d_{t}^{\pi}}\sum_{a}[\pi^{\prime}(a\mid h)-\pi(a\mid h)]b_{t}(h,a).$

The integrability assumptions justify these conditional expectations. For each fixed $h$ , the state-only part cancels because

[Tableau] $$\sum_{a}[\pi^{\prime}(a\mid h)-\pi(a\mid h)]c_{t}(h)=c_{t}(h)(1-1)=0.$$

Subtracting this zero term before taking absolute values yields

[Tableau] $\displaystyle|\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{M}-\mathcal{S}_{M}|$ $\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{h\sim d_{t}^{\pi}}\sum_{a}|\pi^{\prime}(a\mid h)-\pi(a\mid h)|\,|b_{t}(h,a)-c_{t}(h)|$ $\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T}2\alpha_{M}\epsilon_{A,t}.$

Therefore $\mathcal{S}_{M}\geq\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{M}-2\alpha_{M}\sum_{t}\epsilon_{A,t}$ . Substitution into Eq. ( 10 ) proves the theorem. If all $\epsilon_{A,t}\leq\epsilon_{A}$ , summing $T+1$ terms gives the stated uniform-error penalty. ∎


#### A.5 Boundary Critic Error and the Turn-Credit Guarantee

For the turn GAE in Eq. ( 17 ), write $e_{t}(h_{t})=V_{t,1}-V_{t}^{\pi}(h_{t})$ for the boundary critic error.

Under the assumptions of Theorem 3 , suppose rollouts reach true termination, $e_{T+1}=0$ , $\lambda\in[0,1]$ , and $|e_{t}(h)|\leq\epsilon_{B}$ . Then one may take

[Tableau] $$\epsilon_{A,t}=\epsilon_{B}(1-\lambda^{T-t}),\qquad\epsilon_{A}=\epsilon_{B}(1-\lambda^{T}).$$ (42)

Thus, with $\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{M}$ formed from $A_{t}^{\mathrm{turn}}$ ,

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}(\pi)\geq{}$ $\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{M}-2A_{\max}T(T+1)\alpha_{M}^{2}$ (43) $\displaystyle-2(T+1)\alpha_{M}\epsilon_{B}(1-\lambda^{T}).$

Use the exact-critic residual $\delta_{s}^{\pi}$ defined in the proof of Lemma 2 . First fix $(h_{t},a_{t})=(h,a)$ and continue with $\pi$ . Equation ( 28 ) gives $\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\delta_{t}^{\pi}\mid h_{t}=h,a_{t}=a]=A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a)$ . For every $s>t$ , the full history $h_{s}$ contains $(h_{t},a_{t})$ , and

[Tableau] $$\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\delta_{s}^{\pi}\mid h_{s}]=\sum_{a_{s}}\pi(a_{s}\mid h_{s})A_{s}^{\pi}(h_{s},a_{s})=0.$$

The tower property consequently gives $\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\delta_{s}^{\pi}\mid h_{t}=h,a_{t}=a]=0$ for $s>t$ . It follows that the entire exact-critic trace has conditional mean

[Tableau] $$\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\!\left[\sum_{\ell=0}^{T-t}\lambda^{\ell}\delta_{t+\ell}^{\pi}\,\middle|\,h_{t}=h,a_{t}=a\right]=A_{t}^{\pi}(h,a).$$

Replacing exact boundary values by critic predictions changes the residual at turn $s$ by $e_{s+1}(h_{s+1})-e_{s}(h_{s})$ . Suppress only the history arguments in the following pathwise sum:

[Tableau] $\displaystyle A_{t}^{\mathrm{turn}}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{T-t}\lambda^{\ell}\delta_{t+\ell}^{\pi}$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{T-t}\lambda^{\ell}(e_{t+\ell+1}-e_{t+\ell})$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{T-t+1}\lambda^{j-1}e_{t+j}-e_{t}-\sum_{j=1}^{T-t}\lambda^{j}e_{t+j}$ $\displaystyle=-e_{t}+(1-\lambda)\sum_{j=1}^{T-t}\lambda^{j-1}e_{t+j}+\lambda^{T-t}e_{T+1}.$

True termination makes the last term zero. Taking the conditional expectation and subtracting the exact advantage therefore gives

[Tableau] $$b_{t}(h,a)=-e_{t}(h)+(1-\lambda)\sum_{j=1}^{T-t}\lambda^{j-1}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[e_{t+j}(h_{t+j})\mid h_{t}=h,a_{t}=a].$$ (44)

Choose the state-only baseline $c_{t}(h)=-e_{t}(h)$ . For $0\leq\lambda<1$ ,

[Tableau] $$|b_{t}(h,a)-c_{t}(h)|\leq(1-\lambda)\sum_{j=1}^{T-t}\lambda^{j-1}\epsilon_{B}=\epsilon_{B}(1-\lambda^{T-t}).$$

For $\lambda=1$ , the finite sum in Eq. ( 44 ) is multiplied by zero, so the same bound holds with value zero. Empty sums vanish and $\lambda^{0}=1$ , including when $\lambda=0$ ; thus the bound also covers $t=T$ and $T=0$ .

Theorem 3 now gives an estimation penalty $2\alpha_{M}\epsilon_{B}\sum_{t=0}^{T}(1-\lambda^{T-t})$ . Since $1-\lambda^{T-t}\leq 1-\lambda^{T}$ , this is at most $2(T+1)\alpha_{M}\epsilon_{B}(1-\lambda^{T})$ , proving Eqs. ( 42 ) and ( 43 ). ∎

The cancellation of $e_{T+1}$ uses true termination. At a nonterminal truncation, that term must be retained unless its bootstrap error is separately controlled.


#### A.6 Token GAE and Internal Critic Error

For comparison, standard token GAE applies trace decay at every generated assistant token:

[Tableau] $$A_{t,i}^{\mathrm{GAE}}(\lambda)=\delta_{t,i}+\lambda A_{t,i+1}^{\mathrm{GAE}}(\lambda).$$ (45)

The trace continues across turns using $A_{t,L_{t}+1}^{\mathrm{GAE}}(\lambda):=A_{t+1,1}^{\mathrm{GAE}}(\lambda)$ , with $A_{T+1,1}^{\mathrm{GAE}}(\lambda):=0$ at termination. Ordering assistant actions by $k(t,i)=\sum_{u<t}L_{u}+i$ gives $N=\sum_{t=0}^{T}L_{t}$ action positions. In this flattened notation,

[Tableau] $$A_{k}^{\mathrm{GAE}}(\lambda)=\sum_{j=k}^{N}\lambda^{j-k}\delta_{j}.$$ (46)

Environment tokens introduce neither TD residuals nor decay steps, but credit across a turn still decays at each assistant token. At $\lambda=1$ , the value differences telescope to

[Tableau] $$A_{k}^{\mathrm{GAE}}(1)=R(\tau)-V_{k}.$$ (47)

To compare trace lengths, specialize to exactly $T+1$ turns of $L$ tokens each, with $N=(T+1)L$ . At flattened position $j=k(t,i)$ , let $e_{j}^{T}(h)$ be the critic prediction minus the exact old-policy token value at history $h$ , and set $e_{N+1}^{T}=0$ . All critic predictions are frozen during the policy update. Inside expectations, errors are evaluated at the corresponding random histories.

Under the assumptions of Theorem 2 , suppose rollouts reach true termination, $q\in[0,1]$ , and $|e_{j}^{T}(h)|\leq\epsilon_{T}$ . The conditional bias of token GAE relative to the exact token advantage is

[Tableau] $$b_{j}^{T}(h,a)=-e_{j}^{T}(h)+(1-q)\sum_{m=1}^{N-j}q^{m-1}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[e_{j+m}^{T}\mid h_{t,i}=h,a_{t,i}=a].$$ (48)

With $\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{T}=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\rho_{j}-1)A_{j}^{\mathrm{GAE}}(q)$ and finite displayed expectations,

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}(\pi)\geq{}$ $\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{T}-2A_{\max}T(T+1)\alpha_{M}^{2}$ (49) $\displaystyle-2(T+1)A_{\max}^{\mathrm{tok}}L(L-1)\alpha_{T}^{2}-2N\alpha_{T}\epsilon_{T}(1-q^{N-1}).$

Write $\delta_{j}^{\pi}$ for the token TD residual formed with exact values. The token Bellman equation gives $\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\delta_{j}^{\pi}\mid h_{t,i}=h,a_{t,i}=a]=A_{t,i}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}(h,a)$ . At each later position $\ell>j$ , the token action is sampled under $\pi$ , so its exact advantage has conditional mean zero given its prefix. That prefix contains the fixed earlier history and action. The tower property therefore gives $\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\delta_{\ell}^{\pi}\mid h_{t,i}=h,a_{t,i}=a]=0$ . Consequently the conditional mean of $\sum_{m=0}^{N-j}q^{m}\delta_{j+m}^{\pi}$ is the exact advantage at position $j$ .

The difference between the estimated and exact residuals is $e_{j+1}^{T}-e_{j}^{T}$ . Collecting its coefficients in the finite trace gives

[Tableau] $\displaystyle A_{j}^{\mathrm{GAE}}(q)-\sum_{m=0}^{N-j}q^{m}\delta_{j+m}^{\pi}$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{N-j}q^{m}(e_{j+m+1}^{T}-e_{j+m}^{T})$ $\displaystyle=-e_{j}^{T}+\sum_{m=1}^{N-j}(q^{m-1}-q^{m})e_{j+m}^{T}+q^{N-j}e_{N+1}^{T}$ $\displaystyle=-e_{j}^{T}+(1-q)\sum_{m=1}^{N-j}q^{m-1}e_{j+m}^{T}.$

Taking conditional expectations proves Eq. ( 48 ). The geometric sum bounds the remaining action-dependent part by

[Tableau] $$|b_{j}^{T}(h,a)+e_{j}^{T}(h)|\leq\epsilon_{T}(1-q^{N-j}).$$

For $q=1$ this follows directly from the zero factor $1-q$ ; for $j=N$ the sum is empty. We use $q^{0}=1$ also at $q=0$ .

Condition on each old-policy token history in the difference of surrogates. The importance ratio changes only the next-token distribution. Moreover, $\sum_{a}[\pi^{\prime}(a\mid h)-\pi(a\mid h)]e_{j}^{T}(h)=0$ . Thus

[Tableau] $\displaystyle|\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{T}-\mathcal{S}_{T}|$ $\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}_{h\sim d_{j}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}}\sum_{a}[\pi^{\prime}(a\mid h)-\pi(a\mid h)][b_{j}^{T}(h,a)+e_{j}^{T}(h)]\right|$ $\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha_{T}\epsilon_{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(1-q^{N-j})\leq 2N\alpha_{T}\epsilon_{T}(1-q^{N-1}),$

where $d_{j}^{\pi,\mathrm{tok}}$ denotes the old-policy history law at flattened position $j$ . Substituting the resulting lower bound on $\mathcal{S}_{T}$ into Eq. ( 12 ) proves the claim. ∎

The conditioning in Eq. ( 48 ) fixes one token, not a complete turn. This bias therefore cannot be used as the scalar macro bias in Theorem 3 . The bound above includes both the within-turn distribution shift and errors at internal prefixes.


#### A.7 Matched-Budget Comparison and an Internal-Error Example

Assume the hypotheses of Corollary 1 and Proposition 1 . As in Appendix A.6 , consider $(T+1)$ turns of $L$ tokens each, with $N=(T+1)L$ . For $R\in[0,1]$ , a uniform token KL cap $\delta$ gives the bounds $\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}(\pi)\geq\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{M}-P_{M}$ and $\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}(\pi)\geq\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{T}-P_{T}$ , with the penalty envelopes

[Tableau] $\displaystyle P_{M}={}$ $\displaystyle T(T+1)L\delta$ $\displaystyle+2(T+1)\sqrt{L\delta/2}\,\epsilon_{B}(1-\lambda^{T}),$ (50) $\displaystyle P_{T}={}$ $\displaystyle T(T+1)L\delta+(T+1)L(L-1)\delta$ $\displaystyle+2(T+1)L\sqrt{\delta/2}\,\epsilon_{T}(1-q^{(T+1)L-1}).$ (51)

If a common critic-error cap $\epsilon_{B}=\epsilon_{T}=\epsilon>0$ is available, $T>0$ , $\delta>0$ , and $q=\lambda<1$ , the token-to-macro ratio of the estimation terms is

[Tableau] $$\sqrt{L}\,\frac{1-\lambda^{(T+1)L-1}}{1-\lambda^{T}}\geq\sqrt{L}.$$ (52)

This also exceeds $\sqrt{L}$ for $L>1$ when matching boundary decay $q^{L}=\lambda\in(0,1)$ , since $q^{(T+1)L-1}=\lambda^{T+1-1/L}$ . At $q=\lambda=1$ both estimation terms vanish; their ratio is undefined.

Because $R\in[0,1]$ , exact values and action values are in $[0,1]$ , so both macro and token advantages have magnitude at most one. Pinsker’s inequality and Eq. ( 13 ) give $\alpha_{T}^{2}\leq\delta/2$ and $\alpha_{M}^{2}\leq L\delta/2$ . All penalty coefficients in the two improvement bounds are nonnegative. Replacing the advantage bounds by one, $\alpha_{T}$ by $\sqrt{\delta/2}$ , and $\alpha_{M}$ by $\sqrt{L\delta/2}$ therefore gives valid upper bounds on those penalties. In particular the two distribution-shift terms become

[Tableau] $$2T(T+1)\frac{L\delta}{2}=T(T+1)L\delta,\qquad 2(T+1)L(L-1)\frac{\delta}{2}=(T+1)L(L-1)\delta.$$

The estimation terms undergo the same substitution, yielding Eqs. ( 50 )–( 51 ).

Under the common positive error cap, $T>0$ , $\delta>0$ , and $q=\lambda<1$ , the macro estimation term is positive. Dividing gives

[Tableau] $$\frac{2(T+1)L\sqrt{\delta/2}\,\epsilon(1-\lambda^{(T+1)L-1})}{2(T+1)\sqrt{L\delta/2}\,\epsilon(1-\lambda^{T})}=\sqrt{L}\frac{1-\lambda^{(T+1)L-1}}{1-\lambda^{T}}.$$

Since $(T+1)L-1\geq T$ and $0\leq\lambda<1$ , the numerator in the final fraction is at least its denominator. This proves Eq. ( 52 ). If instead $q^{L}=\lambda\in(0,1)$ , the exponent becomes $T+1-1/L$ , strictly greater than $T$ for $L>1$ . Thus $1-q^{(T+1)L-1}>1-\lambda^{T}$ , proving the strict comparison. For $q=\lambda=1$ , each estimation term is zero directly from its formula. ∎

The common error cap is an explicit comparison assumption; it does not follow merely from using one critic. These compare certified worst-case penalties, not actual biases or achieved improvements. In particular, the two surrogates on the right-hand sides need not be equal.

Consider one turn of $L>1$ tokens. Its first token chooses a fair sign $Y\in\{-1,1\}$ under the old policy; the remaining tokens are deterministic. The terminal reward is $R=1/2+cY$ , with $0<c<1/2$ . Use exact boundary values $V_{0,1}=1/2$ , $V_{1,1}=0$ , but internal predictions $V_{0,j}=1/2+(c-\varepsilon)Y$ for $2\leq j\leq L$ . Each internal value error has magnitude $\varepsilon$ . The two estimates relevant to the only nonzero policy score are

[Tableau] $$A_{0}^{\mathrm{turn}}=cY,\qquad A_{1}^{\mathrm{GAE}}(q)=[c-\varepsilon(1-q^{L-1})]Y.$$ (53)

When $0<c<\varepsilon(1-q^{L-1})$ , increasing the token surrogate favors the lower-reward action, whereas the macro surrogate favors the higher-reward action. This holds for arbitrarily small policy updates and with both critics’ predictions in $[0,1]$ , for example $c=0.05$ , $\varepsilon=0.2$ , $q=0.8$ , $L=4$ . The objective and boundary predictions are identical; the difference comes from bootstrapping at internal prefixes.

Only the first and last TD residuals are nonzero: $\delta_{1}=(c-\varepsilon)Y$ and $\delta_{L}=\varepsilon Y$ . Their GAE sum is $(c-\varepsilon+q^{L-1}\varepsilon)Y$ . The macro residual is exactly $cY$ . If the new probability of $Y=1$ is $1/2+u$ , then

[Tableau] $$\mathcal{J}(\pi^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}(\pi)=2cu,\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{T}=2[c-\varepsilon(1-q^{L-1})]u.$$

Choosing $u<0$ makes the second quantity positive and the first negative under the stated condition. For the numerical values in the text and $u=-0.1$ , the surrogate increases by $0.00952$ and the return decreases by $0.01$ . The nonbaseline bias radius at the first token is $\varepsilon(1-q^{L-1})$ , and the other tokens have no policy change. Its exact surrogate-error bound is $2|u|\varepsilon(1-q^{L-1})=0.01952$ , which accounts for this reversal. Letting $|u|$ approach zero also satisfies arbitrarily small positive KL budgets: the only changing conditional action law has $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi\|\pi^{\prime})=-\tfrac{1}{2}\log(1-4u^{2})\to 0$ as $u\to 0$ . For the specified example the internal predictions are $0.35$ and $0.65$ , and $c-\varepsilon(1-q^{L-1})=-0.0476<0$ , verifying the reward-range and sign conditions explicitly. ∎


#### A.8 Macro and Token Ratio Objectives

Broadcasting a fixed scalar turn estimate $\widehat{A}_{t}$ gives the token objective

[Tableau] $$\mathcal{T}(\widehat{A})=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\sum_{t}\sum_{i}(\rho_{t,i}-1)\widehat{A}_{t}.$$

In general, $\mathcal{T}(\widehat{A})$ and $\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{M}$ differ at finite updates. Their gradients agree at the old policy when both use the same frozen estimates and summation weights, token probabilities are differentiable with common support, and differentiation may be interchanged with the rollout expectation.

Let $\theta_{0}$ parameterize the old policy, and let $\rho_{t,i}(\theta)$ be the token ratios on a fixed old-policy rollout. The macro ratio is $\rho_{t}(\theta)=\prod_{i}\rho_{t,i}(\theta)$ , and every token ratio is one at $\theta_{0}$ . The finite-product rule gives

[Tableau] $$\left.\nabla_{\theta}(\rho_{t}(\theta)-1)\right|_{\theta_{0}}=\left.\sum_{i}\left(\prod_{j\neq i}\rho_{t,j}(\theta)\right)\nabla_{\theta}\rho_{t,i}(\theta)\right|_{\theta_{0}}=\left.\nabla_{\theta}\sum_{i}(\rho_{t,i}(\theta)-1)\right|_{\theta_{0}}.$$

Multiplying by the frozen $\widehat{A}_{t}$ , summing over turns, and taking the fixed old-policy expectation proves gradient equality under the stated interchange assumption (for example, integrably dominated gradients suffice).

For finite updates the omitted product terms need not cancel even in expectation. Consider one turn with two independent fair signs $X,Y$ under $\pi$ , a frozen estimate $\widehat{A}=XY$ , and new probabilities $\pi^{\prime}(X=1)=1/2+u$ , $\pi^{\prime}(Y=1)=1/2+v$ , where $|u|,|v|<1/2$ . Then $\rho_{1}=1+2uX$ and $\rho_{2}=1+2vY$ . Independence and zero means give

[Tableau] $$\mathcal{T}=\mathbb{E}[(2uX+2vY)XY]=0,\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{M}=\mathbb{E}[(2uX+2vY+4uvXY)XY]=4uv.$$

These differ whenever $uv\neq 0$ , as claimed. ∎

PPO clipping further changes the objective and does not impose a hard macro trust region [ 29 ] . The proposition concerns the unclipped objectives and does not assert finite-update equivalence.
