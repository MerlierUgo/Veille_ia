## QuantStream3D: Geometry-Aligned Low-Bit KV Cache Quantization for Streaming Visual Geometry Transformers

Streaming visual geometry transformers retain historical key–value (KV) states as cross-view geometric memory, but the growing cache increasingly limits long-sequence inference. Low-bit quantization reduces its storage cost while altering both the historical evidence and the geometric computation that consumes it. We systematically characterize KV quantization in streaming geometry and uncover two linked challenges: Nonuniform Geometric Sensitivity and Cache–Geometry Mismatch. Geometric sensitivity to KV quantization varies across precision choices and cache locations, while cross-view retrieval perturbations alter geometry-head inputs and propagate through subsequent predictions and KV writes. Guided by these findings, we propose QuantStream3D, to our knowledge the first dedicated KV quantization framework for streaming visual geometry transformers. Geometry-Guided Mixed-Precision KV Quantization selectively protects sensitive cross-view evidence, while Geometry-Aligned Quantization-Aware Distillation jointly adapts transformer blocks and geometry heads on student-generated quantized history. Extensive evaluations across five benchmarks and two backbones demonstrate near-lossless geometric accuracy in most settings under predominantly 2-bit KV quantization, with many configurations surpassing their unquantized counterparts. Compared with LLM-oriented KV quantizers, QuantStream3D reduces TUM absolute trajectory error and NeuralRGBD completeness error by up to $57.4\%$ and $69.7\%$ , respectively. We further integrate QuantStream3D with existing native cache-pruning methods, pushing the limits of compression while improving reconstruction quality on NeuralRGBD. Our efficiency analysis shows that our optimized StreamVGGT backend achieves $5.05\times$ physical KV-cache compression and $23.1\%$ lower inference latency at batch size one. Under a fixed memory budget on the same GPU, it supports $5.4\times$ the maximum batch size and delivers up to $1.67\times$ the peak throughput of unquantized inference. Code and pretrained checkpoints will be made publicly available.

All first-frame writes and all writes at global-attention layer 14 use 4 bits; remaining history uses 2 bits. The policy applies to full history or native-pruning survivors. (2) Geometry-Aligned Quantization-Aware Distillation jointly adapts transformer blocks and geometry heads on student-generated quantized history, supervised by a frozen native-precision teacher.


### 1 Introduction

Feed-forward models now jointly recover camera parameters, depth, and scene structure from images, as exemplified by the Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) [ 24 ] . Streaming models such as StreamVGGT and STream3R extend this capability to online inference by reusing historical key–value (KV) states as new views arrive [ 36 , 9 ] . This geometric memory carries cross-view evidence into later predictions, while its growing storage and retrieval costs constrain long-sequence inference and concurrency. Recent efforts address this bottleneck through cache pruning, with InfiniteVGGT and FrameVGGT retaining a compact subset of historical states [ 32 , 30 ] . Quantization offers a complementary route by reducing the bit width of retained KV states, an approach widely explored in large language models (LLMs) [ 12 , 7 , 19 ] . Although XStreamVGGT explores combining pruning with quantization, it focuses primarily on 4-bit KV quantization using LLM-oriented methods [ 20 ] . How lower-bit history affects cross-view geometric computation remains insufficiently understood, hindering accurate and efficient KV quantization for streaming geometry.

In this work, we systematically characterize KV quantization across two streaming geometry backbones. Our study spans quantization granularity and bit width, sensitivity across layers, frames, and special tokens, and representation transforms, revealing two linked challenges. Nonuniform Geometric Sensitivity spans representation choices and cache locations: K is not uniformly more sensitive than V, challenging LLM-derived key-priority assumptions [ 19 , 6 ] . Global attention in layer 14 also forms a shared sensitivity peak across both backbones. Cache–Geometry Mismatch arises when quantized cross-view retrieval alters the features supplied to geometry heads trained with native-precision history. Because streaming inference repeatedly consumes and updates this memory, these perturbations can propagate through later queries, KV writes, camera states, and geometric predictions. Together, these findings motivate selective protection of geometric evidence and joint adaptation of history generation, retrieval, and geometric prediction.

We propose QuantStream3D ( Figure 1 ) to preserve sensitive geometric memory and align the computation that consumes it. Guided by this analysis, we develop Geometry-Guided Mixed-Precision KV Quantization , combining per-channel K/V quantization, selective 2/4-bit precision allocation, and paired Q/K rotation to protect sensitive cross-view evidence. To align geometric computation with this quantized history, we introduce Geometry-Aligned Quantization-Aware Distillation (QAD), jointly adapting transformer blocks and geometry heads under a frozen native-precision teacher. The student generates and consumes its own quantized history, allowing later-view geometric supervision to shape earlier KV writes within each clip.

Across five benchmarks and two backbones, many QuantStream3D configurations surpass their unquantized counterparts. Relative to LLM-oriented KV quantizers, we reduce absolute trajectory error and reconstruction completeness error by up to $57.4\%$ and $69.7\%$ , respectively. We further integrate QuantStream3D with existing native cache-pruning methods using the same quantization policy and adapted weights, pushing the limits of compression. To translate low-bit storage into practical inference gains, we implement fused packed-cache kernels for StreamVGGT that reduce intermediate memory traffic and launch overhead.

Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

- • Revealing the geometric impact of KV quantization. We systematically characterize KV quantization in streaming visual geometry transformers, uncovering Nonuniform Geometric Sensitivity and Cache–Geometry Mismatch. We expose the limits of LLM-derived KV quantization assumptions and establish an empirical and analytical basis for geometry-guided quantization and joint adaptation.

- • Unifying geometry-guided quantization and geometry-aligned adaptation. We introduce QuantStream3D, to our knowledge the first dedicated KV quantization framework for streaming visual geometry transformers. It couples Geometry-Guided Mixed-Precision KV Quantization with Geometry-Aligned QAD, preserving sensitive cross-view evidence while jointly adapting transformer blocks and geometry heads to student-generated quantized history.

- • Pushing the limits of compression beyond pruning. We integrate QuantStream3D with three existing native cache-pruning methods, reusing the same quantization policy and adapted weights. This composition achieves up to $6.27\times$ additional KV compression over pruning alone with improved reconstruction quality on NeuralRGBD.

- • Delivering geometric accuracy and practical inference efficiency. We achieve near-lossless or improved geometric accuracy in most settings under predominantly 2-bit KV quantization. At fixed pretrained weights, our fused StreamVGGT backend delivers $5.05\times$ physical KV compression and $23.1\%$ lower inference latency at batch size one. With the same GPU and memory budget, it supports $5.4\times$ the maximum batch size and delivers up to $1.67\times$ the peak throughput of unquantized inference.


### 2 Related Work


#### 2.1 Streaming 3D Geometry

Feed-forward models infer geometry directly from images: DUSt3R predicts point maps [ 26 ] , while VGGT jointly estimates cameras, depth, and scene structure [ 24 ] . StreamVGGT and STream3R extend feed-forward geometry to causal inference, retaining historical KV states for cross-view reasoning [ 36 , 9 ] . Recent approaches optimize history through diversity-aware retention (InfiniteVGGT) [ 32 ] , coherent memory organization (FrameVGGT) [ 30 ] , spatiotemporal caching (STAC) [ 25 ] , and pose-guided writes (SceneBound) [ 27 ] . QVGGT quantizes weights [ 15 ] . Most closely related, XStreamVGGT combines cache pruning with LLM-oriented KIVI quantization, evaluating geometric tasks primarily at 4-bit precision [ 20 ] . Its released quantization path uses simulated quantization and retains dequantized floating-point KV rather than packed low-bit codes. Ultra-low-bit geometric sensitivity and adaptation to quantized history remain insufficiently addressed. QuantStream3D combines systematic geometric characterization with packed low-bit KV storage and optimized inference kernels.


#### 2.2 KV Quantization and Adaptation

KV cache quantization reduces long-context LLM memory costs by storing lower-precision history. KIVI uses per-channel key and per-token value quantization [ 12 ] ; KVQuant combines per-channel keys, pre-RoPE quantization, and nonuniform formats [ 7 ] . Beyond granularity, per-token methods mitigate outliers through Hadamard rotations (QuaRot) [ 1 ] or channel reordering and clipping (SKVQ) [ 4 ] . OScaR addresses token-norm imbalance in per-channel quantization using canalized rotation and token scaling [ 19 ] , whereas OSCAR derives attention-aware rotations from covariance statistics [ 35 ] . TurboQuant limits reconstruction and inner-product errors through rotated scalar quantization and residual correction [ 33 ] . Complementary attention-aware strategies protect salient tokens [ 21 , 17 ] . For model adaptation, LLM-QAT uses teacher-generated training data [ 11 ] , while NVFP4 QAD distills a higher-precision teacher [ 29 ] . Streaming geometry requires preserving cross-view evidence and adapting geometric computation to quantized history. QuantStream3D couples geometry-guided KV quantization with Geometry-Aligned QAD, jointly adapting transformer blocks and geometry heads using quantized history generated by the student.

### 3 Characterizing KV Quantization in Streaming 3D Transformers

We first introduce streaming inference and KV quantization notation ( Sec. 3.1 ). We then examine granularity and K/V precision ( Sec. 3.2 ), layer, frame, and token sensitivity ( Sec. 3.3 ), and quantization transforms ( Sec. 3.4 ).


#### 3.1 Preliminaries

We use StreamVGGT to illustrate causal geometry inference [ 36 , 20 , 32 ] . The image encoder produces patch tokens that, together with camera and register tokens, pass through alternating frame and global attention (GA). Only GA maintains a cross-frame KV cache; frame attention is confined to the current view. For one head at GA layer $\ell$ and frame $t$ , let $Q_{\ell,t},K_{\ell,t},V_{\ell,t}$ denote the current-frame tensors, with Q and K defined after normalization and positional encoding. Given head dimension $d$ and historical cache $H_{\ell,t-1}=(K_{\ell,<t},V_{\ell,<t})$ , the native-precision GA read is

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\bar{K}_{\ell,t}$ $\displaystyle=[K_{\ell,<t};K_{\ell,t}],\quad\bar{V}_{\ell,t}=[V_{\ell,<t};V_{\ell,t}],$ (1) $\displaystyle A_{\ell,t}$ $\displaystyle=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(Q_{\ell,t}\bar{K}_{\ell,t}^{\top}/\sqrt{d}\right)\bar{V}_{\ell,t}.$

Here $[\,;\,]$ concatenates tokens. Appending current K/V after each read gives $H_{\ell,t}=(\bar{K}_{\ell,t},\bar{V}_{\ell,t})$ , so the cache grows with the stream. Geometry heads predict cameras, depth, and point maps; the camera head also maintains recurrent state.


##### KV quantization and notation.

We therefore quantize only the historical GA cache $H_{\ell,t-1}$ in Equation 1 , decoding it for retrieval while retaining current-frame K/V and camera-head state at native precision. K $a$ V $b$ denotes $a$ -bit keys and $b$ -bit values. Quantization granularity is specified independently of bit width: C and T denote per-channel and per-token quantization. KCVC, KCVT, KTVC, and KTVT denote the channel/channel, channel/token, token/channel, and token/token K/V combinations, respectively. In full-history inference, per-channel groups span tokens at a fixed channel within one frame and head; per-token groups span channels within each token .


#### 3.2 Quantization Granularity and K/V Precision

We compare four K/V granularities in both backbones using affine round-to-nearest quantization with groups of 64, without protection or transforms. Both K and V use 2 bits, isolating granularity in this challenging low-bit regime. Per-channel K/V improves trajectory estimation over per-token K/V in both backbones ( Tab. 1 ). On StreamVGGT, KCVC also yields lower trajectory error than the LLM-oriented asymmetric KCVT layout adopted by KIVI [ 12 ] . Given its strong overall performance, we use KCVC for subsequent analyses unless noted otherwise.

Fixing KCVC, we compare K4V4, K4V2, K2V4, and K2V2 to isolate bit-width and K/V sensitivity. Without protection or transforms, 4-bit caching is comparatively robust, whereas 2-bit caching can substantially increase geometric error ( Tab. 1 ). The relative ranking of K4V2 and K2V4 reverses across geometric outputs and backbones. The key-priority tendency reported in LLM studies [ 6 ] does not consistently transfer: K is not uniformly more precision-sensitive than V in streaming geometry.

Takeaways. • Quantization granularity. Per-channel K/V is a strong overall choice across the evaluated tasks and backbones. • Bit-width sensitivity. Unprotected 4-bit caching is comparatively robust; 2-bit caching can incur large geometric errors. • K/V sensitivity. K is not consistently more sensitive than V.

We next examine how quantization sensitivity varies across layers, frames, and token types. We scan all 24 global-attention layers in both backbones, reducing K, V, or both from 4 to 2 bits at one layer while keeping all others at 4 bits. Global attention in layer 14 yields the largest error increase under all three interventions and across all three TUM trajectory metrics in both backbones. To examine historical roles and special-token sensitivity [ 22 ] , we separately restore all tokens in the first frame, camera tokens across history, or register tokens across history to native precision under otherwise 2-bit caching. First-frame protection lowers translational and rotational relative pose errors in both backbones. Camera- and register-token protection can also improve geometric outputs, but their benefits vary across backbones and metrics ( Sec. A.1 ).

Takeaways. • Layer sensitivity. Global attention in layer 14 is a shared sensitivity peak across both backbones. • Frame sensitivity. First-frame protection yields repeatable gains in both backbones. • Token sensitivity. Camera and register tokens can benefit from protection, with gains that vary across geometric outputs.


#### 3.4 Quantization Transforms

Transformation-based methods reshape activation distributions to ease low-bit quantization. QuaRot and RotateKV redistribute channel outliers through orthogonal mixing [ 1 , 18 ] . SmoothQuant rescales activations with compensating weight transforms, while QServe applies reciprocal Q/K scaling through SmoothAttention [ 28 , 10 ] . We examine whether these benefits transfer to streaming geometry.

Under unprotected 2-bit caching, we compare paired Q/K Hadamard rotation, V-only rotation, their combination, and fixed SmoothQuant-style channel scaling. Paired Q/K rotation reduces all three TUM trajectory errors in both backbones. V rotation can improve individual outputs, but adding it to Q/K rotation increases all three STream3R trajectory errors. Fixed channel scaling likewise offers no consistent benefit across tasks and backbones ( Sec. A.2 ).

Takeaways. • Q/K rotation. Paired Hadamard rotation yields repeatable gains under 2-bit quantization. • Value rotation. V rotation can help, with gains that depend on the backbone and accompanying transforms. • Channel scaling. Fixed channel scaling does not yield consistent gains across the tested settings.


### 4 QuantStream3D

QuantStream3D preserves sensitive cross-view evidence and aligns geometric computation with quantized history. We describe geometry-guided quantization ( Sec. 4.1 ), Geometry-Aligned QAD ( Sec. 4.2 ), fused execution ( Sec. 4.3 ), and composition with native pruning ( Sec. 4.4 ).


#### 4.1 Geometry-Guided Mixed-Precision KV Quantization


##### Per-channel K/V quantization.

Following Sec. 3.2 , we partition K and V independently into contiguous blocks of $G=64$ tokens within each frame and global-attention head. For a block $X\in\mathbb{R}^{G\times d}$ , each column $\mathbf{x}_{c}=X_{:,c}\in\mathbb{R}^{G}$ forms one quantization group with its own scale $s_{c}$ and zero point $z_{c}$ . Nonconstant groups use $b$ -bit affine quantization:

[Tableau] $\displaystyle s_{c}$ $\displaystyle=(M_{c}-m_{c})/(2^{b}-1),\qquad z_{c}=\operatorname{round}(-m_{c}/s_{c}),$ (2) $\displaystyle\mathbf{q}_{c}$ $\displaystyle=\operatorname{clip}\bigl(\operatorname{round}(\mathbf{x}_{c}/s_{c})+z_{c},0,2^{b}-1\bigr),$ $\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{c}$ $\displaystyle=s_{c}(\mathbf{q}_{c}-z_{c}),$

where $m_{c}=\min\mathbf{x}_{c}$ and $M_{c}=\max\mathbf{x}_{c}$ . We bit-pack the integer codes and store scales and zero points in FP32. Current-frame K/V remain at native precision during attention and are quantized only when appended to history.


##### Geometry-guided precision allocation.

For a write at layer $\ell$ and frame $f$ , our allocation protects the first frame or global attention in layer 14:

[Tableau] $$b_{\ell,f}=\begin{cases}4,&f=0\ \lor\ \ell=14,\\ 2,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}$$ (3)

The allocation is shared across backbones and datasets, with no task-specific thresholds or online importance estimation. Protecting sparse camera or register tokens can improve some outputs ( Sec. 3.3 ), but assigning them separate bit widths would fragment the precision layout within each write. A single bit width per write preserves regular per-channel groups for efficient packing and fused history reads.


##### Paired query/key rotation.

Before quantizing keys, we apply the same Hadamard transform to Q and K after normalization and 2D rotary positional encoding (RoPE), leaving V unrotated. With $R_{H}=H_{64}/8$ and $H_{64}H_{64}^{\top}=64I$ , attention scores are preserved in exact arithmetic:

[Tableau] $$\widetilde{Q}=QR_{H},\quad\widetilde{K}=KR_{H},\quad\widetilde{Q}\widetilde{K}^{\top}=QK^{\top}.$$ (4)

Current and historical keys share the rotated query basis, eliminating inverse key rotation during retrieval.

#### 4.2 Geometry-Aligned Quantization-Aware Distillation


##### Cache–geometry mismatch.

Selective protection preserves sensitive cross-view evidence, but quantized history can still perturb attention retrieval and the features supplied to pretrained geometry heads. At a fixed read $A=PV$ , let $P,V$ denote native attention weights and visible values, with current-frame Q/K/V held fixed. Historical-key quantization changes $P$ to $\widehat{P}$ , while historical-value errors $\Delta V$ vanish on current tokens; writing $\widehat{A}=\widehat{P}(V+\Delta V)$ and $\Delta P=\widehat{P}-P$ gives the exact decomposition

[Tableau] $$\widehat{A}-A=\Delta P\,V+P\,\Delta V+\Delta P\,\Delta V.$$ (5)

The terms separate attention redistribution, value distortion, and their interaction, linking readout sensitivity to both KV errors and attention context ( Sec. B.1 ). History reuse can carry these perturbations into later queries, KV writes, and geometry-head inputs. With pretrained parameters $(\theta_{0},\phi_{0})$ fixed, geometry heads predict $y_{t}=g_{\phi_{0}}(z_{t},C_{t-1})$ from backbone features $z_{t}$ and unquantized camera state $C_{t-1}$ . For native ( $0$ ) and quantized ( $\pi$ ) histories, the geometric output change is approximated to first order by

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\Delta y_{t}$ $\displaystyle=g_{\phi_{0}}(z_{t}^{\pi},C_{t-1}^{\pi})-g_{\phi_{0}}(z_{t}^{0},C_{t-1}^{0})$ (6) $\displaystyle\simeq J_{z}\,\Delta z_{t}+J_{C}\,\Delta C_{t-1}.$

Here $\Delta z_{t}=z_{t}^{\pi}-z_{t}^{0}$ and $\Delta C_{t-1}=C_{t-1}^{\pi}-C_{t-1}^{0}$ , with Jacobians $J_{z},J_{C}$ evaluated at the native trajectory. Although $C$ remains unquantized, its values can change through earlier feature perturbations. Paired rollouts on two TUM prefixes show that quantized history changes head inputs, depth, and point predictions at the same view, with pretrained weights and head precision fixed ( Tab. 2 ).

##### Joint geometric supervision.

To align geometric prediction with quantized history, we propose Geometry-Aligned Quantization-Aware Distillation, jointly adapting history generation, retrieval, and geometric interpretation. A frozen native-precision teacher supervises a student initialized from the same pretrained weights [ 11 , 29 ] , with independent KV and camera states for each model. The student generates and consumes its own fake-quantized history under the deployment policy. Within-clip straight-through gradients let later-view geometric supervision shape earlier KV writes and subsequent retrieval ( Figure 3 ). We jointly update complete frame- and global-attention blocks $\theta$ , including FFNs and normalization, and camera, depth, and point heads $\phi$ . The image encoder, seed tokens, and bit assignments remain fixed. Let $Y_{\theta,\phi}^{\pi}$ and $Y_{\theta_{0},\phi_{0}}^{0}$ collect student and teacher clip outputs, with stop-gradient $\operatorname{sg}$ on teacher targets; we jointly optimize both parameter groups:

[Tableau] $$\min_{\theta,\phi}\ \mathbb{E}_{I_{0:T-1}}\mathcal{L}_{\rm QAD}\!\left(Y_{\theta,\phi}^{\pi},\operatorname{sg}(Y_{\theta_{0},\phi_{0}}^{0})\right).$$ (7)

Updates to $\theta$ adapt historical writes and retrieval, while updates to $\phi$ adapt geometric interpretation and camera-state recurrence ( Sec. B.3 ). We retain StreamVGGT’s released distillation objective and loss weights [ 36 ] :

[Tableau] $$\mathcal{L}_{\rm QAD}=20\mathcal{L}_{\rm camera}+20\mathcal{L}_{\rm depth}+10\mathcal{L}_{\rm point}.$$ (8)


#### 4.3 Fused Execution with Packed KV

Packed KV reduces persistent storage but introduces decoding and input-assembly overhead. We implement fused Triton [ 23 ] kernels for StreamVGGT to eliminate redundant history materialization and reduce launch overhead [ 13 , 19 ] .

On each read, a single kernel unpacks mixed 2/4-bit history, applies affine reconstruction, and copies current native-precision K/V directly into the final contiguous attention operands. Direct placement eliminates per-frame decoded buffers and a separate concatenation step while retaining the original scaled dot-product attention (SDPA) and its autocast behavior. For a combined decoded historical K/V payload of $D_{F}$ bytes, direct placement avoids $2D_{F}$ logical bytes of intermediate writes and reads.

We fuse the Q/K Hadamard transforms in a shared launch after normalization and RoPE, leaving V unrotated. Current-frame attention uses native-precision K/V, so each layer stages its writes after the FFN and defers packing until the backbone finishes. Before geometry prediction, one launch per layer jointly performs per-channel min–max reduction, affine quantization, and bit packing for current K/V, while earlier history stays packed ( Appendix C ).


#### 4.4 Composition with Native Cache Pruning

Beyond quantizing unpruned KV caches, we integrate QuantStream3D with existing cache-pruning methods to push the limits of KV compression. Pruning reduces the number of retained states, while quantization lowers their bit width. We instantiate this composition with InfiniteVGGT, XStreamVGGT, and FrameVGGT [ 32 , 20 , 30 ] , reusing the same adapted StreamVGGT weights. Each integration preserves the native selection rule, selection schedule, auxiliary state, and feature basis. Given decoded KV candidates $Z_{\tau}$ and selector state $a_{\tau}$ at event $\tau$ , the cache update is

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\tau}$ $\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{\rm base}(Z_{\tau},a_{\tau}),$ (9) $\displaystyle H_{\tau}^{\pi}$ $\displaystyle=\operatorname{Pack}\!\left[\mathcal{Q}_{\pi}\!\left(\mathcal{G}_{\pi}(Z_{\tau}[\mathcal{I}_{\tau}])\right)\right].$

Here $\mathcal{S}_{\rm base}$ is the native selector, $\mathcal{G}_{\pi}$ forms 64-token groups for each layer, head, and bit width, and $\mathcal{Q}_{\pi}$ applies the per-channel quantizer in Sec. 4.1 . We assign precision using each retained token’s original frame and layer identity. Regrouping may combine retained tokens from different frames, so we recompute per-channel min–max statistics for the resulting groups. Quantized history can also change later selection. We therefore evaluate geometric quality and physical KV storage on each realized rollout ( Sec. 5.3 ).

### 6 Conclusion

We systematically characterized low-bit KV quantization in streaming geometry, revealing the need to preserve cross-view evidence and align geometric computation with quantized history. Guided by these findings, QuantStream3D couples geometry-guided mixed precision with Geometry-Aligned QAD. Extensive evaluations demonstrate strong geometric accuracy with predominantly 2-bit history, further compression beyond native pruning, and substantial memory savings and inference gains, providing a practical foundation for efficient streaming 3D perception.

### Appendix B Geometry-Aligned Quantization-Aware Distillation


#### B.1 Attention Readout Analysis

To interpret the readout identity in Equation 5 , fix a query $q$ , the visible tokens, and the attention mask. Let $p=\operatorname{softmax}(Kq/\sqrt{d})$ and $a=\sum_{i}p_{i}v_{i}$ , with historical errors $e_{i}^{K},e_{i}^{V}$ vanishing on current tokens. For $\Delta\ell_{i}=q^{\top}e_{i}^{K}/\sqrt{d}$ , linearizing the readout gives

[Tableau] $$\Delta a\simeq\Delta a_{\rm lin}=\sum_{i}p_{i}\Delta\ell_{i}(v_{i}-a)+\sum_{i}p_{i}e_{i}^{V}.$$ (S1)

Key errors act through query alignment and value contrast $v_{i}-a$ ; value errors are weighted by attention mass $p_{i}$ . Equal tensor-error norms therefore need not produce equal readout changes. With $\Delta p=\widehat{p}-p$ , the exact remainder is

[Tableau] $$R_{a}=\Delta a-\Delta a_{\rm lin}=V^{\top}[\Delta p-J(p)\Delta\ell]+\Delta V^{\top}\Delta p,\qquad J(p)=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^{\top}.$$ (S2)

The remainder combines nonlinear attention changes and K/V interaction. With operands and support fixed, scaling both perturbations by $\lambda$ gives $R_{a}=O(\lambda^{2})$ as $\lambda\to 0$ . This forward expansion does not differentiate through rounding.

We evaluate the approximation under the deployment policy on two eight-view TUM Freiburg3 prefixes, sitting_halfsphere and walking_halfsphere . At views 1, 4, and 7 in global-attention layers 0, 14, and 23, we compare native and quantized historical K/V for camera, register, and middle-patch queries, holding current operands fixed. Across these 18 reads, the remainder is $0.93$ – $12.10\%$ of the exact change aggregated over the three queries, with a per-query maximum of $24.82\%$ . Together, the analysis shows that readout sensitivity depends on both the direction of KV errors and the attention context. We next trace how these readout perturbations propagate into geometric prediction.


#### B.2 Propagation into Geometric Predictions

We compare native- and quantized-history rollouts at fixed parameters, inputs, token identities, and retained observations to trace the propagation in Equation 6 . The states $s_{t}^{0},s_{t}^{\pi}$ include decoded history in the shared Q/K basis and native-precision camera state. The residual $\eta_{t}^{\pi}$ is the quantized update minus the native update $\mathcal{T}_{t}$ , both evaluated at $s_{t-1}^{\pi}$ :

[Tableau] $$s_{t}^{\pi}=\mathcal{T}_{t}(s_{t-1}^{\pi})+\eta_{t}^{\pi},\qquad s_{t}^{0}=\mathcal{T}_{t}(s_{t-1}^{0}).$$ (S3)

Assume the native transition is $L_{t}$ -Lipschitz on a neighborhood containing both incoming states, with $\|\eta_{t}^{\pi}\|\leq\epsilon_{t}$ . Starting from the same initial state, the state discrepancy satisfies

[Tableau] $$\delta_{t}\leq L_{t}\delta_{t-1}+\epsilon_{t}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{t}\epsilon_{i}\prod_{j=i+1}^{t}L_{j},\qquad\delta_{t}=\|s_{t}^{\pi}-s_{t}^{0}\|.$$ (S4)

The bound follows from the triangle inequality and recursive substitution, with empty products equal to one. Each product bounds the influence of an earlier perturbation through subsequent updates, including camera-state recurrence. It does not prescribe a monotonic trend in geometric prediction error.

We measure propagation on the same TUM prefixes using fixed pretrained weights and independent KV and camera states. Relative L2 changes compare features and predictions at matching views, using the native rollout as the reference. The example in Tab. 2 pairs feature index 17 with depth and point outputs from the fifth view. Across sampled views, head-input changes at feature indices 4, 11, 17, and 23 range from $0.16$ – $11.15\%$ , while depth and point outputs change by $1.00$ – $2.41\%$ and $1.55$ – $3.13\%$ , respectively. These paired rollouts confirm that perturbations introduced by quantized history can reach geometric predictions even when geometry heads remain at native precision.


#### B.3 Local Capacity of Joint Adaptation

At fixed parameters, rollout, masks, alignment branch, and confidence weights, we compare the trainable scopes of Equation 7 . Let $\widetilde{r}$ be the weighted student–teacher residual and $A_{\theta},A_{\phi}$ the block and head columns of one weighted straight-through output Jacobian, with graph boundaries held fixed. For parameter increments $u,v$ , the common linear surrogate is $\widetilde{r}+A_{\theta}u+A_{\phi}v$ . Define $A_{J}=[A_{\theta}\ A_{\phi}]$ and $\Pi_{X}=A_{X}A_{X}^{\dagger}$ , where $\dagger$ denotes the pseudoinverse. The unconstrained least-squares optimum is

[Tableau] $$R_{X}=\|(I-\Pi_{X})\widetilde{r}\|_{2}^{2},\quad X\in\{\theta,\phi,J\},\qquad R_{J}\leq\min\{R_{\theta},R_{\phi}\}.$$ (S5)

The joint correction space contains both component spaces. Set $r_{\theta}=(I-\Pi_{\theta})\widetilde{r}$ and $B_{\phi}=(I-\Pi_{\theta})A_{\phi}$ . The columns of $B_{\phi}$ are head directions orthogonal to the block space, giving

[Tableau] $$R_{\theta}-R_{J}=\|\Pi_{B_{\phi}}r_{\theta}\|_{2}^{2},\qquad\Pi_{B_{\phi}}=B_{\phi}B_{\phi}^{\dagger}.$$ (S6)

The reduction is strict when these directions capture a nonzero component of $r_{\theta}$ : head updates can then complement changes to history generation and retrieval. This is a capacity statement for the shared linear surrogate; the realized gain depends on finite-budget optimization with evolving Jacobians and the original nonquadratic loss. We compare trainable scopes under matched adaptation settings in Tab. S13 .

### Appendix C Packed-Cache Execution


#### C.1 Fused Reads and Paired Writes

The packed-cache implementation in Sec. 4.3 constructs attention operands without intermediate history materialization ( Figure S1 ). One read launch reconstructs historical K/V from mixed 2/4-bit codes and FP32 affine parameters, while a separate program branch copies current native-precision K/V. Both branches write directly into the final contiguous attention operands. With $F$ historical frames, this removes $2F$ per-frame decoded arrays and the subsequent concatenation. Original SDPA and its BF16 autocast remain outside the fused kernel.

Q and K share a rotation launch after normalization and 2D RoPE; V remains unrotated. Each layer stages current K/V after its FFN. Once the backbone completes, one paired encoder launch per layer performs per-channel range reduction, affine quantization, and bit packing before geometry prediction. Each program processes K or V independently within the shared launch, while previously packed history is reused.

[Tableau] $$M=(L+T-1)S_{4}+(LT-L-T+1)S_{2}.$$ (S8)
