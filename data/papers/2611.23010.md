A Preprint


### 1 Introduction

Multimodal physics reasoning requires vision-language models (VLMs) to perform multistep problem solving from text, diagrams, and physical conditions. A key challenge is maintaining the applicability of the premises used in reasoning. The same object may be subject to different constraints at different stages, and a relation valid in one state may not apply to a subsequent process. Even when a model correctly recognizes the objects, numerical values, and scene, using a constraint outside the states or conditions in which it holds can propagate errors through subsequent calculations. Agents for physics reasoning must therefore maintain premise scopes throughout problem solving.

> Figure 1: The motivation: correct scene, stale constraint. (a) A physics problem from PhysAlign [ 3 ] . (b) The updated scene depicts $\theta(B)=53^{\circ}$ , but the calculation at $B$ reuses the $30^{\circ}$ angle from state $A$ . (c) This scope mismatch propagates through a correct energy equation to an incorrect final speed. This failure illustrates why state updates must propagate to premise use and dependent calculations.

Figure 1 illustrates how a mismatch in premise applicability can lead to an incorrect solution. The rope segment makes an angle of $30^{\circ}$ in state $A$ and $53^{\circ}$ in state $B$ . Although the model correctly represents the scene at $B$ , it substitutes the $30^{\circ}$ angle from $A$ into the velocity constraint at $B$ , yielding an incorrect speed. We define the scope of a premise by the entities it concerns, the states in which it applies, and the conditions under which it holds. In this example, the angle at $A$ remains valid historical information, but it cannot be used as the current angle at $B$ . The velocity relation must also satisfy the corresponding rope-length constraint. This scope mismatch shows that an accurate visual representation does not automatically ensure correct use of premises: the link between each premise and its applicability conditions must be preserved in the derivation itself.

External representations and visual memory provide a basis for supporting such reasoning. Visual Sketchpad [ 6 ] supports visual reasoning through intermediate sketches, Canvas-CoT [ 7 ] supports state revision through an editable canvas and visual checks, and VISTA [ 5 ] preserves original visual observations for later inspection during reasoning. These capabilities allow models to use external information to assess and revise their understanding of a problem. For physics problems, these adjustments must also be reflected in premise use: whether a relation applies to the current state, and which conclusions remain supported after it is revised, directly affect subsequent calculations. An agent harness for physics reasoning must therefore connect the maintenance of visual information with updates to the premises used for solving.

To this end, we introduce PhysScope , an inference-time visual harness that requires no updates to model weights. PhysScope organizes working memory around physical premises with explicit scopes, generates textual feedback and derived views from the same premises, and supports revisions to reasoning grounded in source evidence. The framework connects visual observations, premise use, and updates to derivations, supporting the model in identifying scope mismatches and revising the relevant derivations during problem solving.

PhysScope’s innovation lies in explicitly managing the applicability of physical premises and the consequences of their revision through the harness. Canvas-CoT [ 7 ] organizes reasoning around editable structured states, and VISTA [ 5 ] preserves original observations for visual reinspection. PhysScope maintains the premises used for solving by tracking their scopes and derivation dependencies. Under this design, whether a premise applies to the current state becomes checkable, and accepted premise corrections invalidate registered derivations that depend on the superseded content. This preserves physical relations that remain valid in their respective states and identifies which conclusions need to be re-established. Updates to visual memory can therefore influence subsequent calculations and provide targeted feedback for model self-correction.

We assess this design through evaluations across models and experiments examining its mechanisms. On PhysAlign [ 3 ] , PhysScope achieves higher solving scores than direct solving across all six evaluated VLMs. With Gemini-3.8-flash [ 2 ] , it exceeds Canvas-CoT [ 7 ] by 9.34 percentage points and improves problem-solving accuracy in external evaluation on PhysUniBench [ 8 ] . Controlled comparisons with shared physical content also show gains in solving scores from adding derived views. Ablation studies show that removing scope management leaves content recognition accuracy unchanged but markedly reduces solving scores, removing dependency maintenance also lowers solving scores. These results support explicitly maintaining premise applicability and the consequences of revision in multimodal physics reasoning.

Our main contributions are as follows:

- • Scope-aware physical working memory. We organize working memory around physical premises associated with entities, states, and applicability conditions. This makes premise applicability an explicit responsibility of the harness and supports checking the scope within which visual information is used in subsequent derivations.

- • Visual feedback consistent with physical premises. We generate textual feedback and derived views from shared premises, preserving applicability conditions and unknowns while presenting differences between states. This connects visual observations to applicable premises and helps the model identify scope mismatches.

- • Controlled, dependency-aware memory revision. We combine source and scope checks and, upon accepting a premise correction, invalidate registered derivations that depend on the previous premise. This identifies conclusions that need to be re-established and allows local corrections to propagate to subsequent problem solving.


### 2 Related Work


##### Multimodal physics reasoning and role grounding.

Multimodal benchmarks evaluate how models combine visual evidence with domain knowledge. MathVista [ 9 ] studies mathematical reasoning in visual contexts. In physics, SeePhys [ 24 ] spans educational levels and diagram types, PhysUniBench [ 8 ] targets undergraduate problems, and PhyX [ 16 ] covers diverse physical domains and reasoning types. PhysReason [ 26 ] evaluates final answers and intermediate steps. Targeted diagnostics separate perception from physical interpretation: PhysAlign [ 3 ] distinguishes content recognition from role grounding via localized probes and controlled variants, while SeePhys Pro [ 25 ] transfers critical information from text to images across semantically equivalent variants. For solving, Zhang et al. [ 27 ] train models to separate physical modeling from mathematical execution via supervised fine-tuning and rubric-based reinforcement learning. Physics Supernova [ 13 ] integrates image analysis and answer review into an agent workflow with access to computational tools. PhysScope focuses on premise use during inference, linking explicit object, state, and condition scopes to dependent calculations and subsequent revisions.

Visual reasoning with external representations. External representations make intermediate reasoning accessible through programs, image regions, and editable diagrams. VISPROG [ 4 ] and ViperGPT [ 19 ] compose visual and language modules into executable programs. Visual CoT [ 15 ] focuses successive reasoning turns on relevant image regions, while Visual Sketchpad [ 6 ] lets models draw and inspect intermediate visual artifacts. Canvas-CoT [ 7 ] maintains a mutable document object model (DOM) and uses a critic to compare its rendered state with the original image. PhysScope organizes intermediate physical content as claims with sources, explicit scopes, and versioned dependencies. Derived views preserve registered conditions and unknowns, and both text and visual feedback come from Scope-Aware Working Memory. This supports comparisons that examine visual organization while matching the physical content available to the solver.

Agent memory and evidence-grounded revision. Agent memory enables revisiting evidence and prior reasoning. VISTA [ 5 ] retains original visual observations for active retrieval and input reorganization. ReMAP [ 7 ] combines global and local latent visual memories with a learned policy governing when and which memory to access. For video plausibility assessment, PhysMRV [ 23 ] retrieves scene descriptions, physical-event graphs, and rule summaries from training videos to support a frozen VLM.

For revision, Self-Refine [ 10 ] uses the same model to critique and revise its outputs, whereas Reflexion [ 17 ] stores reflections on task feedback for subsequent trials. EVPV [ 20 ] matches explicit visual premises against independently extracted constraints to calibrate process rewards and candidate ranking. Classical truth-maintenance systems [ 2 ] record justifications and revise beliefs as their assumptions change. PhysScope maintains Scope-Aware Working Memory within the current problem. Local revisions are checked against recorded sources and scopes, accepted changes invalidate registered dependent facts, equations, and views. Semantic repair uses an updated snapshot when an unresolved issue is answer-relevant, actionable, and within budget, protocol-triggered regeneration is accounted for separately.


### 3 Method

PhysScope maintains physical premises as source-linked records with explicit scopes and versioned dependencies. Its design connects premise applicability, visual feedback, and memory revision: the same registered content supports text and views, while accepted premise replacements invalidate registered dependents. A shared VLM interprets the problem and derives physical content, a deterministic harness checks declared references and controls memory updates. Figure 2 summarizes this interaction.

> Figure 2: Overview of PhysScope. The shared VLM performs Call 1 ( Draft ) and Call 2 ( Solve & Decision-making ). The deterministic harness manages three connected components: (a) Scope-Aware Working Memory associates premises with entities, states, conditions, sources, and versioned dependencies. (b) Grounded Visual Feedback presents the same registered physical content in text and graphics. A Delivered Snapshot records the memory, source evidence, check feedback, and views supplied to a model call. (c) Source-Grounded Memory Revision checks proposed changes, commits accepted replacements, and invalidates registered dependents. Pending or rejected revisions leave active versions unchanged. Dashed arrows mark the optional Repair path (Call 3). Semantic repair and protocol recovery share one additional call.


#### 3.1 Agent–harness inference protocol

Given a statement $Q$ and diagram $I$ , the core protocol runs Call 1 ( Draft ) and Call 2 ( Solve & Decision-making ) on the same VLM $\mathcal{L}$ .

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}$ $\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(Q,I,\mathrm{draft}),$ (1) $\displaystyle(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{2})$ $\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{prepare}}(Q,I,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}),$ $\displaystyle(\hat{y}_{2},\Delta_{2})$ $\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(Q,I,\mathcal{O}_{2},\mathrm{solve}).$

Call 1 drafts $\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}$ by recording entities, source readings, relations, candidate states, and unknowns. The Protocol Compiler assigns identifiers and versions while preserving declared scopes and dependencies. The Consistency Checker evaluates source delivery, version validity, and scope compatibility. The Tool Scheduler selects source inspections and supported views. These components prepare memory $\mathcal{M}$ and the Delivered Snapshot $\mathcal{O}_{2}$ . Given $Q$ , $I$ , and $\mathcal{O}_{2}$ , Call 2 returns a provisional answer $\hat{y}_{2}$ and an incremental submission $\Delta_{2}$ containing new conclusions, revision requests, and unresolved issues. Key equations and answer claims specify applicable states and direct dependencies.

The agent supplies physical content, source locations, and dependencies. The harness executes tools and determines which proposed updates to commit. Original inputs remain available throughout inference. The harness may authorize one additional call (Call 3) for an eligible semantic issue or recoverable protocol failure (Algorithm 1 ). Reference answers, ground-truth entity assignments, and analysis-only labels are excluded from inference and scheduling.


#### 3.2 Scope-Aware Working Memory

A premise may remain valid for an earlier state while being inapplicable to the current derivation. PhysScope therefore records the scope of each premise separately from its content. Memory distinguishes immutable source evidence, model reading claims, working facts, and unresolved issues. Each fact version is represented as

[Tableau] $$f_{i}^{(v)}=\bigl(p_{i},e_{i},s_{i},c_{i},\rho_{i},D_{i},\tau_{i}\bigr),$$ (2)

where $p_{i}$ is the content, $e_{i}$ , $s_{i}$ , and $c_{i}$ specify the associated entities, state scope, and applicability conditions, $\rho_{i}$ records sources, $D_{i}$ lists direct dependencies with exact versions, and $\tau_{i}$ denotes claim status. Scopes distinguish temporal phases, physical regimes, and spatial regions. Claim statuses are observed , derived , hypothesis , and unknown . An observed claim is a model reading of a source, a derived claim must identify its supporting premises.

Figure 2 (a) illustrates these distinctions. The parameters $m$ , $(J,r)$ , and $k$ belong to the block, pulley, and spring, respectively. The readings $x=0$ and $v=0$ apply to release state $S_{0}$ . In descent state $S_{1}$ , $\omega=v/r$ holds under ideal-rope and no-slip conditions, while the turning position $x^{\ast}$ in $S_{2}$ remains unknown. State evolution adds scoped records without overwriting valid historical readings. An omitted scope does not imply global applicability. Appendix A.1 details applicability checks, typed temporal contracts, and the use of historical initial conditions.

The Consistency Checker evaluates declared references, source delivery, exact versions, and scope compatibility. Each check records its target, rule, outcome, and reason, with outcomes pass , fail , unknown , or unsupported . Optional physical checks cover predefined relations with explicit premises. A passing result establishes consistency with the executed rules. This representation makes declared premise applicability available to both programmatic checks and subsequent visual feedback.


#### 3.3 Grounded Visual Feedback

To expose scope distinctions during reasoning, PhysScope generates textual feedback and derived views from the same registered physical content. The operation inspect_source retrieves a specified text span or original image region. The operation render_view constructs a view from selected facts and states:

[Tableau] $$\mathcal{V}=R\bigl(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{sel}},\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sel}},\sigma\bigr),$$ (3)

where $\sigma$ specifies the view type and layout. Every displayed physical relation, condition, and check result has a corresponding textual record. Rendering introduces no additional physical inference: rule-derived content must first be registered with its supporting premises and rule.

> Figure 3: Scope distinctions in physical reasoning. Examples from PhysAlign [ 3 ] (left) and PhysUniBench [ 8 ] (right). Each example compares recorded PhysScope and Canvas-CoT [ 7 ] trajectories, intermediate diagrams are explanatory redraws. In the impact example, the Canvas-CoT trajectory carries the pre-impact rolling relation across the collision, while the PhysScope trajectory separates impact dynamics from climbing energy. In the gas example, the Canvas-CoT [ 7 ] trajectory treats the adiabatic leg as isothermal and reuses $\Delta U_{AB}$ for the full process, the PhysScope trajectory uses $T_{C}=T_{A}$ to obtain $\Delta U_{AC}=0$ . Red boxes mark scope mismatches, green boxes highlight the relevant state or endpoint evidence.

Views preserve the conditions and unresolved quantities of their source records. For example, $v=\omega=0$ at $S_{2}$ in Fig. 2 (b) denotes an instantaneous turning point, it does not establish static equilibrium, and $x^{\ast}$ remains unknown. Conditional branches retain their status in text and graphics, and missing defining conditions prevent a definite depiction. Single-state tasks use local relation views. Entity identities and coordinate conventions remain consistent, while unsupported magnitudes are represented schematically.

The package actually supplied to model call $t$ is its Delivered Snapshot :

[Tableau] $$\mathcal{O}_{t}=\bigl(\mathcal{M}_{t}^{\mathrm{in}},\mathcal{E}_{t},\mathcal{C}_{t},\mathcal{V}_{t}\bigr).$$ (4)

Here, $\mathcal{M}_{t}^{\mathrm{in}}$ is the exposed versioned memory, $\mathcal{E}_{t}$ the delivered source evidence, $\mathcal{C}_{t}$ the check feedback, and $\mathcal{V}_{t}$ the delivered views. Each view records its dependent fact versions and conditions, and delivery records identify the receiving call. A generated image or file path alone does not establish that the model received visual input. These records allow revision checks to distinguish available evidence from artifacts that were only generated.

Figure 3 makes two scope distinctions concrete. In the impact problem, the rolling relation $\omega^{-}=v/a$ applies before impact. Post-impact motion uses corner $A$ as the pivot, and the subsequent climbing calculation must use the post-impact angular velocity. In the gas problem, the adiabatic condition governs $B\rightarrow C$ , whereas $T_{C}=T_{A}$ relates the endpoints of $A\rightarrow C$ and yields $\Delta U_{AC}=0$ . The first example separates successive physical stages, the second distinguishes a local process from an endpoint relation. Both illustrate why premise applicability must be preserved from intermediate representations through the final equations.


#### 3.4 Source-Grounded Memory Revision

Correcting a premise requires reconsidering the conclusions supported by its previous version. PhysScope distinguishes new derivations and state-specific additions from replacements of existing premises. Additions extend memory, replacements use revise_facts and undergo source, reference, version, and scope checks (Fig. 2 (c)). The harness accepts, rejects, or defers each request. Revising a source reading requires matching source reinspection evidence delivered to the proposing call. Without this evidence, the request remains pending , and its candidate content cannot become an established premise in a rendered view. Pending and rejected requests leave active fact versions unchanged.

An accepted replacement commits a new version $f_{i}^{(v+1)}$ while preserving source evidence and revision history. Registered dependencies form a directed graph $\mathcal{D}$ from premises to dependent records, including facts, equations, and views. Replacing $f_{i}^{(v)}$ invalidates

[Tableau] $$\mathcal{I}_{i}^{(v)}=\{f_{i}^{(v)}\}\cup\operatorname{Desc}_{\mathcal{D}}\!\bigl(f_{i}^{(v)}\bigr).$$ (5)

Affected derivations must be re-established from current premises. Stale references are not automatically redirected to the new version. Propagation follows registered dependencies but cannot detect omitted ones.

For example, correcting a mass reading invalidates a registered energy equation and any views that depend on that version, even when the equation’s symbolic form is unchanged. Unrelated records remain usable. Invalidation identifies which derivations have lost valid support without deriving their replacements.

A committed change can affect subsequent reasoning only if updated memory and feedback reach another model call. After Call 2, the harness processes revision requests and delivers accepted changes in $\mathcal{O}_{3}$ if Call 3 occurs. This distinguishes committed updates from their delivery to subsequent inference. Appendix C.5 defines the trajectory-level activation criterion.


#### 3.5 Tool scheduling and bounded repair

The Tool Scheduler selects source inspections and supported views under a policy fixed before inference. The core configuration uses show-when-supported : it delivers relevant views that the renderer supports and current memory can faithfully express. Alternative policies additionally require an agent request or a registered issue involving state conditions, scope, or relations across states. All policies preserve unresolved quantities instead of filling them in to complete a diagram.

An unresolved semantic issue $u$ is eligible for continuation only if

[Tableau] $\displaystyle g(u)=\mathbf{1}\bigl[$ $\displaystyle\operatorname{AnswerRelevant}(u)$ (6) $\displaystyle\land\operatorname{Actionable}(u)$ $\displaystyle\land\operatorname{BudgetAvailable}\bigr].$

Eligible issues include ambiguous source readings, revisions lacking source reinspection, invalidated answer dependencies, and conflicting or missing conditions in key states. An issue must affect the answer and admit a concrete inspection or memory update. An unknown marker or an ordinary new derivation alone does not trigger semantic repair. The agent may request continuation, but the harness determines its eligibility.

Protocol recovery responds to a failed output contract and may regenerate an answer with changed physical content. The labels S and P identify the initiating cause of continuation: semantic repair or protocol recovery. Both paths share at most one additional model call. Before that call, the harness delivers current accepted memory, relevant check or protocol feedback, and supported evidence and views. The resulting submission undergoes the same checks, and residual blocking issues remain unresolved at termination. Candidates, trigger causes, and statuses are archived, operational retries and their costs are recorded separately.

> Algorithm 1 PhysScope with a shared continuation budget

Assess checks the output contract ( $p=1$ indicates a recoverable protocol failure) and applies UpdateCheck to a valid submission. UpdateCheck commits accepted changes, invalidates registered dependents, and reports unresolved issues. Delivery operations refresh permitted inputs while preserving history. The algorithm shows post-core continuation, transport retries are accounted for separately. Validation status reports the outcome of harness checks, not answer correctness.

#### 4.3 Mechanism Analysis


##### Visual feedback under matched information.

We compare H-T, H-S (static relation or force views), and H-F@2 (also supporting phase views). They share the draft from Call 1, memory, source evidence, and check feedback. Every depicted fact, condition, and unknown is also available in their shared text. H-S uses the same static layout as H-F@2 and leaves its phase region empty. The scores include tasks with unsupported or failed renders. Static views improve SolveAcc by 0.56 pp, and phase views add 0.83 pp. The corresponding JAcc gains are 0.53 and 0.94 pp (Table 3 ). These gains favor visual organization of shared premises. Repeated-run uncertainty is unmeasured.


##### Scope and dependency management.

Removing scope management reduces SolveAcc from 41.15% to 24.31%, while CAcc remains 93.75% (Table 4 ). Thus, recognizing content correctly does not suffice to use it under the right physical conditions. Removing dependency invalidation costs 5.44 pp in SolveAcc, with a 0.02 pp change in CAcc. Without invalidation, registered equations and views can remain available even after a premise they depend on has been replaced. Removing source checks within Source-Grounded Memory Revision reduces CAcc by 7.68 pp and SolveAcc by 2.71 pp. Removing physical checks preserves all four reading/grounding metrics while reducing SolveAcc by 3.08 pp (Appendix C.2 ). These distinct metric profiles further separate accurate source reading from maintaining usable physical premises during solving.


##### Delivery of revised premises.

We traced premise replacements accepted after Call 2 across Solve and Ground tasks (Appendix C.5 ). The harness invalidated at least one registered dependent and delivered the updated snapshot to Call 3 in 216 of 217 such trajectories for Gemini, 187 of 201 for DeepSeek, and 209 of 229 for GPT. For Gemini, 332 of 667 trajectories with accepted additions delivered new facts to a later call. Attributing the 5.44 pp loss to delivery requires trajectory-matched ablation results.

##### Controlled repair.

A separate policy experiment on all 986 PhysAlign [ 3 ] problems compares follow-up strategies after the H-F@2 core. Generic review, localized review, and controlled repair reach 24.25%, 25.99%, and 30.70% SolveAcc, respectively, compared with 22.92% for Stop (Fig. 4 a). Scores retain every parent, including cases without follow-up. The 4.71 pp advantage over localized review measures the combined policy’s net accepted-score gain. Across 889 pairs of Gemini answers (H-F@2 vs. H-F+R, including untriggered problems), 85 gained full content credit and none lost it (Appendix D.3 ). These scores measure content independently of acceptance. The 4.71 pp gap uses a different comparator, localized review.


##### Interventions on physical premises.

On 256 problems with verifiable key relations, correcting a memory premise raises SolveAcc from 31.92% to 34.29%, perturbing it lowers accuracy to 23.57%, and a harmless edit leaves it at 31.91% (Fig. 4 b). These results show sensitivity to premise edits, but correcting one premise need not resolve other reasoning errors. Corrected premises are used only in this analysis and excluded from the main method scores.


### 5 Discussion and Conclusion

PhysScope explicitly maintains premise applicability and revision consequences in VLM working memory. Results across six VLMs on PhysAlign [ 3 ] and Gemini on the evaluated PhysUniBench [ 8 ] subset support this design. Component studies distinguish source reading, scope enforcement, and dependency maintenance. These gains reflect complete inference policies. Protocol-triggered regeneration may alter answers, and accepted submissions may still be physically incorrect. Checks cover declared premises and dependencies under supported rules, but incorrect readings, omitted dependencies, and unsupported arguments may escape them. The point estimates establish neither repeated-run reliability nor equal inference cost. Future work should extend check coverage and assess repair reliability across tasks and runs.

### Appendix A Implementation Details

#### A.1 Memory Schema and Scope Semantics

Each fact stores an id , version , kind , claim text, and optional value/unit fields. Entity references ( owner , target ) identify participants, scope , conditions , sources , and depends record applicability, prerequisites, provenance, and direct dependencies. References use exact handles, such as f0008@1 . States also carry versions, conditions, and certainty labels, and derived records retain their registered state context. Only an explicit global scope grants shared applicability.

Claim status, validity, and validation have different meanings. Claim statuses are observed , derived , hypothesis , and unknown . Observed means a model reading. Validity tracks usability after updates. Check outcomes ( pass , fail , unknown , unsupported ), transaction decisions (accepted, pending, rejected), and answer status are separate fields.

In Case A, f0008@1 reads “ $B$ is initially uncharged” from text:question , with owner $B$ , value $0\,\mathrm{C}$ , and scope X. The derived f0013@1 records charge conservation in Y, depending on the switch condition f0010@1 and parallel-connection relation f0012@1 . Initial charge can legitimately support a conservation argument, but its role as an initial condition must remain distinct from a current charge in Y.


##### Operational applicability rule.

For an ordinary, untyped fact $f$ used directly in state $s$ , the domain check is

[Tableau] $$\operatorname{Applies}(f,s)=\mathbf{1}[\texttt{global}\in\operatorname{scope}(f)\;\lor\;s\in\operatorname{scope}(f)].$$ (7)

A local derivation checks each input in its declared producer context, its output must belong to that context. Historical ancestors retain their producer contexts and exact versions. Thus, preserving a historical record and using it as a current value are distinct operations.


##### Initial parameters and current values.

A fixed parameter $v_{0}:=v(A)$ may be declared globally when its meaning remains the initial velocity throughout the argument, this does not assert $v(B)=v_{0}$ . By contrast, a current-angle reading $\theta(A)$ belongs to A, and $\theta(B)$ belongs to B. The following schema example uses the same applicability rule as Case A:

[Tableau] Record Declared scope Direct use at B $v_{0}:=v(A)=0$ global Allowed as declared $\theta(A)=30^{\circ}$ A Blocked $\theta(B)=53^{\circ}$ B Allowed as declared

At B, a derivation that uses $v_{0}$ as an initial parameter and $\theta(B)$ as its current angle satisfies this domain contract. Substituting the A-scoped angle fails the contract. In Case A, the rejected derivation explicitly treated X-scoped inputs as current in Y, the accepted continuation instead cites the stored Y-scoped conservation relation. The numerical result stays $V/4$ , so the trace isolates a change in declared support.


##### Typed temporal contracts.

For typed quantities, value_at distinguishes invariant, interval, and point values, with an explicit state and boundary side. An initial-role input must match the receiving interval’s start boundary on its permitted side, the same point value cannot be used as an interval-current input. The implemented continuity transfer checks adjacent states, quantity and owner identity, frame and unit agreement, matching values, and an explicit continuity condition. Ordinary untyped records use Eq. ( 7 ), merely naming an input role initial does not override it. These checks enforce supplied declarations. An incorrect global annotation can therefore satisfy the domain test, and a physically valid argument still requires correct premises and sufficient declared support.

State evolution and premise correction have different semantics. Recording a later velocity adds a state-specific fact while preserving the initial velocity, correcting an erroneous reading replaces a version and invalidates its registered dependents. Neither a change of physical state nor the presence of an older record alone warrants invalidation.

Image boxes use normalized $(x_{0},y_{0},x_{1},y_{1})$ coordinates from the upper-left origin, rounded outward to pixel boundaries. Returned evidence contains the localized text or crop, source coordinates, identifier, and delivery record. Original inputs remain in the conversation history, neither case requests an additional inspection.

A delivered snapshot $\mathcal{O}_{t}$ fixes the memory versions, source evidence, check feedback, and views supplied to call $t$ . Memory and checks are serialized as text, visual evidence is attached as image content. Receipts associate view identifiers and image hashes with the receiving request, including retained history. Rendering alone does not establish delivery. In the figures, $\mathcal{O}_{1}$ denotes the initial question, image, and task contract, before memory construction, $\mathcal{O}_{2}$ is the first compiled snapshot. #### A.3 Rendering Constraints and Check Coverage

The renderer supports global and local relation views and comparisons between declared states. Temporal or mode panels require a meaningful state difference, a single state uses a local view. Unknowns and conditional states retain their labels, and missing defining conditions prevent a definite depiction. Schematic layout adds no physical premises. Accordingly, Case A compares switch modes, while Case B uses relational views of isotherm membership, G0 and G1 denote successive memory views, not different thermodynamic states.

Checks operate on declared records and delivery receipts. They cover reference and graph integrity, exact versions, input/output scopes, dependency validity, source reinspection, and cited-view scope and delivery. Answer checks require accepted support and agreement between answer slots and their declared results. Each check records its rule, affected records, outcome, enforcement status, and repairability. Unknown or unsupported outcomes block an answer only under the applicable enforcement and relevance rules.

Both cases use the ordinary relational profile, which indexes derivations as declared_only . Their domain and answer checks establish declared consistency, without proving the charge-conservation or ideal-gas arguments. Finite physical templates, when enabled, require specified quantities, units, conditions, and domains, they do not constitute a general physics solver. Ordinary scope checks, typed temporal contracts, and finite physical rules therefore have distinct coverage. A declared dependency can be structurally valid without its physical derivation being verified. The physical-check ablation measures the enabled module’s contribution within its evaluation configuration, it does not measure universal rule coverage or per-check detection accuracy.


#### A.4 Revision Commit and Dependency Invalidation


##### Staging and commit.

UpdateCheck binds a submission to its delivered snapshot and stages changes in a copy of memory. It resolves local keys and validates references, scopes, and the dependency graph before committing the core transaction. A source-backed replacement also requires matching reinspection evidence delivered to the proposing call. Missing reinspection leaves the transaction pending, a violated update contract rejects it. Both preserve active facts and retain diagnostic feedback. Accepted additions receive new identifiers at version 1, replacements increment the existing identifier’s version.


##### Propagation and answer validation.

Replacement invalidates the old premise and its registered descendants, unrelated records remain usable. For a registered chain $f_{i}^{(v)}\to d\to q$ , where $d$ is a derivation and $q$ a dependent result or view, committing $f_{i}^{(v+1)}$ requires $d$ and $q$ to be re-established against current premises. References are not redirected, and invalidation does not compute new conclusions. Neither displayed case instantiates this replacement rule: Case A rejects an extension, while Case B adds records. Older artifacts can remain visible in the preserved history, but invalidated versions cannot serve as active support in a new accepted submission. This restriction governs the explicit workspace and submission checks, it cannot guarantee that the VLM ignores stale information in its internal reasoning.

Answer validation follows the memory decision. It detects unavailable support after rejection in Case A and a mismatch after acceptance in Case B, preserving independently accepted memory updates. Coverage is limited to explicit declarations: omitted dependencies cannot be invalidated, and removing a flagged dependency does not establish a complete physical argument.


#### A.5 Tool Scheduling and Termination

The core show-when-supported policy delivers views that current memory can faithfully express. Semantic repair requires an enforced, answer-relevant blocking issue, an available corrective action, and remaining call budget. Actions include source reinspection and correction of memory or answer-support declarations. An unknown marker, a new fact, or an agent request alone is insufficient. Both cases trigger declaration repair without a new source crop.

Semantic repair and protocol recovery share one additional call after the two-call core. The harness refreshes memory, evidence, checks, and supported views before that call while retaining history. Updates affect only later inputs, never the snapshot that produced them. At budget exhaustion, remaining blocking checks accompany the latest candidate as unresolved , both the two-call answer and repair outcome are preserved. Termination does not certify the candidate or resolve the remaining uncertainty. Undelivered views remain audit artifacts, semantic repair, protocol recovery, and operational retries retain separate cost records. Protocol recovery can request a newly generated answer, allowing changes to physical content as well as submission form. The S/P categories in Table 11 identify the recorded trigger cause, independently of the resulting answer changes. Neither path grants another continuation after the shared allowance is spent. Assess in Algorithm 1 separates output-contract validation from UpdateCheck , an invalid submission cannot silently commit unvalidated changes.

#### B.2 Metrics and Answer Scoring


##### Local correctness.

We follow PhysAlign’s deterministic recognition and role-grounding definitions [ 3 ] . For probe $j$ , let $\hat{c}_{j}$ and $\hat{u}_{j}$ be the predicted reading and candidate identifier, $c_{j}$ and $r_{j}$ the reference reading and role, $N_{j}$ the prescribed normalizer, $E_{j}$ the candidate set, and $\phi_{j}$ its role mapping. The scores are

[Tableau] $\displaystyle C_{j}$ $\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}\!\left[N_{j}(\hat{c}_{j})=N_{j}(c_{j})\right],$ (8) $\displaystyle G_{j}$ $\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}\!\left[\hat{u}_{j}\in E_{j}\ \land\ \phi_{j}(\hat{u}_{j})=r_{j}\right],$ $\displaystyle J_{j}$ $\displaystyle=C_{j}G_{j}.$

Reading is evaluated at the specified evidence anchor. The normalizer admits only predefined formatting equivalences, it does not repair a physical interpretation. The two fields are scored independently, so a correct reading can coexist with an incorrect owner. No language-model judge is used for these local scores.


##### Balanced aggregation.

For each parent pool, let $\mathcal{S}_{it}$ contain the probes of parent $i$ and task type $t$ in evaluation set $\mathcal{S}$ . Write $m_{it}^{\mathcal{S}}=|\mathcal{S}_{it}|$ , let $N_{t}^{\mathcal{S}}$ count parents represented in type $t$ , and let $\alpha_{t}^{\mathcal{S}}\geq 0$ be the fixed type weights, summing to one. PhysAlign’s aggregation is

[Tableau] $$\operatorname{Agg}_{\mathcal{S}}(z)=\sum_{t}\frac{\alpha_{t}^{\mathcal{S}}}{N_{t}^{\mathcal{S}}}\sum_{i:m_{it}^{\mathcal{S}}>0}\frac{1}{m_{it}^{\mathcal{S}}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{it}}z_{j}.$$ (9)

Thus, parents with more annotations do not receive more weight within a task type. On $\mathcal{L}$ , the probes with independent reading and grounding targets, we report

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathrm{CAcc}$ $\displaystyle=100\operatorname{Agg}_{\mathcal{L}}(C),$ (10) $\displaystyle\mathrm{GAcc}_{L}$ $\displaystyle=100\operatorname{Agg}_{\mathcal{L}}(G^{\mathrm{base}}),$ $\displaystyle\mathrm{JAcc}$ $\displaystyle=100\operatorname{Agg}_{\mathcal{L}}(CG^{\mathrm{base}}).$

Here $C$ uses the Base condition. $\mathrm{GAcc}_{L}$ includes both correct and incorrect readings, JAcc measures their joint success on each probe.


##### Combined Base/+GT grounding.

Our tables combine the two reading conditions into one $\mathrm{GAcc}_{\mathcal{P}_{m}}$ column. For backbone $m$ , $\mathcal{P}_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{P}$ denotes the scheduled matched probe support, where $\mathcal{P}$ contains probes with valid +GT inputs. Base and +GT retain the same question, evidence anchors, candidates, and scoring rules, and use separate contexts. +GT additionally provides verified local readings, with the queried role withheld. With identical support and weights for both conditions, pooling their records gives

[Tableau] $$\mathrm{GAcc}_{\mathcal{P}_{m}}=100\operatorname{Agg}_{\mathcal{P}_{m}}\!\left(\frac{G^{\mathrm{base}}+G^{+\mathrm{GT}}}{2}\right).$$ (11)

The denominator accounts for both condition records per probe, this is a combined accuracy, not a sum of percentages or a Base-to-+GT gain. Supplied readings receive no CAcc or JAcc credit. The combined score should therefore be interpreted as grounding across two input conditions, not as performance without reading assistance.


##### Original-problem scoring.

For a pool of $N$ parents, PhysAlign SolveAcc averages normalized problem credit:

[Tableau] $$\mathrm{SolveAcc}=\frac{100}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}s_{i},\qquad s_{i}\in[0,1].$$ (12)

Choice answers use fixed matching rules, fill-in answers use binary reference-equivalence criteria, and short-answer or calculation responses receive rubric-based credit for conclusions and visible derivation steps. Scores are normalized by the problem’s maximum credit, the rubric and judge settings are specified in Appendix B.5 . Each parent has equal weight. Consequently, a PhysAlign SolveAcc difference cannot generally be converted into a count of fully solved problems. The PhysUniBench experiment instead reports binary final-answer correctness, so Eq. ( 12 ) uses $N=1{,}024$ and $s_{i}\in\{0,1\}$ there.


##### Missing outputs and validity.

Evaluation retains the scheduled denominator. Unanswered API requests, including timeouts, receive zero credit, failed local fields receive zero on their applicable metrics. This end-to-end failure policy differs from restricting evaluation to returned responses. Consequently, our API-based B0 scores can differ from benchmark reports that use a different response-availability policy. Fields without a recognition target, including supplied +GT readings, are inapplicable rather than incorrect. For solving, system failures and candidates with unresolved blocking dependencies are unsuccessful under the harness protocol, a valid answer produced by the predefined fallback is scored normally. The two-call candidate and any repaired candidate are retained separately. Harness acceptance does not itself establish answer correctness. All reported scores are percentages, and differences are in percentage points.

##### Visual representation.

##### Component ablations.

The 512-parent study removes one mechanism from the full harness at a time. Without scope management, stage annotations remain but runtime applicability enforcement is disabled. Without revision source checks, source reinspection no longer gates changes to original readings. Without dependency invalidation, records retain their versions and declared dependencies, but upstream revisions no longer invalidate affected descendants. Without physical checks, the finite physical-rule module is disabled. An ablated mechanism must not be reinstated through another filtering or feedback path. Its intervention takes effect before the next solving call, changing only final acceptance would test validation rather than the subsequent reasoning process. In particular, invalidation can influence reasoning only when a revision affects registered dependencies and a later call receives the changed workspace.


##### Review and controlled repair.

This separately executed study compares Stop, Generic review, Localized review, and Controlled repair on all 986 PhysAlign parents. Stop preserves the H-F@2 result. Generic review requests reconsideration. Localized review directs attention to an identified issue, Controlled repair applies the source, revision, and dependency checks described in Appendix A.5 . Controlled repair requires an answer-relevant ambiguity, a reading revision lacking required source inspection, an invalid answer dependency, or a conflicting or missing state condition, together with an actionable correction. An unknown quantity alone does not justify another call. S and P share a one-call continuation budget, problems without follow-up retain their two-call result in the full-pool score. Reaching the budget preserves residual issues as unresolved, it does not certify the latest candidate. Views generated only after the final call are audit artifacts, not evidence used by that call. The trigger counts in Table 11 belong to the main-run diagnostics, not this separate policy experiment. Sharing the 986-parent evaluation set does not establish shared generated candidates or identical trigger membership across experiments. We therefore interpret the review results as full-pool policy comparisons, without attributing their differences to a fixed number of semantic-only repairs.


##### Premise interventions.

The 256-parent study compares Predicted memory with Corrected premise, Perturbed premise, and Harmless edit conditions. The first two edits change the physical content of a selected premise, the harmless edit preserves that content and controls for editing alone. Each condition is compared with Predicted on this pool, with Read/Grounding targets excluded from the Solve task. Reference-assisted correction is a diagnostic intervention, not an inference-time capability of PhysScope. The resulting differences measure sensitivity to the edited premises. They do not establish that all unedited memory is correct or that every error is detectable by the harness.


##### Statistical interpretation.

We report aggregate point estimates without confidence intervals or repeated-run variance estimates. Small differences, including the static/phase increments and the harmless-edit contrast, are descriptive rather than claims of statistical significance. The independent unit for population-level uncertainty is the parent problem, multiple Ground queries and candidate versions from one parent are not independent replications.


#### B.5 Scoring and Inference Settings


##### Judge and scoring rubric.

Non-deterministic judgments use GPT-6 Astra [ 5 ] with medium reasoning effort, a 16,384-token output limit, no explicit temperature setting, and a 900-second timeout. Rubrics are fixed before candidate answers are inspected. They follow reference point allocations when available, otherwise, requested subparts receive equal weight, distributed equally across independent steps. Choice answers use fixed matching rules, and fill-in answers receive binary credit for reference equivalence. Calculation and short-answer responses can also earn credit for correct steps in the submitted explanation. The same judge resolves equivalence cases that require semantic assessment. Scoring uses the designated final-answer fields and, where applicable, the visible solution, omitted steps and hidden deliberation receive no credit. Each score is normalized as $\sum_{k}\mathrm{awarded}_{k}/\sum_{k}\mathrm{maximum}_{k}$ .


##### Quality control.

An independent audit covers 10% of samples. Human reviewers examine all disputed samples, resolve reference or scoring disagreements, and assign the final scores. The same frozen rubric applies to each candidate for a given problem. Appendix F.4 provides the grading instructions.


##### Inference settings.

The Gemini solver and Canvas-CoT [ 7 ] critic use Gemini-3.8-flash [ 2 ] with medium reasoning effort, temperature 1, a 32,768-token output limit, automatic image detail, and a 900-second timeout. Each method’s resource use includes its initial draft, even when that draft is shared across experimental conditions.

#### C.5 Committed Updates and Subsequent Delivery


##### Activation criterion.

A committed revision can affect later reasoning only if the updated memory reaches a subsequent call. For trajectory $i$ , let $R_{i}$ denote an accepted replacement, $I_{i}$ invalidation of a registered descendant or dependent view, and $D_{i}$ delivery of the updated snapshot to a later call. Complete activation is

[Tableau] $$z_{i}=\mathbf{1}[R_{i}\land I_{i}\land D_{i}].$$ (14)

Invalidating only the replaced root does not satisfy $I_{i}$ . Additions are counted separately from replacements.

##### Trigger causes.

##### Frequency and net score change.

Let $\mathcal{T}$ be the triggered problems and $s_{i}^{-},s_{i}^{+}\in[0,1]$ their accepted scores under H-F@2 and H-F+R. Since untriggered scores are unchanged, the full-pool gain in percentage points is

where $\overline{\Delta s}_{\mathcal{T}}$ is the mean net score change across all triggered problems. DeepSeek has more full-credit crossings, but crossing that threshold does not measure the initial score deficit or changes that remain below full credit. The aggregate differences therefore do not isolate the efficacy of semantic repair from protocol-triggered regeneration.


##### Acceptance and correctness.

An accepted full-credit transition requires both successful submission and full answer credit. An initially unaccepted answer receives zero accepted credit even if its content is correct. Table 13 therefore checks full-credit transitions on independently scored candidate pairs. Zero full-to-non-full transitions excludes that observed threshold regression. It does not exclude score decreases within non-full answers or guarantee future correctness. The harness checks declared premises and dependencies, unreported assumptions and unsupported physical rules remain outside its coverage.


#### D.3 Candidate Scores and Acceptance


##### Evaluation records and Raw inclusion.

We distinguish task-level evaluation records from candidate-level diagnostic records. Accepted uses the final task-level evaluation results for all 986 parent problems. Under the evaluation protocol, a problem receives zero credit if no valid answer is returned or the output is not accepted. Raw examines the relationship between answer content and acceptance status and requires complete, unambiguous links among the candidate answer, its version, and its content-scoring record. Inclusion depends solely on record completeness and linkage accuracy, irrespective of answer score or acceptance status.


##### Candidate identity and acceptance.

Let $q_{i}\in[0,1]$ be a candidate’s rubric score and $a_{i}\in\{0,1\}$ its acceptance status. On the Raw subset, both metrics use the same answer and content-scoring record. Accepted additionally applies the acceptance status, giving $s_{i}=a_{i}q_{i}\leq q_{i}$ . For matched H-F@2 ( $-$ ) and H-F+R ( $+$ ) candidates,

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\Delta(aq)_{i}={}$ $\displaystyle a_{i}^{+}(q_{i}^{+}-q_{i}^{-})$ (16) $\displaystyle+(a_{i}^{+}-a_{i}^{-})q_{i}^{-}.$

The terms separate content change at final acceptance from acceptance change at initial content quality. This decomposition requires candidate-level correspondence, an aggregate Raw–Accepted difference does not identify either term.


##### Coverage and complete-pool accounting.

Some parents have complete final-answer and task-scoring records but lack the version links required in the candidate-level diagnostic export. These parents are excluded from Raw but remain in the full-pool Accepted evaluation, with scores taken from their final task-level evaluation records, never imputed from the Raw mean. [Tableau] $$\bar{s}=\frac{n}{N}\bar{s}_{\mathcal{R}}+\frac{N-n}{N}\bar{s}_{\mathcal{U}}.$$

#### F.1 Drafting Prompt and Output Schema


##### Task and answer contracts.

Solve requests a solution to the original problem, whereas Ground requests the specified local reading or association. Prompts and define these tasks. PhysScope and B0/B1 reuse the same task-specific final-answer contract. These contracts govern the final answer payload, while drafting uses a separate workspace submission.


##### Drafting.

The first call submits submit_draft : source-backed facts, their scope, requested targets, and proposed inspections or views. The stage wrapper explicitly distinguishes this object from a final task answer. Prompt defines the shared record conventions, Prompt specifies workspace preparation. Scope and provenance are supplied by the model, so an observed record remains a model reading rather than a certified fact. The runtime checks explicit declarations under the coverage described in Appendix A.3 .


##### Output schema.

The following summary lists the principal draft and review fields. Solve freezes answer_targets during drafting. Ground instead registers the requested output bindings. Temporal profiles additionally expose the state, boundary, frame, and derivation declarations required by their supported rules.

Provider encodings differ: native tool calls carry a function schema, while the JSON-text interface includes the contract in the instructions. The strict interface also represents optional fields through nullable entries. Required fields follow the selected interface and task schema.


#### F.2 Solving and Repair Prompts


##### Solving against the delivered snapshot.

The second call submits submit_review using the original task, prior response, accepted memory, check feedback, and delivered evidence or views. The packet includes review_requirements for Solve or ground_review_requirements for Ground, together with the exact reference namespaces available to that call. Ordinary Solve links each answer to a supporting result (Prompt ), the temporal profile additionally requires explicit producers and boundary transfers (Prompt ). Ground maintains the distinction between a reading and its association with an entity (Prompt ).


##### Conditional repair.

Semantic repair and protocol recovery are classified by their initiating issue, protocol-triggered regeneration can also change answer content (Appendix D.1 ). Semantic repair continues through the same review interface with an updated delivered snapshot. It adds the preceding check outcomes and transaction status, available state references, and targeted source evidence or views, Controlled also receives the corresponding issue-localization packet. Prompt specifies source-backed revision, and Prompt supplies the repair feedback instruction. S and P share one extra-call budget, enforced by the host, a regenerated submission is checked normally, with no further continuation after that allowance is spent. Unresolved outcomes are retained as described in Appendix A.5 .

These instructions require the model to expose the support for its answer, acceptance is determined by the harness. In particular, naming a source or requesting a revision does not establish that the relevant evidence was delivered or that the replacement was committed. The snapshot and delivery records establish those events (Appendix A.2 ). This separation makes repair auditable while preserving the coverage limit demonstrated in Appendix E.3 : internally consistent support can still contain an incorrect premise.


#### F.3 Baseline and Review Prompts


##### Direct solving and self-review.

B0 receives the original task and shared answer contract without a workspace draft. B1 appends B0’s actual response and the instruction in Prompt , the appropriate answer-format contract follows it. If the first response is unavailable, the already-budgeted second call receives the explicit fallback instruction in Prompt .


##### Review policies.

Each continuation acts on its two-call candidate and the corresponding memory and feedback. Stop returns the two-call candidate without another call. Generic adds a general recheck without the newly localized issue/evidence packet. Localized receives issue localization and source reinspection, but submits only an answer. Controlled uses localization through the review interface, retaining versioned updates and runtime acceptance checks. Prompts – give the instructions for these review policies. Generic and Localized differ in targeted feedback, Controlled additionally represents and checks workspace updates. Both contrasts concern complete continuation policies and their accepted scores, neither alone separates a content improvement from a change in submission acceptance. The prompt definitions specify these policy differences, not shared trigger counts between the policy experiment and the main-run diagnostics.
