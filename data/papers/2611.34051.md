## Stable Gated DeltaNet-2: Understanding and Mitigating Recurrent State Instability

Linear attention offers an efficient solution for long-sequence modeling via linear complexity and fixed-size recurrent states. Gated DeltaNet-2 (GDN-2) introduces independent channel-wise control over erasure and writing, enhancing state-update expressiveness and in-context retrieval, yet it suffers from training failures during long-context training. We trace these failures to the asymmetry between reading and modification directions during erasure, which yields non-normal erase operators whose directional gains accumulate across tokens, triggering exponential state growth even without new writes. To resolve this, we propose Stable GDN-2 , employing symmetric erasure to ensure non-expansive state propagation and decay-regulated residual writing to preserve writing flexibility. Theoretically, we establish an $O(\sqrt{T})$ upper bound on recurrent state growth while retaining chunkwise parallel computation. Experiments on 1.6B parameter models show that Stable GDN-2 achieves stable training alongside improved long-context retrieval. Compared to GDN-2, it boosts the 128K average RULER score by 6.80 points in the recurrent setting and average accuracy across nine downstream tasks by 0.62 points in the hybrid setting.

### 1 Introduction

“A small leak will sink a great ship.”

– Benjamin Franklin

The growing demand for long-context understanding and extended generation ( Sun et al., 2025 ; Zhang et al., 2026 ) has made efficient sequence modeling an increasingly important challenge for large language models. To address the computational and memory bottlenecks of standard softmax attention ( Llama, 2023 ; Lu et al., 2026 ) , linear attention ( Katharopoulos et al., 2020 ; Sun et al., 2023 ) has emerged as an efficient alternative that compresses historical information into a fixed-size recurrent state, thereby enabling linear-time sequence processing and constant memory during autoregressive decoding. Building on this approach, recent advances in gating mechanisms and delta-rule-based updates ( Yang et al., 2024a ; Yang et al., 2025 ; Yang et al., 2024b ) have substantially enhanced the expressiveness of recurrent memory, enabling more fine-grained control over information retention and modification. Gated DeltaNet-2 (GDN-2) ( Hatamizadeh et al., 2026 ) further advances this line of work by replacing the shared scalar update gate in Gated DeltaNet (GDN) ( Yang et al., 2025 ) and Kimi Delta Attention (KDA) ( Team et al., 2025 ) with independent channel-wise erase and write gates, supporting more expressive recurrent state updates and improved in-context retrieval. Specifically, the erase gate reweights the key coordinates used to read content for erasure, while the modification is applied along the original key, allowing more flexible selection of content for erasure.

However, we argue that the asymmetry between the reading and modification directions during erasure in GDN-2 constitutes an inherent source of instability in its recurrent dynamics , potentially triggering catastrophic state explosion and ultimately causing training failures at long context. Our analysis shows that the asymmetric coupling between the erase gate and the key can introduce shear, preserving some state components while inducing additional components along other directions, as illustrated in Figure 1 (a). The resulting non-normal erasure operator can amplify certain state directions despite all its eigenvalues having magnitude at most one. When amplified output directions of earlier transitions align with expanding input directions of subsequent transitions, these gains can accumulate across the sequence, allowing the recurrent state to continue growing to the point of numerical overflow, even after new writes cease. As Franklin famously remarked, “A small leak will sink a great ship.”

To thoroughly investigate the underlying mechanisms of this instability, we examine recurrent state dynamics and their numerical and representational consequences in practical training. At a context length of 64K, we observe numerical failures across both pretraining and post-training settings, affecting recurrent as well as hybrid GDN-2 architectures. More importantly, numerical failures represent only the most visible manifestation of a broader issue: even in runs that complete training with finite losses, individual entries of the recurrent state can exceed $10^{36}$ , indicating substantial internal state amplification. Replay analysis shows that amplification from asymmetric erasure can concentrate state energy in a dominant direction and sharply reduce effective capacity before numerical failure, with little warning in the training loss. These observations highlight a fundamental limitation of loss-based monitoring and motivate a closer investigation of the underlying recurrent state dynamics that govern memory propagation over long sequences.

These findings motivate Stable GDN-2 , which controls recurrent state amplification through symmetric erasure while retaining channel-wise gating. Distributing the erase gate symmetrically across the two key factors aligns the reading and modification directions, ensuring non-expansive propagation under the standard key normalization and gate constraints, as illustrated in Figure 1 (b). To complement the resulting gated write, we introduce a residual writing path whose amplitude is regulated by decay. This design supports additional writing in channels where the erase gate is small while controlling the energy introduced by the residual. The update retains an input-dependent affine structure compatible with chunk-wise parallel computation.

We evaluate Stable GDN-2 through 64K-context pretraining in recurrent and hybrid architectures, comparing language modeling, downstream performance, and long-context retrieval against GDN-2 and existing stabilization methods. The results show that controlling recurrent state amplification while retaining channelwise erase gating supports improved retrieval and downstream performance, alongside competitive language modeling quality.

In summary, our contributions lie in the following aspects:

- • Understanding recurrent state instability in GDN-2. We characterize recurrent state instability in GDN-2 theoretically and empirically, showing that amplification can persist even without further writes. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to empirically identify training instability in GDN-2 and trace its origin to asymmetric erasure.

- • A principled stabilization mechanism for GDN-2. We propose Stable GDN-2 , which employs symmetric erasure to guarantee non-expansive state propagation while retaining channel-wise gating. We further introduce a decay-regulated residual writing path to preserve writing flexibility without compromising the stability guarantee. The resulting formulation supports chunkwise parallel computation.

- • Comprehensive empirical evaluation. We evaluate Stable GDN-2 against GDN-2 and existing stabilization methods using recurrent and hybrid models pretrained on 64K-token sequences. Compared with GDN-2, Stable GDN-2 improves the average RULER score at 128K by 6.80 points in the recurrent setting and average accuracy across nine downstream tasks by 0.62 percentage points in the hybrid setting, while maintaining competitive language modeling performance.

### 2 Preliminary

We consider a single attention head and omit head and layer indices. At position $t$ , the query and key are $\bm{q}_{t},\bm{k}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}$ , the value is $\bm{v}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{v}}$ , and the recurrent state is $\mathbf{S}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}\times d_{v}}$ . All vectors are column vectors. We use $\odot$ for elementwise multiplication and $\operatorname{Diag}(\bm{x})$ for the diagonal matrix with diagonal $\bm{x}$ .


#### 2.1 Linear Attention and the Gated Delta Rule

Linear attention accumulates historical key–value associations in a fixed-size matrix state and retrieves them using the current query ( Katharopoulos et al., 2020 ) . Following recent delta-rule models ( Yang et al., 2024b ; Yang et al., 2025 ) , we adopt the linear-kernel formulation without an attention normalizer:

[Tableau] $$\mathbf{S}_{t}=\mathbf{S}_{t-1}+\bm{k}_{t}\bm{v}_{t}^{\top},\quad\bm{o}_{t}=\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\top}\bm{q}_{t}.$$ (1)

Each outer product adds a new association to the memory, whose dimensions are independent of sequence length. This additive update does not explicitly revise the content already associated with the incoming key.

The delta rule corrects an existing association using the residual between the incoming value and the value retrieved from memory ( Schlag et al., 2021 ; Yang et al., 2024b ) . For a scalar update gate $\beta_{t}\in[0,1]$ , DeltaNet applies

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{t}$ $\displaystyle=\mathbf{S}_{t-1}+\beta_{t}\bm{k}_{t}\bigl(\bm{v}_{t}-\mathbf{S}_{t-1}^{\top}\bm{k}_{t}\bigr)^{\top}$ (2) $\displaystyle=\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\beta_{t}\bm{k}_{t}\bm{k}_{t}^{\top}\bigr)\mathbf{S}_{t-1}+\beta_{t}\bm{k}_{t}\bm{v}_{t}^{\top}.$

Here, $\mathbf{S}_{t-1}^{\top}\bm{k}_{t}$ is the value predicted by the current memory. We consider $\ell_{2}$ -normalized keys, $\|\bm{k}_{t}\|_{2}=1$ , throughout the delta-rule recurrences below. The expanded form separates the removal of retrieved content from the insertion of the new value. Both operations are controlled by the same scalar gate $\beta_{t}$ . Gated DeltaNet introduces a forget gate that decays the existing memory before applying the delta-rule correction ( Yang et al., 2025 ) . The resulting update takes the form

[Tableau] $$\mathbf{S}_{t}=\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\beta_{t}\bm{k}_{t}\bm{k}_{t}^{\top}\bigr)\mathbf{D}_{t}\mathbf{S}_{t-1}+\beta_{t}\bm{k}_{t}\bm{v}_{t}^{\top}.$$ (3)

Gated DeltaNet uses a scalar decay, $\mathbf{D}_{t}=d_{t}\mathbf{I}$ with $d_{t}\in(0,1]$ . Kimi Delta Attention instead uses $\mathbf{D}_{t}=\operatorname{Diag}(\mathbf{d}_{t})$ , where $\mathbf{d}_{t}\in(0,1]^{d_{k}}$ contains a separate decay factor for each key channel ( Team et al., 2025 ) . Setting $\mathbf{D}_{t}=\mathbf{I}$ recovers DeltaNet. Although KDA provides finer control over forgetting, its erase and write operations remain tied to the same scalar update gate.


#### 2.2 Gated DeltaNet-2

GDN-2 replaces the shared scalar update gate with separate channel-wise gates for erasing and writing ( Hatamizadeh et al., 2026 ) . Let $\bm{b}_{t}\in(0,1)^{d_{k}}$ and $\bm{w}_{t}\in(0,1)^{d_{v}}$ denote the erase and write gates, respectively.

Retaining KDA’s diagonal decay, GDN-2 updates the state as

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{t}={}$ $\displaystyle\bigl[\mathbf{I}-\bm{k}_{t}(\bm{b}_{t}\odot\bm{k}_{t})^{\top}\bigr]\mathbf{D}_{t}\mathbf{S}_{t-1}+\bm{k}_{t}(\bm{w}_{t}\odot\bm{v}_{t})^{\top}.$ (4)

The erase gate weights the key coordinates used to retrieve old content for removal, while the write gate scales the value coordinates committed to memory. These gates are input-dependent and parameterized independently.

GDN-2 allows the erase operation to read and modify the state along different directions. In GDN and KDA, both the retrieval of old content and the subsequent modification use the key $\bm{k}_{t}$ . In GDN-2, retrieval uses $\bm{b}_{t}\odot\bm{k}_{t}$ , while the modification is still applied along $\bm{k}_{t}$ . These vectors need not be parallel, so the erase matrix $\mathbf{I}-\bm{k}_{t}(\bm{b}_{t}\odot\bm{k}_{t})^{\top}$ need not be symmetric. This structural change arises from the channel-wise erase gate; the independent write gate determines the content injected into the state. The recurrence reduces to KDA when $\bm{b}_{t}=\beta_{t}\mathbf{1}_{d_{k}}$ and $\bm{w}_{t}=\beta_{t}\mathbf{1}_{d_{v}}$ , and further reduces to Gated DeltaNet when $\mathbf{D}_{t}=d_{t}\mathbf{I}$ .

GDN-2 evaluates the same recurrence through chunkwise parallel computation during training ( Hatamizadeh et al., 2026 ) . For chunks of length $C$ , let $\mathbf{S}_{[n]}=\mathbf{S}_{nC}$ denote the state entering chunk $n$ , which contains positions $nC+1,\ldots,(n+1)C$ ; its exit state is $\mathbf{S}_{[n+1]}=\mathbf{S}_{(n+1)C}$ . Token interactions within each chunk are computed through parallel matrix operations, while the boundary states carry information between successive chunks.


### 3 Understanding State Instability in GDN-2

In GDN-2, the asymmetric coupling between the channel-wise erase gate and the key yields a generally non-normal erase operator, breaking the non-expansiveness guaranteed by symmetric erasure in GDN and KDA. Across time-varying transitions, directional gains from this non-normality can accumulate when amplified state directions align with subsequent expanding directions, allowing stored memory to grow even after new writes cease. In the following, we theoretically formalize this mechanism (Section 3.1 ) and show that it manifests in long-context training as both sudden numerical failures and extreme state excursions hidden beneath normal loss trajectories (Section 3.2 ).


#### 3.1 Theoretical Analysis

GDN-2 employs channel-wise erase gates to offer fine-grained control over recurrent states. Erasure reads the decayed recurrent state $\mathbf{D}_{t}\mathbf{S}_{t-1}$ using the gated key $\mathbf{b}_{t}\odot\mathbf{k}_{t}$ and applies the resulting modification along the original key $\mathbf{k}_{t}$ . The stability question is whether this asymmetric modification can amplify the recurrent state even when every gate entry lies in $(0,1)$ .

The nontrivial action of erasure is confined to the subspace spanned by the key and its gated counterpart. Omitting the time index, let $\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{b}\odot\mathbf{k}$ and $\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{k}\mathbf{a}^{\top}$ . For $\|\mathbf{k}\|_{2}=1$ , choose $\mathbf{e}_{\parallel}=\mathbf{k}$ and decompose

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathbf{a}$ $\displaystyle=\mu\mathbf{e}_{\parallel}+\gamma\mathbf{e}_{\perp},$ (5) $\displaystyle\mu$ $\displaystyle=\mathbf{k}^{\top}\mathbf{a},\qquad\gamma=\|\mathbf{a}-\mu\mathbf{k}\|_{2},$

where $\mathbf{e}_{\perp}$ is a unit vector orthogonal to $\mathbf{e}_{\parallel}$ ; when $\gamma=0$ , any such vector may be chosen. Expressing $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{e}_{\parallel}=(1-\mu)\mathbf{e}_{\parallel}$ and $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{e}_{\perp}=\mathbf{e}_{\perp}-\gamma\mathbf{e}_{\parallel}$ in this orthonormal basis gives the two-dimensional representation

[Tableau] $$\mathbf{T}=\begin{pmatrix}1-\mu&-\gamma\\ 0&1\end{pmatrix},$$ (6)

with the identity acting on the orthogonal complement. Here, $\mu$ controls attenuation along the key, while $\gamma$ measures the shear. It means that an orthogonal component is retained while contributing an additional component along the key. This coupling depends jointly on the erase gate and the key.

Nonzero shear $\gamma$ creates directions in which erasure amplifies the recurrent state. The following theorem gives the exact maximum gain of a single erasure step.

Let $\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{k}(\mathbf{b}\odot\mathbf{k})^{\top}$ , where $d_{k}\geq 2$ , $\|\mathbf{k}\|_{2}=1$ , and $\mathbf{b}\in[0,1]^{d_{k}}$ . With $\mu$ and $\gamma$ defined in Eq. ( 5 ), its operator spectral norm is

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}$ $\displaystyle=\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A})$ (7) $\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\mu^{2}+\gamma^{2}}+\sqrt{(2-\mu)^{2}+\gamma^{2}}}{2}.$

In particular, $\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}=1$ if and only if $\gamma=0$ , equivalently, $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{k}$ are collinear. Otherwise, $\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}>1$ .

The proof is given in Appendix A.1 . The spectral norm $\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}$ measures the maximum energy amplification factor of the recurrent state $\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{S}\|_{F}/\|\mathbf{S}\|_{F}$ . Accordingly, Theorem 3.1 shows that erasure alone can increase the energy of recurrent state, defined as $E(\mathbf{S})=\|\mathbf{S}\|_{F}^{2}$ , even without any new write. For GDN and KDA, scalar erase gating gives $\mathbf{a}=\beta\mathbf{k}$ and $\gamma=0$ , recovering non-expansive symmetric erasure. As a simple example of the geometry in Figure 1 , consider a single-column recurrent state $\mathbf{S}_{t}=\mathbf{e}_{\perp}$ . When $\gamma>0$ , erasure gives

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\mathbf{S}_{t}$ $\displaystyle=\mathbf{e}_{\perp}-\gamma\mathbf{e}_{\parallel},$ (8) $\displaystyle E(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{S}_{t})$ $\displaystyle=1+\gamma^{2}>E(\mathbf{S}_{t})=1.$

This amplification can persist in the full transition $\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{D}$ . In particular, an admissible scalar decay $\mathbf{D}=d\mathbf{I}$ with $1/\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}<d<1$ gives $\|\mathbf{M}\|_{2}=d\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}>1$ .

The analysis of a single update shows that a full transition $\mathbf{M}_{t}=\mathbf{A}_{t}\mathbf{D}_{t}$ can increase recurrent state energy even without a new write. Along a token sequence, the recurrent state evolves through successive transitions that depend on the input. Their composition determines whether this amplification can persist. Suppose no further content is written after token $s$ . The recurrent state at token $t>s$ then satisfies

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{t}$ $\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{t:s+1}\mathbf{S}_{s},$ (9)

where $\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{t:s+1}=\mathbf{M}_{t}\cdots\mathbf{M}_{s+1}$ . When an amplified output direction of $\mathbf{M}_{j}$ aligns with an expanding input direction of $\mathbf{M}_{j+1}$ , the transition at the next token can amplify it further. Repeated alignment across tokens can therefore allow gains to accumulate, posing a risk of sustained recurrent state energy growth even after writing has ceased.

Such alignment can be realized by an admissible sequence of GDN-2 updates, yielding unbounded recurrent state growth after a single finite write.

There exist normalized keys, together with erase, write, and decay gates whose entries lie in $(0,1)$ , such that the GDN-2 recurrence, initialized at $\mathbf{S}_{0}=\mathbf{0}$ , satisfies

[Tableau] $$\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\mathbf{S}_{t}\|_{F}=\infty,$$ (10)

after a finite nonzero write at the first token, even with no writes at subsequent tokens.

Appendix A.2 provides an explicit example that alternates two admissible transitions with $\mathbf{M}_{b}=\mathbf{M}_{a}^{\top}$ and $\lambda=\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{M}_{a})^{2}>1$ . Starting from an initial write along a leading right singular direction of $\mathbf{M}_{a}$ , each pair returns the recurrent state to the same direction and multiplies its norm by $\lambda$ , yielding exponential growth without further writes.

In summary, the asymmetric coupling between the erase gate and the key in GDN-2 can yield a non-normal erase operator with expanding directions, breaking the non-expansiveness of symmetric erasure in GDN and KDA. When amplified output directions align with expanding input directions at subsequent tokens, the gains can accumulate, allowing sustained growth in recurrent state energy without further writes and creating a risk of numerical overflow in finite precision arithmetic. The analysis establishes the existence of such trajectories, while the occurrence and extent of amplification in practical training depend on how the transitions induced by the token sequence and learned parameters interact with the recurrent state.

### 4 Stable GDN-2

The analysis above shows that the asymmetric coupling between the channel-wise erase gate and the key in GDN-2 can amplify the recurrent state. Can we retain the fine-grained control of erase while restoring non-expansive recurrent state propagation? To this end, we propose Stable GDN-2 , which restores non-expansive propagation through symmetric erasure and introduces a residual write, regulated by decay, to supplement writing suppressed by symmetric gating.

> Figure 3: Stable GDN-2 architecture and recurrent state update.


#### 4.1 Symmetric Erasure and Residual Writing

The channel-wise erase gate in GDN-2 provides fine-grained control over erasure, but its asymmetric coupling with the key in $\mathbf{A}_{t}$ (Eq. ( 4 )) introduces a risk of recurrent state amplification. To retain channel-wise erase gating while aligning the reading and modification directions, we split the erase gate into two square-root factors and apply one to each key factor. Defining $\mathbf{u}_{t}=\sqrt{\mathbf{b}_{t}}\odot\mathbf{k}_{t}$ gives the symmetric erase operator

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{sym}}$ $\displaystyle=\mathbf{I}-(\sqrt{\mathbf{b}_{t}}\odot\mathbf{k}_{t})(\sqrt{\mathbf{b}_{t}}\odot\mathbf{k}_{t})^{\top}$ (11) $\displaystyle=\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{u}_{t}(\mathbf{u}_{t})^{\top}.$

This preserves the diagonal attenuation of the original GDN-2 operator, $(\mathbf{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{sym}})_{ii}=(\mathbf{A}_{t})_{ii}=1-b_{t,i}k_{t,i}^{2}$ .

Using $\mathbf{u}_{t}$ for reading, modification, and writing, we can rewrite the delta update in this symmetric formulation as

[Tableau] $$\mathbf{S}_{t}=\mathbf{D}_{t}\mathbf{S}_{t-1}+\mathbf{u}_{t}\left[\mathbf{z}_{t}-(\mathbf{D}_{t}\mathbf{S}_{t-1})^{\top}\mathbf{u}_{t}\right]^{\top},$$ (12)

where $\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{w}_{t}\odot\mathbf{v}_{t}$ is the gated value. This retains the delta update structure and ensures non-expansive recurrent state propagation. However, as $b_{t,i}$ approaches zero, both erasure and the base write in channel $i$ vanish. This tightly couples erasing stored content with injecting new information, restricting the ability to write while suppressing erasure.

To supplement the suppressed writes, we introduce a residual along $\mathbf{k}_{t}-\mathbf{u}_{t}$ and regulate its amplitude by decay. Decay retains a fraction $d_{t,i}^{2}$ of the recurrent state energy in channel $i$ . We therefore scale the residual by the square root of the complementary fraction,

[Tableau] $$\mathbf{r}_{t}=\sqrt{1-\mathbf{d}_{t}^{2}}\odot(\mathbf{k}_{t}-\mathbf{u}_{t}).$$ (13)

As $d_{t,i}$ approaches one, the residual write vanishes in that channel; stronger decay permits a larger additional write. The complete update becomes

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{t}$ $\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathbf{D}_{t}\mathbf{S}_{t-1}+(\mathbf{u}_{t}+\mathbf{r}_{t})\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}.$ (14)

Beyond non-expansive propagation, this design yields a tighter bound on recurrent state energy under continued writing. Section 4.2 establishes that its growth is controlled by cumulative write energy, $\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\mathbf{z}_{t}\|_{2}^{2}$ .

Figure 3 shows the architecture and the two write contributions. We retain the gate parameterization of GDN-2 ( Hatamizadeh et al., 2026 ) , using separate sigmoid projections for the erase $\mathbf{b}_{t}$ and write gates $\mathbf{w}_{t}$ and the same exponential decay parameterization with rates constrained by softplus. Queries and keys follow the original $\ell_{2}$ normalization; $\mathbf{u}_{t}$ and $\mathbf{r}_{t}$ retain their gate-dependent magnitudes without further normalization.


#### 4.2 Stability Guarantees

Symmetric erasure removes the expanding directions identified in Theorem 3.1 . For the transition $\mathbf{M}_{t}=\mathbf{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathbf{D}_{t}$ in Eq. ( 14 ), this prevents recurrent state amplification in the absence of new writes.

The symmetric erase operator in Stable GDN-2 satisfies $\|\mathbf{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{sym}}\|_{2}\leq 1$ , and the corresponding transition obeys

[Tableau] $$\|\mathbf{M}_{t}\|_{2}\leq\|\mathbf{d}_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq 1.$$ (15)

When the write term at token $t$ is zero, the recurrent state therefore satisfies $\|\mathbf{S}_{t}\|_{F}\leq\|\mathbf{d}_{t}\|_{\infty}\|\mathbf{S}_{t-1}\|_{F}\leq\|\mathbf{S}_{t-1}\|_{F}$ .

Key normalization and the erase gate parameterization give $\|\mathbf{u}_{t}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i}b_{t,i}k_{t,i}^{2}\leq\|\mathbf{k}_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 1$ . Hence $0\preceq\mathbf{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{sym}}\preceq\mathbf{I}$ . As in the analysis of symmetric erasure in GDN and KDA in Section 3.1 , the erase operator has spectral norm at most one. By submultiplicativity, $\|\mathbf{M}_{t}\|_{2}\leq\|\mathbf{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{sym}}\|_{2}\|\mathbf{D}_{t}\|_{2}\leq\|\mathbf{d}_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq 1$ . With no new write, $\mathbf{S}_{t}=\mathbf{M}_{t}\mathbf{S}_{t-1}$ , which gives the stated Frobenius norm bound. ∎

Consequently, if writes cease after position $s$ , then $\|\mathbf{S}_{t}\|_{F}\leq\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{t:s+1}\|_{2}\|\mathbf{S}_{s}\|_{F}\leq\|\mathbf{S}_{s}\|_{F}$ for every $t>s$ , using the propagation notation from Eq. ( 9 ). With continued writing, the recurrent state can grow through the accumulation of new content, with each contribution propagated with gain at most one. Expanding Eq. ( 14 ) gives $\mathbf{S}_{T}=\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{T:1}\mathbf{S}_{0}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{T:t+1}(\mathbf{u}_{t}+\mathbf{r}_{t})\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}$ , with empty products equal to $\mathbf{I}$ . Together with $|(\mathbf{u}_{t}+\mathbf{r}_{t})_{i}|\leq|k_{t,i}|$ , this yields

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{T}\|_{F}$ $\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{S}_{0}\|_{F}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|(\mathbf{u}_{t}+\mathbf{r}_{t})\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\|_{F}$ (16) $\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{S}_{0}\|_{F}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\mathbf{k}_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\|_{F}.$

Appendix C.1 gives the coordinatewise argument. This bound has the same additive form as recurrent state accumulation in the Linear Transformer of Katharopoulos et al. (2020) . For $\mathbf{S}_{0}=\mathbf{0}$ and $\|\mathbf{v}_{t}\|_{2}\leq V$ , it gives $\|\mathbf{S}_{T}\|_{F}\leq TV$ , since $\|\mathbf{z}_{t}\|_{2}=\|\mathbf{w}_{t}\odot\mathbf{v}_{t}\|_{2}\leq\|\mathbf{v}_{t}\|_{2}$ .

Decay regulation tightens this bound from $O(T)$ to $O(\sqrt{T})$ by jointly controlling propagation and writing.

Consider the Stable GDN-2 recurrence in Eq. ( 14 ), initialized at $\mathbf{S}_{0}=\mathbf{0}$ . If $\|\mathbf{v}_{t}\|_{2}\leq V$ for all tokens, with a constant $V>0$ , then for every $T\geq 1$ ,

[Tableau] $$\|\mathbf{S}_{T}\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{\frac{3T}{2}}\,V.$$ (17)

The proof in Appendix C.2 jointly bounds propagation and writing, without requiring decay factors to remain uniformly below one. Symmetric erasure eliminates the exponential amplification through transition products identified in Section 3.1 , while decay regulation further provides an $O(\sqrt{T})$ bound on the recurrent state norm under bounded values and zero initialization. Removing decay regulation preserves non-expansive transitions but need not admit an $O(\sqrt{T})$ bound with a constant independent of the gate values, as shown in Appendix C.3 .


#### 4.3 Chunkwise Parallel Training

Now we show that Stable GDN-2 can keep the same chunkwise structure as GDN and KDA. Its transition remains Diagonal-Plus-Low-Rank (DPLR), while the residual path contributes an additional input-dependent write. Defining the modification vector $\boldsymbol{\delta}_{t}=\mathbf{z}_{t}-(\mathbf{D}_{t}\mathbf{S}_{t-1})^{\top}\mathbf{u}_{t}$ , then Eq. ( 14 ) becomes

[Tableau] $$\mathbf{S}_{t}=\mathbf{D}_{t}\mathbf{S}_{t-1}+\mathbf{u}_{t}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{t}^{\top}+\mathbf{r}_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}.$$ (18)

Each token introduces a single state-dependent correction $\boldsymbol{\delta}_{t}$ , while the residual write $\mathbf{r}_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}$ is fully determined by the input. The residual contributions therefore enter the correction equations as known terms on the right-hand side. Since each correction depends only on earlier corrections, these equations form a single unit lower triangular system over the token positions within a chunk.

Consider a chunk of $C$ tokens, with local indices $i=1,\ldots,C$ and initial state $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{in}}$ . Following the decay factorization used in GDN-2, write $\mathbf{d}_{i}=\exp(\mathbf{g}_{i})$ and define the cumulative decay vector $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{g}_{j}\right)$ , with $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{0}=\mathbf{1}$ , where exponentials are applied elementwise. Let $\Delta,Z\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times d_{v}}$ contain rows $\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{\top}$ and $\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\top}$ , respectively, and define the decay-weighted factors

[Tableau] $\displaystyle E_{i,:}$ $\displaystyle=(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}\odot\mathbf{u}_{i})^{\top},$ (19) $\displaystyle\bar{U}_{i,:}$ $\displaystyle=(\mathbf{u}_{i}\oslash\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i})^{\top},$ $\displaystyle\bar{R}_{i,:}$ $\displaystyle=(\mathbf{r}_{i}\oslash\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i})^{\top}.$

where $\oslash$ denotes element-wise division. The causal interactions are

[Tableau] $\displaystyle L$ $\displaystyle=\operatorname{tril}\left(E(\bar{U})^{\top},-1\right),$ (20) $\displaystyle J$ $\displaystyle=\operatorname{tril}\left(E\bar{R}^{\top},-1\right).$

Here, the strictly lower triangular masks restrict interactions to earlier positions. Thus, $L\Delta$ and $JZ$ capture the effects of past delta corrections and residual writes, respectively, giving

[Tableau] $$(I_{C}+L)\Delta=(I_{C}-J)Z-E\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{in}}.$$ (21)

The DPLR transition determines the coefficient matrix $I_{C}+L$ , while the residual path adds $-JZ$ to the right-hand side. Since $JZ$ depends only on inputs within the chunk, it can be precomputed independently of the incoming state and incorporated into the same triangular solve.

To separate the incoming-state dependence, we define the erase-side and write-side WY auxiliaries $\mathcal{W}$ and $\mathcal{U}$ through

[Tableau] $$(I_{C}+L)\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{W}&\mathcal{U}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}E&(I_{C}-J)Z\end{bmatrix}.$$ (22)

Both auxiliaries depend only on chunk inputs and can therefore be computed independently across chunks. Once the incoming state is available, the corrections are recovered as $\Delta=\mathcal{U}-\mathcal{W}\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{in}}$ .

The final state and all token outputs within the chunk are computed by aggregating contributions from the incoming state and both update paths. Define the factors propagated to the chunk boundary as $U_{\mathrm{tail}}=\bar{U}\operatorname{Diag}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{C})$ and $R_{\mathrm{tail}}=\bar{R}\operatorname{Diag}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{C})$ . For output computation, let $(Q_{\gamma})_{i,:}=(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}\odot\mathbf{q}_{i})^{\top}$ and define

[Tableau] $\displaystyle P_{u}$ $\displaystyle=\operatorname{tril}\left(Q_{\gamma}(\bar{U})^{\top}\right),$ (23) $\displaystyle P_{r}$ $\displaystyle=\operatorname{tril}\left(Q_{\gamma}\bar{R}^{\top}\right).$

These masks include the diagonal because each output reads the state after the current update. The outgoing state and output block are then

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{out}}$ $\displaystyle=\operatorname{Diag}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{C})\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{in}}+(U_{\mathrm{tail}})^{\top}\Delta+R_{\mathrm{tail}}^{\top}Z,$ (24) $\displaystyle O$ $\displaystyle=Q_{\gamma}\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{in}}+P_{u}\Delta+P_{r}Z,$

where row $i$ of $O$ is $\mathbf{o}_{i}^{\top}$ . Together, these expressions complete the forward computation within each chunk, yielding all token outputs and the state passed to the next chunk.

Together, Eqs. ( 22 ) and ( 24 ) organize the complete Stable GDN-2 update into the standard chunkwise parallel framework. The auxiliary matrices can be prepared independently for each chunk, followed by recurrent state propagation across chunks and parallel computation of the output blocks. Within this schedule, residual writing contributes additional matrix products to the correction, state, and output computations while retaining the same $C\times C$ triangular system size. Stable GDN-2 can therefore reuse the chunkwise training structure of delta-rule models, with linear complexity in sequence length for fixed chunk size and head dimensions.


### 5 Experiments


#### 5.1 Experimental Setup


##### 5.1.1 Datasets

In this work, we train models through 64K-context pretraining on Prolong dataset ( Gao et al., 2025 ) . We merge the 64K and 512K ProLong data, retokenize the combined corpus with the TinyLlama ( Zhang et al., 2024 ) tokenizer, and repack it into uniform sequences of 64K tokens. The resulting training stream contains approximately 82.78B tokens and uses next-token prediction over all non-padding tokens. Recurrent states are reset at each training-sample boundary and propagate continuously across documents within each packed sequence.


##### 5.1.2 Baselines

We compare Stable GDN-2 with four baselines under the same training protocol. GDN-2 ( Hatamizadeh et al., 2026 ) provides the direct reference for assessing the effect of stabilization, while KDA ( Team et al., 2025 ) represents a related delta-rule model with channel-wise forgetting. D-Clip ( Su, 2026 ) caps each decay factor at $1/\|\mathbf{A}_{t}\|_{2}$ , making the full transition non-expansive through stronger decay while preserving the original erase gate. $\beta$ -Guard ( Hatamizadeh, 2026 ) instead shrinks channel-wise deviations of the erase gate from its key-weighted mean when needed to satisfy a sufficient condition for non-expansive transitions, preserving both this mean and the original decay. Both methods retain the original GDN-2 write term. In comparison, Stable GDN-2 combines symmetric erasure with decay-regulated residual writing, as described in Section 4.1 .

Evaluation. Following ( Hatamizadeh et al., 2026 ) , we assess zero-shot common-sense reasoning on nine tasks: PIQA ( Bisk et al., 2020 ) , HellaSwag ( Zellers et al., 2019 ) , WinoGrande ( Sakaguchi et al., 2020 ) , ARC-C and ARC-E ( Clark et al., 2018 ) , OpenBookQA ( Mihaylov et al., 2018 ) , SocialIQA ( Sap et al., 2019 ) , BoolQ ( Clark et al., 2019 ) , and LAMBADA ( Paperno et al., 2016 ) . Overall Analysis . Taken together, Stable GDN-2 achieves a favorable balance between recurrent state stability and modeling capability. In particular, under the Recurrent setting, our method consistently achieves the highest average RULER scores across all three evaluated context lengths. These results suggest that effective control of recurrent state amplification can be achieved without sacrificing the model’s ability to utilize long-term historical information through a carefully designed combination of state erasure and information writing mechanisms.

### 6 Related Work


#### 6.1 Linear Attention and Delta-rule Models

Recent advances in linear attention have improved sequence modeling through more expressive gating mechanisms and recurrent state updates ( Yang et al., 2024a ; Yang et al., 2025 ; Team et al., 2025 ; Sun et al., 2026 ; Hatamizadeh et al., 2026 ) . The basic formulation uses kernel feature maps to summarize the prefix in a recurrent state of key–value outer products, allowing queries to retrieve information at a cost independent of context length ( Katharopoulos et al., 2020 ) . Interpreting this recurrence as a fast-weight programmer ( Schlag et al., 2021 ) motivates replacing additive writes with delta-rule updates that selectively revise existing associations ( Irie et al., 2021 ) , while chunkwise parallel algorithms make these updates practical for training at scale ( Yang et al., 2024b ) . A parallel thread governs how long the state retains its contents, through fixed exponential decay in RetNet ( Sun et al., 2023 ) , input-dependent per-channel decay in gated linear attention ( Yang et al., 2024a ) , depth-wise gate floors and state expansion in HGRN and HGRN2 ( Qin et al., 2023 ; Qin et al., 2024 ) , and selective discretizations in the Mamba line ( Gu & Dao, 2024 ; Dao & Gu, 2024 ; Lahoti et al., 2026 ) . Gated DeltaNet applied the delta correction to a decayed state ( Yang et al., 2025 ) , and Kimi Delta Attention refined the decay to a diagonal ( Team et al., 2025 ) . GDN-2 ( Hatamizadeh et al., 2026 ) then split the shared scalar gate into independent channel-wise erase and write gates, an asymmetry EDA approaches from the write-address side ( Li et al., 2026 ) , while DeltaProduct ( Siems et al., 2025 ) and RWKV-7 ( Peng et al., 2025 ) pursue expressive transitions by other means. In our work, we identify recurrent state amplification as a fundamental source of instability in GDN-2 and propose Stable GDN-2 to ensure non-expansive memory propagation through symmetric erasure.


#### 6.2 Memory Capacity and Stabilization of Recurrent Models

A fixed-size state bounds how much history a recurrent model can carry. Attention reads as associative memory ( Bietti et al., 2023 ) ; capacity rather than parameter count separates attention from gated convolution, and the resulting recall–throughput trade-off can be quantified ( Arora et al., 2024a ; Arora et al., 2024b ) ; no fixed latent state reproduces arbitrary inputs verbatim ( Jelassi et al., 2024 ) . Designs respond by widening the state ( Qin et al., 2024 ) , letting it grow logarithmically ( Guo et al., 2026 ) , or externalizing it ( Behrouz et al., 2025 ) . Whether a state of a given size propagates without numerical degradation is a separate question with older roots: long-range learning is tied to products of per-step Jacobians ( Bengio et al., 1994 ) , gradients are rescaled by their norm ( Pascanu et al., 2013 ) , and transitions may be confined to unitary matrices ( Arjovsky et al., 2016 ) . In transformers the analogue targets logits rather than states, via query–key normalization ( Wortsman et al., 2024 ) or direct bounds on how far logits may move in one step ( Anson & Aitchison, 2025 ) . Each of these constrains a single transition; accumulation is left implicit, which suffices for normal transitions but not for the non-symmetric erase matrix of GDN-2. In our work, we show that non-expansive state propagation of GDN-2 can be guaranteed through symmetric erasure, preventing cumulative amplification while preserving flexible memory updates.


### 7 Conclusion

In this work, we identify asymmetric erasure as a fundamental source of recurrent state amplification in GDN-2, showing theoretically how non-normal state transitions can accumulate across tokens and lead to numerical instability. To address this issue, we propose Stable GDN-2 , which combines symmetric erasure with decay-regulated residual writing to ensure non-expansive state propagation while preserving flexible memory updates and chunkwise parallel computation. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method stabilizes long-context training and delivers strong long-context retrieval and downstream task performance, while maintaining competitive language modeling quality. These findings highlight the importance of controlling recurrent state dynamics and demonstrate that numerical stability can be substantially improved without sacrificing the expressive capacity of recurrent memory.

### Appendix A Proofs for the Theoretical Analysis


#### A.1 Single-Step Erasure Gain

Under the assumptions of the theorem, Eq. ( 5 ) gives

[Tableau] $$\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}b_{i}k_{i}^{2}\in[0,1].$$ (25)

The second column of $\mathbf{T}$ in Eq. ( 6 ) has norm $\sqrt{1+\gamma^{2}}\geq 1$ . Together with the identity action on the orthogonal complement, this implies $\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}=\|\mathbf{T}\|_{2}$ .

Let $\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq 0$ be the singular values of $\mathbf{T}$ . Their squared sum and product satisfy

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}$ $\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{T}\|_{F}^{2}=(1-\mu)^{2}+\gamma^{2}+1,$ (26) $\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}$ $\displaystyle=|\det\mathbf{T}|=1-\mu.$

Consequently,

[Tableau] $\displaystyle(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2})^{2}$ $\displaystyle=(2-\mu)^{2}+\gamma^{2},$ (27) $\displaystyle(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})^{2}$ $\displaystyle=\mu^{2}+\gamma^{2}.$

Taking the nonnegative square roots yields

[Tableau] $$\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}=\sigma_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{\mu^{2}+\gamma^{2}}+\sqrt{(2-\mu)^{2}+\gamma^{2}}}{2},$$ (28)

which proves Eq. ( 7 ). When $\gamma=0$ , this expression equals $1$ because $\mu\in[0,1]$ . When $\gamma>0$ , the two square roots strictly exceed $\mu$ and $2-\mu$ , respectively, giving $\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}>1$ . Hence $\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}=1$ if and only if $\gamma=0$ , which is equivalent to $\mathbf{a}=\mu\mathbf{k}$ and therefore to collinearity of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{k}$ . ∎

The same representation also verifies that $\mathbf{A}$ is non-normal when $\gamma>0$ , since

[Tableau] $$\mathbf{T}^{\top}\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{T}\mathbf{T}^{\top}=\begin{pmatrix}-\gamma^{2}&\mu\gamma\\ \mu\gamma&\gamma^{2}\end{pmatrix}\neq\mathbf{0}\quad\text{when }\gamma>0.$$ (29)

For the recurrent state energy introduced in Section 3.1 , the maximum amplification factor is

[Tableau] $$\sup_{\mathbf{S}\neq\mathbf{0}}\frac{E(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{S})}{E(\mathbf{S})}=\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}^{2}.$$ (30)

The upper bound follows by applying the operator norm bound to each column of $\mathbf{S}$ . Equality is attained when one column lies along a leading right singular direction of $\mathbf{A}$ and all other columns are zero. Thus, nonzero shear permits erasure alone to increase the energy of the recurrent state, without any new write.


#### A.2 Unbounded Growth Without Further Writes

A periodic sequence of two alternating transitions realizes the alignment described in Section 3.1 . We construct it on two key coordinates and one recurrent state column, then embed it in higher dimensions. For this example, choose $\mathbf{D}_{t}=d\mathbf{I}$ with $d=0.99$ at every token.

Choose the following pairs of keys and erase gates,

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathbf{k}_{a}$ $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\begin{pmatrix}2\\ 1\end{pmatrix},$ $\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{a}$ $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{5}\begin{pmatrix}1\\ 4\end{pmatrix},$ (31) $\displaystyle\mathbf{k}_{b}$ $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\ 2\end{pmatrix},$ $\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{b}$ $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{5}\begin{pmatrix}4\\ 1\end{pmatrix}.$

Both keys have unit norm, and all erase gate entries lie in $(0,1)$ . The corresponding erase operators are

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{a}$ $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{25}\begin{pmatrix}21&-8\\ -2&21\end{pmatrix},$ (32) $\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{b}$ $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{25}\begin{pmatrix}21&-2\\ -8&21\end{pmatrix}=\mathbf{A}_{a}^{\top}.$

Hence the full transitions $\mathbf{M}_{a}=d\mathbf{A}_{a}$ and $\mathbf{M}_{b}=d\mathbf{A}_{b}$ satisfy $\mathbf{M}_{b}=\mathbf{M}_{a}^{\top}$ . For $\mathbf{A}_{a}$ , the geometric coefficients are $\mu=8/25$ and $\gamma=6/25$ . Theorem 3.1 therefore gives

[Tableau] $$\lambda=d^{2}\frac{19+6\sqrt{2}}{25}\approx 1.077533>1.$$ (33)

At the first token, choose the write

[Tableau] $$\mathbf{S}_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{100+10\sqrt{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}7\\ -(1+5\sqrt{2})\end{pmatrix},\qquad\|\mathbf{S}_{1}\|_{2}=1.$$ (34)

This write is realized by $\mathbf{k}_{1}=\mathbf{S}_{1}$ , $\mathbf{b}_{1}=(1/2,1/2)^{\top}$ , $w_{1}=1/2$ , and $v_{1}=2$ . Indeed, the initial recurrent state is zero, so the first update reduces to $\mathbf{k}_{1}(w_{1}v_{1})=\mathbf{S}_{1}$ . For every subsequent token $t\geq 2$ , set $w_{t}=1/2$ and $v_{t}=0$ , so that no further content is written while the write gate remains strictly between zero and one. Alternate the pairs in Eq. ( 31 ) so that

[Tableau] $$\mathbf{M}_{2n+2}=\mathbf{M}_{a},\qquad\mathbf{M}_{2n+3}=\mathbf{M}_{b},\qquad n\geq 0.$$ (35)

Direct multiplication gives

[Tableau] $$\mathbf{M}_{b}\mathbf{M}_{a}=\frac{d^{2}}{125}\begin{pmatrix}89&-42\\ -42&101\end{pmatrix},$$ (36)

and the initial column in Eq. ( 34 ) satisfies $\mathbf{M}_{b}\mathbf{M}_{a}\mathbf{S}_{1}=\lambda\mathbf{S}_{1}$ . It follows by induction that

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{2n+1}$ $\displaystyle=\lambda^{n}\mathbf{S}_{1},$ (37) $\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{2n+2}$ $\displaystyle=\lambda^{n}\mathbf{M}_{a}\mathbf{S}_{1},\qquad n\geq 0.$

Moreover, the transpose pairing implies

[Tableau] $$\|\mathbf{M}_{a}\mathbf{S}_{1}\|_{2}^{2}=\mathbf{S}_{1}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{b}\mathbf{M}_{a}\mathbf{S}_{1}=\lambda.$$ (38)

Since $\lambda=\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{M}_{a})^{2}$ , this equality shows that $\mathbf{S}_{1}$ lies along a leading right singular direction of $\mathbf{M}_{a}$ . The first transition maps it to the corresponding left singular direction, and the second maps it back. Each pair both amplifies the recurrent state and restores the direction required for the next pair. In particular, for every $t\geq 1$ ,

[Tableau] $$\|\mathbf{S}_{t}\|_{2}=\lambda^{(t-1)/2},\qquad E(\mathbf{S}_{t})=\lambda^{t-1}\longrightarrow\infty.$$ (39)

This growth occurs entirely after writing ceases, despite the strict decay applied at every step.

For a recurrent state with more coordinates or columns, embed every key, including $\mathbf{k}_{1}$ , in the same two key coordinates and set all remaining key entries to zero. Set the remaining erase gate entries and all write gate entries to $1/2$ , retain $\mathbf{D}_{t}=d\mathbf{I}$ , and place the initial value $2$ in one value coordinate, with all other values zero. All subsequent value vectors are zero. The resulting recurrent state remains supported on the chosen two coordinates and one column, so

[Tableau] $$\|\mathbf{S}_{t}\|_{F}=\lambda^{(t-1)/2}\longrightarrow\infty.$$ (40)

All keys remain normalized and all erase, write, and decay gate entries lie in $(0,1)$ , so the embedded sequence satisfies the corollary. ∎


### Appendix B Details of Empirical Characterization


#### B.1 Training Protocol and Failure Accounting

The Pure Linear and Hybrid architectures, tokenizer, pretraining data, and shared optimization settings follow Section 5.1 . Pure Linear uses GDN-2 in all 20 layers, while Hybrid repeats three GDN-2 layers followed by one full attention layer. Their parameter counts are approximately 1.595B and 1.554B, respectively. Pretraining starts from random initialization and uses the ProLong corpus ( Gao et al., 2025 ) with next token supervision over all nonpadding tokens. The maximum packed sequence length is 65,536 tokens. The recurrent state propagates across documents within each pretraining pack and is reset at the start of the next pack.

Post-training uses the official training split of nvidia/ChatQA2-Long-SFT-data ( Xu et al., 2025 ) , with loss applied only to answer and EOS targets. The standalone post-training condition starts from random initialization, whereas post-training after pretraining starts from the final weights of the corresponding pretraining run. The latter resets the optimizer, learning rate schedule, training random number state, and step counter. We retain 143,367 complete QA examples that fit within 65,536 tokens, yielding approximately 2.170B nonpadding input tokens and 79.987M supervised targets. Examples exceeding the length limit are excluded without truncation. Each QA example is an independent segment within a pack, with positions, recurrent state, and convolution state reset at its boundary; full attention in Hybrid is also restricted to that segment. Thus, the 64K pack limit does not imply uninterrupted recurrent state propagation across separate QA examples. Both post-training conditions use one data epoch, a global batch of 16 packs, and 2,331 optimizer updates. The learning rate warms up to $3\times 10^{-4}$ over 100 updates, remains constant, and decays linearly to $3\times 10^{-5}$ over the final 233 updates. Other optimizer settings follow Section 5.1 .

The four seed configurations form a $2\times 2$ design crossing data order seeds $\{2026,2027\}$ with initialization and training seeds $\{42,43\}$ . Seeds 1–4 correspond to the pairs $(2026,42)$ , $(2026,43)$ , $(2027,42)$ , and $(2027,43)$ , respectively. The packing seed is fixed at 3407, so changing the data seed changes pack order rather than the underlying packing. The same seed configuration identifies corresponding stages within each architecture. A failed pretraining run does not launch its dependent post-training stage, leaving three evaluated stages for Pure Linear and two for Hybrid in the final column of Table 1 .

We count failures according to the loss NaN criterion in Section 3.2 . Table 1 uses one designated trajectory per seed configuration and training stage. #### C.1 Write Norm and Additive Accumulation

The residual in Eq. ( 13 ) gives, for each coordinate,

[Tableau] $$(\mathbf{u}_{t}+\mathbf{r}_{t})_{i}=\left[\sqrt{b_{t,i}}+\sqrt{1-d_{t,i}^{2}}(1-\sqrt{b_{t,i}})\right]k_{t,i}.$$ (41)

The coefficient in brackets lies in $[0,1]$ , because both $\sqrt{b_{t,i}}$ and $\sqrt{1-d_{t,i}^{2}}$ lie in that interval. Hence $\|\mathbf{u}_{t}+\mathbf{r}_{t}\|_{2}\leq\|\mathbf{k}_{t}\|_{2}\leq 1$ , and the Frobenius norm of an outer product yields

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{u}_{t}+\mathbf{r}_{t})\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\|_{F}$ $\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{u}_{t}+\mathbf{r}_{t}\|_{2}\|\mathbf{z}_{t}\|_{2}$ (42) $\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{k}_{t}\|_{2}\|\mathbf{z}_{t}\|_{2}=\|\mathbf{k}_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\|_{F}.$

Expanding Eq. ( 14 ), applying the triangle inequality, and using $\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{T:t+1}\|_{2}\leq 1$ gives Eq. ( 16 ). This argument accounts for arbitrary alignment between successive writes.


#### C.2 Proof of the Recurrent State Norm Bound

Fix a token and omit its index. Introduce the auxiliary quantities

[Tableau] $$\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{u},\qquad\mathbf{C}=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{D}^{2})^{1/2},\qquad\nu=\|\mathbf{u}\|_{2}^{2}.$$ (43)

Equation ( 13 ) gives $\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{C}\mathbf{f}$ . Since $\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}$ is symmetric and $\mathbf{I}-(\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{sym}})^{2}=(2-\nu)\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{u})^{\top}$ ,

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^{\top}$ $\displaystyle=\mathbf{I}-(\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{sym}})^{2}+\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{D}^{2})\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}$ (44) $\displaystyle=(2-\nu)\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{u})^{\top}+\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathbf{C}^{2}\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}=\mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^{\top},$

where the auxiliary matrix is $\mathbf{L}=[\sqrt{2-\nu}\,\mathbf{u}\;\;\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathbf{C}]$ . The write vector admits a factorization through the same matrix,

[Tableau] $$\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{L}\boldsymbol{\xi},\qquad\boldsymbol{\xi}=\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\frac{1+(\mathbf{u})^{\top}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{f}}{\sqrt{2-\nu}}\\[4.0pt] \mathbf{f}\end{bmatrix}.$$ (45)

Indeed, the first block contributes $\mathbf{u}[1+(\mathbf{u})^{\top}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{f}]$ , while the second contributes $\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{C}\mathbf{f}-\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{u})^{\top}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{f}$ . Their sum is $\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{r}$ .

To bound $\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{2}$ , set $\eta=(\mathbf{u})^{\top}\mathbf{f}$ and $\tau=\|\mathbf{f}\|_{2}^{2}$ . The coordinate products $u_{i}f_{i}=\sqrt{b_{i}}(1-\sqrt{b_{i}})k_{i}^{2}$ are nonnegative. Since the diagonal entries of $\mathbf{C}$ lie in $[0,1]$ ,

[Tableau] $$\begin{gathered}0\leq(\mathbf{u})^{\top}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{f}\leq\eta,\qquad\eta^{2}\leq\nu\tau,\\ \nu+2\eta+\tau=\|\mathbf{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 1.\end{gathered}$$ (46)

The denominator in Eq. ( 45 ) is positive because $\nu\leq 1$ . Moreover,

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{2}-\tau\right)(2-\nu)-(1+\eta)^{2}$ (47) $\displaystyle=2(1-\nu-2\eta-\tau)+\frac{\nu}{2}+2\eta$ $\displaystyle+(\nu\tau-\eta^{2})\geq 0.$

It follows that

[Tableau] $$\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\tau+\frac{(1+\eta)^{2}}{2-\nu}\leq\frac{3}{2}.$$ (48)

Combining Eqs. ( 44 )–( 48 ) gives

[Tableau] $$(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{r})(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{r})^{\top}=\mathbf{L}\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}\mathbf{L}^{\top}\preceq\frac{3}{2}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^{\top}).$$ (49)

Thus, the block matrix $[\mathbf{M}\;\;\sqrt{2/3}(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{r})]$ has operator norm at most one. Restoring the time index, apply this contraction to the stacked recurrent state and gated value to obtain

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{t}\|_{F}^{2}$ $\displaystyle=\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{M}_{t}&\sqrt{2/3}(\mathbf{u}_{t}+\mathbf{r}_{t})\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{S}_{t-1}\\ \sqrt{3/2}\,\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{F}^{2}$ (50) $\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{S}_{t-1}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\|\mathbf{z}_{t}\|_{2}^{2}.$

Since $\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{w}_{t}\odot\mathbf{v}_{t}$ and the write gate has entries in $(0,1)$ , $\|\mathbf{z}_{t}\|_{2}\leq\|\mathbf{v}_{t}\|_{2}\leq V$ . Summing the preceding inequality over tokens and using $\mathbf{S}_{0}=\mathbf{0}$ gives

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{T}\|_{F}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\mathbf{z}_{t}\|_{2}^{2}$ (51) $\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\mathbf{v}_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}TV^{2}.$

Taking square roots proves Eq. ( 17 ). ∎


#### C.3 Role of Decay Regulation

Removing $\sqrt{1-\mathbf{d}_{t}^{2}}$ from the residual leaves the transition unchanged and makes the total write equal to $\mathbf{k}_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}$ . Non-expansive transitions are therefore preserved, but the resulting recurrence need not admit an $O(\sqrt{T})$ norm bound with a constant independent of the gate values and sequence length. To see this, consider a scalar invariant direction with constant $k=1$ , $b=\varepsilon^{2}$ , $d=1-\varepsilon^{2}$ , and $z=1$ , starting from $S_{0}=0$ . These choices can be embedded along one coordinate of any key dimension; for example, the gated value $z=1$ is realized by $w=1/2$ and $v=2$ . For $0<\varepsilon<1$ , the unscaled residual gives

[Tableau] $$\begin{gathered}S_{t}=mS_{t-1}+1,\qquad m=(1-\varepsilon^{2})^{2}<1,\\ S_{T}=\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}m^{j}.\end{gathered}$$ (52)

For any $T\geq 4$ , choose $\varepsilon=1/T$ . Bernoulli’s inequality gives $m^{T-1}=(1-T^{-2})^{2(T-1)}\geq 1-2(T-1)/T^{2}\geq 1/2$ , and hence $S_{T}\geq T/2$ . Thus, the values remain bounded by $V=2$ , while $\|\mathbf{S}_{T}\|_{F}\geq T/2$ . For any constant $C$ independent of the gate values and sequence length, this family violates $\|\mathbf{S}_{T}\|_{F}\leq CV\sqrt{T}$ for sufficiently large $T$ . The growth comes from repeated writes under weak decay, rather than amplification through the transition products analyzed in Section 3.1 . This separates the control of write accumulation provided by decay regulation from the non-expansive propagation provided by symmetric erasure.


#### C.4 A Bound under Uniform Strict Decay

If, in addition, $\|\mathbf{d}_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq\rho<1$ and $\|\mathbf{v}_{t}\|_{2}\leq V$ for all tokens, then $\|\mathbf{z}_{t}\|_{2}\leq V$ . Proposition 4.1 and Eq. ( 42 ) therefore imply

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{T}\|_{F}$ $\displaystyle\leq\rho^{T}\|\mathbf{S}_{0}\|_{F}+V\sum_{t=1}^{T}\rho^{T-t}$ (53) $\displaystyle=\rho^{T}\|\mathbf{S}_{0}\|_{F}+V\frac{1-\rho^{T}}{1-\rho}\leq\|\mathbf{S}_{0}\|_{F}+\frac{V}{1-\rho}.$

This bound is independent of sequence length under the additional uniform decay condition. The $O(\sqrt{T})$ norm bound in Proposition 4.2 does not require a uniform gap between the decay factors and one.
