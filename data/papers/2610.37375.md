## Agent-Conditioned Routing: Diverse Reasoning Paths Within One Frozen Model

Generating several solutions can expose alternative methods, but additional trajectories often paraphrase one dominant approach. We investigate whether the conditional capacity of a frozen sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) model can provide such diversity from one problem prompt, without user-assigned roles, prescribed methods, fine-tuning, or model copies. Agent-Conditioned Routing ( ACR ) assigns $m$ parallel trajectories persistent, untrained shared/private expert-eligibility masks while preserving the learned, input-dependent token router. On a screened 126-question multi-mode mathematics and physics benchmark, with model judges reading the reported derivations in full, we count the number of problems yielding at least two distinct primary methods among soft-correct solutions in four trajectories. ACR reaches approximately twice as many such problems as the strongest temperature setting under GLM 5.3 (37.3 versus 18.7), and 1.67 times as many under GPT-5.6 Sol (45.00 versus 27.00), at matched output budgets. A blinded four-expert audit on a stratified sample of 30 problems also finds more multi-method solutions with ACR on both backbones, with lower correctness. Matched-eligibility ablations show modest multi-method count advantages for ACR , while token-resampled masks attain higher correctness. An informed sequential prompt elicits more diversity still, using previous solutions and explicit requests for alternative methods. ACR adds about 160 KiB of mask state. In a three-problem microbenchmark capped at 1,024 tokens, its latency and peak VRAM usage remain within 3% of batched temperature sampling.


### 1 Introduction

Inference-time scaling treats additional generations as additional chances of success, but samples that repeat one argument inherit the same blind spots. Answer-level techniques such as self-consistency still benefit from repeated samples on verifiable tasks ( Wang et al., 2023 ) , yet agreement does not reveal whether derivations differ meaningfully. For mathematical and scientific reasoning, we therefore study correct method diversity : on how many problems do four trajectories yield at least two distinct primary solution methods with correct conclusions under a fixed generation budget?

One can seek diversity through decoding, method prompts, trained adapters, or coordinated models. We target autonomous method diversification : the user supplies a problem without having to identify alternative solution techniques in advance. We constrain expert eligibility in a frozen sparse MoE while retaining its learned token router. MoEs expose alternative feed-forward paths through conditional computation ( Shazeer et al., 2017 ; Lepikhin et al., 2021 ; Fedus et al., 2022 ; Du et al., 2022 ) . The question is whether these constraints change the executed derivation while preserving correctness, not merely whether they change the text.

Agent-Conditioned Routing ( ACR ) executes $m{=}4$ route-conditioned trajectories on one frozen Qwen3.6-35B-A3B instance in one batched request. Here agent denotes a route-conditioned trajectory. All trajectories receive the same prompt and use deterministic decoding, with no role prompts, role generation, updates, communication, or model copies. A seeded construction assigns each a persistent shared/private eligibility mask; the frozen learned router still selects its top- $k$ experts within that eligible set.

Our contributions are twofold:

- 1. Agent-Conditioned Routing. We introduce a training-free inference mechanism that uses expert eligibility as a control for reasoning diversity. Shared/private masks assign fixed routing constraints during each trajectory’s prefix, enabling diversification from one problem prompt without user-assigned roles, prescribed methods, or parameter updates.

- 2. A multi-mode reasoning benchmark for diversity. We construct a 126-question benchmark spanning 12 mathematics and physics categories and supporting multiple solution modes. The evaluation measures how often four trajectories yield at least two distinct primary methods among soft-correct solutions. Full-benchmark evaluations by two model judges are complemented by a blinded four-expert audit on a stratified 30-problem subset across three seeds. Correctness and mean UCM@4 accompany threshold counts.


### 2 Related work

ACR connects inference-time reasoning diversity with sparse-MoE conditional computation, seeking distinct primary solution methods across independent trajectories within one frozen model without specifying methods in the prompt.


#### 2.1 Inference-time diversity from one model

Chain-of-thought prompting elicits intermediate reasoning, and self-consistency samples reasoning chains before aggregating their answers ( Wei et al., 2022 ; Wang et al., 2023 ) . Tree search, self-refinement, and test-time compute scaling use additional inference to improve reasoning ( Yao et al., 2023 ; Madaan et al., 2023 ; Snell et al., 2025 ) . These approaches motivate generating and evaluating alternatives, but answer-level success does not establish that the alternatives execute different methods.

Other approaches target variation more directly. Diverse beam search separates decoded outputs ( Vijayakumar et al., 2018 ) , while diverse prompting and Flow of Reasoning target reasoning-path diversity ( Lee et al., 2024 ; Yu et al., 2025 ) . STaR develops reasoning ability through training on generated rationales ( Zelikman et al., 2022 ) . ACR introduces a different control: it holds the prompt, weights, and decoding rule fixed while varying expert eligibility across trajectories. We evaluate primary executed methods among correct trajectories rather than lexical variation or answer votes.


#### 2.2 Parallel-agent reasoning

Parallel-agent systems obtain complementary outputs through multiple reasoning processes and mechanisms for combining them. Mixture-of-Agents and Sparse Mixture-of-Agents combine outputs across model agents ( Wang et al., 2025 ; Li et al., 2024 ) ; multiagent debate exchanges critiques between models ( Du et al., 2024 ) . Group Think and Hogwild! Inference share partial state among concurrent workers ( Hsu et al., 2025 ; Rodionov et al., 2025 ) .

ACR instead places trajectory identity in routing constraints: trajectories share one frozen backbone but generate independently, so differentiation does not require separate weights, role prompts, or inter-agent communication.


#### 2.3 Expert routing as an inference-time control

MoE research offers several ways to organize and select conditional computation, including BASE layers, expert-choice routing, ST-MoE, Mixtral, and DeepSeekMoE ( Lewis et al., 2021 ; Zhou et al., 2022 ; Zoph et al., 2022 ; Jiang et al., 2024 ; Dai et al., 2024 ) . Related interventions contrast selected and unselected experts in SCMoE ( Shi et al., 2024 ) , learn router parameters in HyperRouter ( Do et al., 2023 ) , or learn adapter experts and a router in Self-MoE ( Kang et al., 2025 ) . Semantic routing probes further motivate expert selection as an intervention point ( Olson et al., 2025 ) . Recent work finds a domain-invariant “Standing Committee” of heavily reused experts, questioning clean domain specialization and motivating shared capacity ( Wang et al., 2026 ) ; our random shared set does not identify that committee.

The closest inference-time comparisons are RoE and Expert-Sample. RoE resamples noisy expert routes at each token and aggregates the resulting proposals into a prediction ( Zibakhsh et al., 2025 ) . Expert-Sample retains the router’s certain selections and Gumbel-resamples its uncertain tail, seeking generation diversity while preserving answer-level performance ( Chen et al., 2026 ) . Both provide training-free ways to vary the computation of a frozen MoE, and we evaluate them as routing-noise controls.

ACR instead assigns persistent shared/private eligibility masks, within which the pretrained router selects experts deterministically. We evaluate Expert-Sample in its published routing configuration and RoE as a proposal-averaging control. Section 5.4 further separates temporal persistence from mask construction through matched-cardinality eligibility controls.


### 3 Method


#### 3.1 Persistent shared/private eligibility

Let the frozen MoE have a routed-expert universe $\mathcal{E}$ , $|\mathcal{E}|=E$ , and let its learned router emit logits $r_{\ell}(h_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{E}$ at layer $\ell$ and token $t$ . We execute $m$ route-conditioned trajectories ( $m{=}4$ in the main benchmark; the construction requires $P+\lfloor(E-P)/m\rfloor\geq k$ ). At each MoE layer, a seeded permutation selects a shared core $S_{\ell}$ of size $P$ ; the remaining experts are split round-robin into disjoint private sets $V_{\ell,1},\dots,V_{\ell,m}$ . This construction constrains distributed computation rather than assuming that individual experts correspond to distinct semantic skills. Agent $a$ is eligible for

[Tableau] $$\mathcal{E}_{\ell,a}=S_{\ell}\cup V_{\ell,a},\qquad|\mathcal{E}_{\ell,a}|\approx P+\frac{E-P}{m},$$ (1)

and the routed logits become

[Tableau] $$\widetilde{r}_{\ell,t,e}^{(a)}=\begin{cases}r_{\ell,t,e},&e\in\mathcal{E}_{\ell,a},\\ -\infty,&e\notin\mathcal{E}_{\ell,a}.\end{cases}$$ (2)

The pretrained router still chooses its usual top- $k$ experts, now within the eligible set of Equation ( 2 ). Nothing is trained, and the mask persists throughout the routed prefix. At $P=E$ , all trajectories reduce to deterministic copies of the base model; decreasing $P$ increases private capacity but risks excluding useful experts. For Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, $E=256$ , $k=8$ , and there are 40 MoE layers ( Qwen Team, 2026 ) . At the main benchmark setting $P=160$ , each trajectory can access 184 of 256 experts per layer; at $P=192$ , it can access 208.

The four trajectories share weights and execute along the batch dimension, while retaining separate token histories and KV caches. A batched generation request therefore contains multiple autoregressive forward steps over one model instance.

The hybrid configuration limits constrained routing to a 2,000-token prefix, after which unfinished trajectories continue with unrestricted expert routing in the same frozen model. Appendix G analyzes cap attainment and recorded handoffs in the saved runs.

### 4 Experiments


#### 4.1 Tasks, model, and conditions


##### Task and model.

The main experiments use a frozen BF16 Qwen3.6-35B-A3B checkpoint, a 35B-parameter sparse MoE with roughly 3B active parameters per token ( Qwen Team, 2026 ) . We use a synthetic 11-problem development set of mathematics and physics derivations with verifiable answers and plausibly multiple methods for configuration selection. We report these development probes separately from the main evaluation, which uses a screened benchmark drawn from a hand-authored 200-problem, 12-category candidate pool spanning integrals, series, products, combinatorial identities, probability, functional equations, number theory, inequalities, mechanics, E&M, thermodynamics, and quantum physics. Each problem was screened using six temperature samples and retained only if the outputs showed multiple solution paths and at least one correct solution; 126 problems passed. The result is a diagnostic set conditioned on evidence of multiple solution paths in temperature-generated outputs. Our conclusions concern this selected population; broader task coverage is discussed in Section 5.5 .


##### Comparison methods and generation budget.

All methods in the main benchmark use the same output budget: four generations per problem and at most 4,096 new tokens each. This matches trajectory count and output caps, not total compute or input-token usage. ACR uses deterministic decoding and one persistent mask per trajectory, sweeping $P\in\{96,160,192\}$ at benchmark scale, with a repetition penalty of 1.05 and thinking disabled. The baseline draws four i.i.d. temperature samples from the unpatched model ( $T\in\{0.5,0.9,1.1\}$ at benchmark scale and on the development sweep). The Expert-Sample baseline applies routing noise ( Chen et al., 2026 ) at benchmark scale: at every token and MoE layer, the top $k_{\mathrm{keep}}=5$ experts by gating logit are retained, the remaining three of the $k=8$ selections are Gumbel-sampled from candidate ranks $6$ – $32$ at $\tau{=}1.0$ , and weights are renormalized from the original global softmax, following the published defaults for this backbone class. The RoE baseline applies routing noise ( Zibakhsh et al., 2025 ) at benchmark scale: at every generated token the selection becomes $\operatorname{TopK}(R+\tau G,k)$ with $G$ i.i.d. standard Gumbel on the middle MoE layers (the first and last two layers stay clean), and $N{=}8$ perturbed proposals per token run in one batched forward pass whose next-token distributions are probability-averaged into a single greedy prediction, with only the clean path’s KV cache retained. The proposal count and the fixed $\tau{=}0.5$ are compute-driven deviations from the published per-model-tuned settings ( $N{=}32/64$ ); decoding is greedy and otherwise identical to ACR . We also compare against informed sequential diversity prompting, where calls 2–4 repeat the problem with excerpts of all prior solutions and an instruction to use a fundamentally different method. It uses the same four-call budget, but later calls receive inter-attempt information and an explicit method hint, and the sequential dependency precludes batching (Table 1 , sequential arm). All other methods share one neutral problem prompt with no method hints. The held-out saturation probe draws up to 50 temperature samples to test whether a solution path merely has low sampling probability.


#### 4.2 Judging protocol


##### Correctness.

A trajectory is soft-correct when it reaches the mathematically correct answer in its reasoning, including when that answer is omitted from the final box or affected by LaTeX formatting errors. Judges read the complete raw prediction rather than relying on boxed-answer extraction. We use “correct methods” for the mathematical routes in these answer-correct trajectories; intermediate-step validity is a separate criterion from final-answer correctness.


##### Method diversity.

For problem $x$ and trajectory $i$ , let $\mu_{i}(x)$ denote its executed mathematical route and $C_{i}(x)$ indicate soft-correctness. Each trajectory contributes at most one method. We define

[Tableau] $$\mathrm{UCM}@m(x)=\left|\left\{\mu_{i}(x)\;:\;i\in\{1,\ldots,m\},\ C_{i}(x)=1\right\}\right|.$$ (3)

Judges count substantive differences in mathematical steps as method diversity, including differences within the same broad method family. Rephrasing and reordering equivalent steps do not qualify. Thus the comparison operates at the level of executed routes rather than only named techniques. Multiple derivations within a single trajectory receive at most one unit of credit; repeated routes across trajectories count once. With four trajectories per problem, $0\leq\mathrm{UCM}@4(x)\leq 4$ .


##### Multi-method problem counts.

We report the number of benchmark problems with at least two distinct primary methods among soft-correct trajectories:

[Tableau] $$N_{\geq 2}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{1}\!\left[\mathrm{UCM}@4(x)\geq 2\right].$$ (4)

The normalized rate $N_{\geq 2}/|\mathcal{D}|$ includes all 126 problems, including those with no correct solution. Counts are computed within runs and averaged across seeds, not unioned across runs. Mean UCM@4 and $N_{\geq 3}$ provide complementary measures of method count; per-trajectory soft-correctness is reported alongside diversity. “Two solutions” means two distinct primary methods, not repeated answers.

All model-judged arms use the same judging instructions (Appendix A ). Judges are instructed to read raw JSON predictions in full and assess them without consulting previous results, artifacts, judgments, or manuals. The prompt requests pool-wise UCM@4 and counts of problems with at least two and three distinct solutions. GLM 5.3 with max thinking and GPT-5.6 Sol with high thinking provide separate model-based evaluations. Main-table GLM results average three seeds; Sol results use the per-arm seed counts in Table 2 . GLM and Sol are evaluators of generated solutions, not additional generation backbones. Judge-specific analyses are reported separately; the human-audit protocol and results appear in Section 5.2 .


#### 4.3 Resource accounting

ACR stores one backbone and preserves the top- $k$ active-expert count. Its FP32 masks require 160 KiB (40 layers, four agents, 256 experts); a packed representation would require about 5 KiB. No adapters or extra weights are trained. Expert-Sample likewise preserves the activation count; RoE instead processes multiple proposals and temporarily retains their KV rows before keeping only the clean path.

Table 1 reports a short-generation microbenchmark. ACR remains close to batched temperature sampling in latency and peak memory, while RoE and sequential prompting take longer. The 1,024-token cap never reaches the 2,000-token handoff, so these measurements do not characterize the full 4,096-token hybrid configuration. Backbone weights dominate memory throughout.

### 5 Results

We report the screened benchmark, blinded human audit, qualitative example, and ablations. Development and held-out probes appear in Appendix D .


#### 5.1 Benchmark scale: the screened 126

The benchmark contains 126 screened problems. The method-diversity evaluation covers ACR at its main setting $P{=}160$ , three temperature settings, both routing-noise controls, and the informed sequential prompting control; the remaining pool sizes are ablated in Section 5.4 .


##### Method diversity under GLM 5.3.

At the main setting, ACR yields at least two correct primary methods on 37.3 of 126 problems, versus 18.7 for the strongest temperature setting on this metric ( $T=0.9$ ): approximately a twofold increase, averaged over three seeds (Table 3 ). Correctness is 95.77% versus 95.63%. The secondary mean UCM@4 is 1.323 versus 1.159. The approximately twofold increase refers to the prevalence of problems with alternative methods. The most diverse examples include order statistics, spin measurement, and the alternating Basel sum. Figure 2 shows the correctness–diversity comparison across pool sizes.

##### Routing-noise controls.

Expert-Sample and RoE produce fewer multi-method problems despite high correctness (Table 3 ). On spin measurement, all eight baseline trajectories use operator-eigenvector derivations; rotation-operator checks add no primary-method credit. This comparison changes both eligibility and structure, so it does not isolate persistence.


##### Informed sequential prompting.

Showing each later call prior solutions and requesting a different method yields the highest GLM diversity (Table 3 ) and finds routes absent from every blind arm. This protocol can also be automated, but its later calls depend on earlier solutions and explicit requests for alternatives. ACR generates its trajectories independently from one neutral prompt. Among the tested blind, batched, prompt-matched arms, it yields the most problems with at least two correct methods, requiring no user-assigned roles or prescribed techniques.

#### 5.4 Ablation studies


##### Persistence and mask structure.

We fix eligibility at 184 experts per trajectory and layer, matching $P=160$ , and retain the same handoff and generation settings. Token-resampled ACR redraws the shared/private assignment at each decoding step during the routed prefix, isolating persistence. Independent-persistent masks sample 184 experts per trajectory and layer without a common core or disjoint private sets. This tests the shared/private construction as a whole, including its effect on overlap between trajectories.

#### 5.5 Limitations and future work

Masks constrain computation without assigning semantic specializations and may yield the same method ( Wang et al., 2026 ) . Matched-eligibility contrasts remain descriptive; paired uncertainty and expert assessment of intermediate-step validity are outside this evaluation. UCM@4 and $N_{\geq 3}$ complement the two-method threshold.

The screened benchmark targets problems with multiple solution methods. The human audit covers 30 problems; agreement between reviewers is not quantified. Broader task and model coverage, layer-specific masks, resource scaling, and downstream selection or aggregation remain future work.


### 6 Conclusion

Expert eligibility provides an inference-time control for reasoning diversity within one frozen MoE, without user-assigned roles or prescribed methods. At matched output budgets, ACR yields at least two correct primary methods on approximately twice as many problems as the strongest temperature setting under GLM and 1.67 times as many under GPT-5.6 Sol. A blinded four-expert audit supports the direction of these gains on both backbones, with lower correctness for ACR. The matched-eligibility controls retain much of the diversification effect, with modest advantages for the full construction. Informed sequential prompting achieves greater diversity using previous attempts; ACR explores alternatives independently in one batched request.

##### Element-by-element counting: ACR 1–2; temperature 1–4.

For each $x\in A\cup B\cup C$ , count its contribution to the right-hand side of Equation ( 5 ). If it belongs to exactly one set, its contribution is $1$ . If it belongs to exactly two sets, its contribution is $2-1=1$ . If it belongs to all three, its contribution is $3-3+1=1$ . Thus each element of the union contributes exactly once. Summing these contributions proves the formula. Writing the same membership cases with indicator functions does not create a different route.


##### Disjoint-region decomposition: ACR 3.

Let $r_{A},r_{B},r_{C}$ count elements in only $A$ , only $B$ , and only $C$ ; let $r_{AB},r_{AC},r_{BC}$ count elements in exactly the indicated pair; and let $r_{ABC}=|A\cap B\cap C|$ . These seven regions partition the union. Expanding the individual and intersection cardinalities gives

[Tableau] $\displaystyle S_{1}$ $\displaystyle=|A|+|B|+|C|$ $\displaystyle=r_{A}+r_{B}+r_{C}+2(r_{AB}+r_{AC}+r_{BC})+3r_{ABC},$ $\displaystyle S_{2}$ $\displaystyle=|A\cap B|+|A\cap C|+|B\cap C|$ $\displaystyle=r_{AB}+r_{AC}+r_{BC}+3r_{ABC}.$

Consequently $S_{1}-S_{2}+r_{ABC}$ is the sum of the seven disjoint region sizes, which equals $|A\cup B\cup C|$ . This is the final region calculation in trajectory 3 after it revisits the triple-intersection coefficient.


##### Reduction to the two-set identity: ACR 4.

First establish $|X\cup Y|=|X|+|Y|-|X\cap Y|$ by checking the four membership possibilities for an element. Apply this identity to $A\cup B$ and $C$ :

[Tableau] $$|A\cup B\cup C|=|A\cup B|+|C|-|(A\cup B)\cap C|.$$

Distributivity gives $(A\cup B)\cap C=(A\cap C)\cup(B\cap C)$ . A second application of the two-set identity yields

[Tableau] $$|(A\cup B)\cap C|=|A\cap C|+|B\cap C|-|A\cap B\cap C|.$$

Substituting this and $|A\cup B|=|A|+|B|-|A\cap B|$ into the first line proves Equation ( 5 ).

### Appendix H Matched switch-versus-no-switch evaluation

We select 20 problems from the screened benchmark using subset seed 20260926: one random problem from each of the 12 categories, followed by eight problems sampled uniformly from the remainder. Selection uses only problem IDs, without consulting outcomes. Both conditions use $P=160$ , mask seed 0, four trajectories, greedy decoding, repetition penalty 1.05, thinking disabled, and a 4,096-token output cap.

Each pair shares the same masked prefix of up to 2,000 tokens. Trajectories that reach EOS in this prefix are retained unchanged in both conditions. Unfinished trajectories receive up to 2,096 further tokens, with either unrestricted routing or the original eligibility mask. Both conditions rebuild the prefix cache under their respective routing settings and use the same active trajectory batch; execution order alternates by problem. Thus the comparison controls restart mechanics while varying routing eligibility during continuation.

Of the 80 trajectories, 52 reach EOS before or at the prefix boundary, while 28 remain unfinished on 10 of the 20 problems. Among these 28 paired continuations, 22 reach EOS with the switch versus 14 without it. The repetition heuristic flags three full trajectories in the switch condition and four in the no-switch condition. These diagnostics distinguish termination and repetition from mathematical correctness: a trajectory may reach a correct answer before exhausting its token budget without emitting EOS. The observed gain is therefore eight additional EOS completions and one additional soft-correct trajectory, with equal aggregate diversity metrics. This subset supports a completion benefit; it does not establish that unrestricted routing is universally necessary for correctness.
