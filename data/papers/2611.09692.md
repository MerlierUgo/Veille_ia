## PoseRieFlow: Riemannian Flow Matching for Whole-Body Text-to-Pose Generation

We present PoseRieFlow, a compact flow-matching framework for text-conditioned generation of whole-body 3D human poses. Prior text-to-pose methods either neglect articulated hands or model rotations in Euclidean space despite their non-Euclidean geometry. PoseRieFlow instead formulates generation directly on the product manifold of SMPL-H joint rotations, learning a text-conditioned vector field that transports samples from an isotropic $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ Gaussian prior to the data distribution along Riemannian geodesics. A part-tokenized Diffusion Transformer aggregates the 52 joints into eight body-part tokens, allocating a dedicated token and nonlinear projector to each hand, and restores per-joint resolution through part-specific decoders, shortening the sequence while improving kinematic fidelity over per-joint tokenization. With only 21.6M trainable parameters, PoseRieFlow supports expressive SMPL-H generation with detailed hand articulation at a fraction of the scale of recent LLM-based generators. On PoseScript, it attains the lowest whole-body kinematic error among all hand-producing methods, with consistent kinematic gains from the Riemannian formulation over its Euclidean counterpart at every practical inference budget, while distributional and retrieval metrics remain comparable between them.


### 1 Introduction

Generating 3D human poses from natural language is a fundamental problem spanning vision, graphics, and embodied AI. Recent progress in text-conditioned generative modeling has advanced text-to-motion synthesis through diffusion and flow-based approaches [ 37 , 16 , 36 ] . In contrast, text-to-pose generation [ 8 , 15 , 23 ] remains comparatively underexplored despite posing a distinct challenge: unlike motion generation, which benefits from strong temporal continuity priors, single-frame pose generation must model a highly multimodal distribution over articulated 3D configurations from language alone. This challenge becomes harder when extending from coarse body posture to whole-body articulation with expressive hand poses.

Current text-to-pose methods exhibit two limitations. First, most approaches model joint rotations in Euclidean parameterizations such as axis-angle or continuous 6D rotations [ 53 ] , and optimize Euclidean regression objectives. However, articulated pose lies on a product manifold of rotation groups rather than a flat vector space. As a result, linear interpolation and Euclidean velocity regression produce optimization targets that are not aligned with the geodesic structure of rotations, particularly for large joint rotations and high-dimensional whole-body configurations. Recent work on geometry-aware articulated modeling [ 18 , 30 ] suggests that respecting manifold structure can improve generative learning, but these ideas have not yet been explored in text-conditioned single-frame pose generation.

Second, existing text-to-pose systems largely ignore detailed hand articulation. Most prior methods operate on body-only skeletons with 22 joints, even when datasets are stored in richer SMPL-H or SMPL-X formats [ 27 , 34 , 32 , 8 , 9 ] . Recent LLM-based generators [ 15 , 23 ] similarly omit finger degrees of freedom. Yet hand articulation carries a substantial fraction of pose semantics in natural language descriptions, including gestures such as pointing, grasping, waving, or open-palm interactions. Modeling full SMPL-H articulation therefore requires learning coherent dependencies across 52 coupled rotations spanning body posture and dexterous hand structure. At the same time, naively scaling transformer architectures to whole-body pose increases sequence length and attention cost, motivating architectures that better exploit anatomical structure as an inductive bias.

We address these challenges with PoseRieFlow , a Riemannian flow-matching framework for text-conditioned whole-body 3D pose generation. PoseRieFlow formulates generation directly on the product manifold $\mathrm{SO}(3)^{52}$ of SMPL-H joint rotations, learning a text-conditioned vector field that transports samples from an isotropic Riemannian Gaussian prior to the data distribution along geodesic paths. Instead of regressing Euclidean rotation updates, the model predicts tangent vectors in the local Lie algebra of each joint, yielding training signals aligned with the geometry of articulated rotations. To efficiently model high-dimensional whole-body structure, we introduce a part-tokenized Diffusion Transformer that aggregates joints into semantic body-part tokens while preserving per-joint outputs through lightweight decoders. PoseRieFlow thereby bridges geometry-aware generative modeling and expressive whole-body text-to-pose synthesis.

Our contributions are as follows:

- • We introduce PoseRieFlow , a flow-matching model for text-conditioned whole-body 3D pose generation on the product manifold of 52 SMPL-H joint rotations. The model learns a text-conditioned vector field between an isotropic $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ Gaussian prior and the data distribution along Riemannian geodesics, yielding lower rotational and joint-position error than Euclidean flow matching at every practical inference budget.

- • We bring part-based tokenization [ 40 , 11 , 39 ] from discriminative pose models to a generative Diffusion Transformer. As $30$ of the $52$ SMPL-H joints are fingers, we give each hand its own token with a nonlinear projector, and recover per-joint resolution through part-specific decoders. This shortens the attention sequence from 52 to 8 tokens while improving kinematic fidelity over per-joint tokenization at matched data.

- • We demonstrate that geometry-aware whole-body pose generation can be achieved with a lightweight task-specific architecture. PoseRieFlow contains only 21.6M trainable parameters, whereas recent text-to-pose systems have billions, while additionally supporting full SMPL-H generation with articulated hands.


### 2 Related Work

Text-to-pose generation maps natural language to static 3D human poses, a building block for motion synthesis and character control. PoseScript [ 8 ] introduced the canonical benchmark, pairing 100k SMPL-H poses from AMASS [ 29 ] with human-written and automatic captions, and proposed a conditional VAE. Extensions include PoseFix [ 9 ] for text-guided editing and PoseEmbroider [ 10 ] for multimodal embeddings. More recent systems generate pose tokens autoregressively with billion-parameter language backbones, including ChatPose [ 15 ] , PoseLLaVA [ 14 ] , UniPose [ 23 ] , and CoT-Pose [ 5 ] . These methods focus on body-only generation and do not model articulated hands, limiting the pose semantics they can capture.

Human pose naturally lives on a product manifold of joint rotations. NRDF [ 18 ] learned neural Riemannian distance fields on the quaternion manifold as an articulated pose prior, extended to motion by Geometric Neural Distance Fields [ 45 ] . Flow matching [ 24 ] learns continuous vector fields between distributions without simulation and was generalized to Riemannian manifolds via geodesic interpolation [ 6 ] . On Lie groups, denoising diffusion and flow matching over $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ and $\mathrm{SE}(3)$ have been applied to rotational alignment [ 21 ] , protein backbone generation [ 3 , 42 ] , and robot motion [ 4 ] . In human motion, Motion Flow Matching [ 19 ] , MotionFLUX [ 16 ] , and HY-Motion 1.0 [ 36 ] scale Euclidean flow matching, and, closest to our work, RMG [ 30 ] applied Riemannian flow matching to text-to-motion on HumanML3D [ 17 ] . Rectified-flow variants straighten transport paths for few-step sampling [ 26 , 13 ] , an axis orthogonal to the choice of geometry, which we study via geodesic rectification in the supplementary material. We differ in three respects: we target single-frame generation, where no temporal prior constrains the output, we model full SMPL-H articulation including fingers, and we provide a controlled Euclidean vs. Riemannian comparison at matched architecture, data, and compute, isolating the effect of geometry-aware learning.

Hand articulation carries much of the semantics in natural language, such as pointing and grasping, yet most text-to-pose methods ignore finger degrees of freedom. HanDiffuser [ 31 ] targets hand-realistic image synthesis rather than pose benchmarks. BOTH2Hands [ 49 ] synthesizes two-hand motion from body dynamics and text, and MotionCraft [ 2 ] generates SMPL-X whole-body motion with plug-and-play multimodal controls. Both target motion with specialised control signals, whereas we study single-frame text-to-pose.

Grouping joints into semantic parts is a long-standing inductive bias [ 12 , 20 , 38 ] , realised in transformers either as one token per joint [ 22 , 1 , 52 ] or one token per part. IIP-Transformer [ 40 ] encodes 25 joints as 5 tokens, cutting attention cost, and TORE [ 11 ] and GTPT [ 39 ] confirm that token reduction pays off for mesh recovery and whole-body keypoints, with the largest gains on keypoint-dense regions such as the hands [ 46 , 39 ] . Part-level representations likewise underpin recent motion–language retrieval models, which form transformer patches from body-part joint groups [ 43 ] or retrieve reference motions by part-level features [ 44 ] . In generative human modelling, by contrast, parts have not been placed on the sequence axis of a single transformer: prior systems either give each part its own codebook or generator network [ 54 , 35 , 41 , 25 , 28 ] , or slice each frame token’s channels into per-part groups with separate projections and heads, relating parts through a fixed mixing matrix [ 48 ] or a per-frame attention branch over those slices [ 47 ] rather than through part tokens. Concurrent work [ 50 ] finds eight learned part queries preferable to global and per-joint tokens for motion modelling over arbitrary skeletons. We place part tokens on the sequence axis of a single DiT, giving each hand, which carries $15$ of the $52$ joints, its own token and a nonlinear projector. Although coarser tokens are expected to degrade DiT quality [ 33 ] , that law concerns lossy patchification. A joint rotation is already a complete semantic unit, so anatomical grouping with part-specific decoders preserves per-joint resolution (Section 4.4 ).


### 3 Method

We formulate text-to-pose generation as Riemannian flow matching on the product manifold of SMPL-H joint rotations. Our approach combines two key components: (i) a geometry-aware flow-matching objective defined directly on $\mathrm{SO}(3)^{52}$ , which learns intrinsic tangent-space vector fields along geodesic paths, and (ii) a part-tokenized Diffusion Transformer that efficiently models whole-body articulation, including detailed hand articulation, through anatomical structure-aware tokenization.


#### 3.1 Pose Representation on $\mathrm{SO}(3)^{52}$

A SMPL-H pose is parameterised by 52 joint rotations: one global orientation, 21 body joint rotations, and $2\times 15$ finger rotations. We represent each joint rotation as an element of the special orthogonal group $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ , and the full pose as an element of the product manifold $\mathcal{M}=\mathrm{SO}(3)^{52}$ . Articulated rotations naturally admit a Lie-group structure with well-defined geodesics and tangent spaces, making them suitable for intrinsic flow matching. For each joint, the Riemannian exponential and logarithm are

[Tableau] $\displaystyle\mathrm{Exp}_{R}(v)$ $\displaystyle=R\,\exp([v]_{\times}),$ (1) $\displaystyle\mathrm{Log}_{R}(R^{\prime})$ $\displaystyle=[\log(R^{\top}R^{\prime})]_{\vee},$ (2)

where $[\cdot]_{\times}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathfrak{so}(3)$ is the skew-symmetric mapping and $[\cdot]_{\vee}$ its inverse. The geodesic distance is $d_{g}(R,R^{\prime})=\|\mathrm{Log}_{R}(R^{\prime})\|_{2}$ , and we extend these definitions to the product manifold by acting independently on each of the 52 joints. Importantly, the product-manifold formulation defines local rotational geometry but does not assume statistical independence between joints. Dependencies are modeled by the transformer vector field.


#### 3.2 Riemannian Flow Matching

> Figure 1 : Riemannian flow matching on the product manifold of joint rotations (schematic). Valid poses lie on a manifold $\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$ embedded in the ambient representation space. Noise $x_{0}\sim p_{0}$ is transported to data $x_{1}\sim p_{1}$ along the on-manifold geodesic (Eq. 3 ), whereas the Euclidean baseline follows the straight chord and leaves the manifold. At $x_{t}$ the network regresses the tangent velocity (Eq. 4 ), and integration stays on $\mathcal{M}$ .

> Figure 2 : PoseRieFlow architecture. Part-specific input projectors (linear for body parts, MLPs for the hands) map the noisy pose $R_{t}$ (52 joints, 6D-encoded) to eight anatomical part tokens with part-type embeddings. Each DiT block applies self-attention over the part tokens, cross-attention to frozen Flan-T5-Base text features, and a SwiGLU FFN, modulated by AdaLN conditioned on the timestep with zero-initialised gates. Mirrored decoders map each token back to its joints, yielding the per-joint tangent velocity $\hat{v}_{\theta}$ (Sec. 3.3 ).

Flow matching trains a time-dependent vector field $v_{\theta}$ to transport samples from a tractable prior $p_{0}$ to the data distribution $p_{1}$ along a prescribed interpolation path. Unlike Euclidean flow matching, which learns velocities in an ambient parameter space, we learn intrinsic tangent-space velocities on the manifold of rotations. On $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ , the prior $p_{0}$ on each joint is an isotropic Gaussian in the Lie algebra mapped to the group by the exponential map: we draw an axis-angle vector $\omega\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{3})$ in $\mathfrak{so}(3)\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}$ and set $R_{0}=\mathrm{Exp}_{I}(\omega)=\exp([\omega]_{\times})$ , with $\sigma=1$ radian throughout. The full-pose prior is the product of 52 such independent draws. Given a data sample $R_{1}$ and a prior sample $R_{0}\sim p_{0}$ , we connect them through the geodesic interpolant

[Tableau] $$R_{t}=\mathrm{Exp}_{R_{0}}\!\big(t\cdot\mathrm{Log}_{R_{0}}(R_{1})\big),\quad t\in[0,1].$$ (3)

Unlike linear interpolation in Euclidean rotation parameterizations, the geodesic interpolant remains on the manifold and follows the shortest rotational path between poses.

Rather than regressing Euclidean rotation updates, the model predicts the transport direction in the local tangent space of each joint. Writing the remaining rotation in the body frame at $R_{t}$ as $\omega=\mathrm{Log}_{R_{t}}(R_{1})=[\log(R_{t}^{\top}R_{1})]_{\vee}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$ , the geodesic velocity is

[Tableau] $$v_{\text{target}}(R_{t},R_{1},t)=\frac{\omega}{1-t}=\frac{\mathrm{Log}_{R_{t}}(R_{1})}{1-t},$$ (4)

where $\omega$ is the remaining rotation and $(1-t)$ is the remaining time to reach $R_{1}$ . The induced body-frame dynamics are $\tfrac{d}{dt}R_{t}=R_{t}\,[\omega/(1-t)]_{\times}$ . The network predicts a 3-dimensional tangent vector per joint, $\hat{v}_{\theta,j}(x_{t},t,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$ , for $j=1,\ldots,52$ , matching this target directly. Similar to other pose and motion generation models we encode the current point $R_{t}$ using the continuous 6D rotation representation [ 53 ] , which we denote $x_{t}$ , as input for the model, while all supervision and integration operate in tangent space.

We supervise the predicted tangent vectors with a mean-squared error objective on the product manifold,

[Tableau] $$\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{t,R_{0},R_{1},c}\!\left[\sum_{j=1}^{52}\big\|\hat{v}_{\theta,j}(x_{t},t,c)-v_{\text{target},j}\big\|_{2}^{2}\right].$$ (5)

The expectation is over $t\sim\mathcal{U}[0,0.999]$ , $R_{0}$ from the Riemannian-Gaussian prior, and $R_{1}$ with paired caption $c$ from the data. Operating in the tangent space yields gradient directions aligned with the geodesic structure of articulated rotations, in contrast to Euclidean flow matching that interpolates and regresses in $\mathbb{R}^{6}$ . Because geodesic interpolation follows valid rotational trajectories, the resulting vector field is more consistent with the pose geometry. For comparison, our Euclidean baseline replaces the manifold interpolant with linear interpolation in 6D space,

[Tableau] $$x_{t}=(1-t)x_{0}+tx_{1},$$

where $x_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ , and regresses the constant velocity $x_{1}-x_{0}$ . In this setting the network predicts $\hat{v}_{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{6}$ and is trained with a standard 6D MSE objective.


#### 3.3 Network Architecture

Naively treating each SMPL-H joint as an independent transformer token increases sequence length and attention cost, while weakening the modeling of correlated local structure, particularly in the hands. Instead, following part-token designs in discriminative skeleton and pose models [ 40 , 11 , 39 ] , we aggregate joints into eight semantic body-part tokens: torso (5 joints), head (1), left arm (4), right arm (4), left leg (4), right leg (4), left hand (15), and right hand (15). This granularity provides a favorable trade-off between attention efficiency and preservation of local articulation structure, particularly for dexterous hand articulation. Each part is mapped to a single $d$ -dimensional token by a part-specific projector applied to its concatenated joint inputs. Body parts use a single linear layer, while the two hands use a one-hidden-layer MLP with GELU activation to better model finger coordination and higher articulation complexity. A learned part-identity embedding is added to each part token. The eight part tokens form the transformer input sequence. After transformer processing, a part-specific decoder maps each part token back to its constituent joints (linear for body parts and a one-hidden-layer MLP for hands). The resulting per-joint outputs are scattered back into the canonical SMPL-H joint ordering. The output dimension is 3 per joint in the Riemannian setting (tangent vectors) and 6 in the Euclidean ablation.

We condition on natural language through cross-attention to a frozen Flan-T5-Base [ 7 ] encoder. For a given caption we extract the per-token features

[Tableau] $$\mathbf{E}_{\text{text}}=[\,\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{L}\,]\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times 768}$$

and project them once to the model dimension, $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}=\mathrm{LN}(\mathbf{E}_{\text{text}}\,W_{\text{text}})\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times d}$ . At 250M parameters the encoder is an order of magnitude smaller than the billion-scale text and vision-language backbones used by prior text-to-pose systems, yet sufficient for strong text-pose alignment. A pooled-conditioning variant and a larger $4$ B embedding encoder are ablated in Table 6 and shown to be inferior.

The part-token sequence $\mathbf{S}$ is processed by a stack of transformer blocks with adaptive LayerNorm (AdaLN) time conditioning. Each block comprises three sublayers. (i) Self-attention over the eight part tokens, which models long-range dependencies such as coordinated hand, arm, and torso configurations. (ii) Cross-attention , in which the part tokens attend to the text features $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$ , giving each part direct token-level access to the caption. (iii) A SwiGLU feed-forward network. The scale, shift, and gate parameters for all three sublayers are conditioned on the sinusoidal timestep embedding. The gates are zero-initialised so that each block initially behaves as the identity, which stabilizes optimization during training.


#### 3.4 Implementation Details

During training, we apply classifier-free guidance dropout with probability $0.1$ , replacing the text embedding with a learned null embedding optimized jointly with the model. At inference, classifier-free guidance is applied as

[Tableau] $$v_{\theta}^{\text{cfg}}=v_{\theta}(\emptyset)+w\cdot\big(v_{\theta}(\mathbf{e}_{\text{text}})-v_{\theta}(\emptyset)\big),$$

where $w$ denotes the guidance scale. We train using AdamW with learning rate $5\times 10^{-4}$ and a warmup-cosine learning-rate schedule. Sampling integrates the predicted vector field along geodesics on $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ using an explicit Euler scheme. Given the current state $R_{t}$ , the network predicts a body-frame tangent vector $\hat{v}_{\theta}$ , and updates

[Tableau] $$R_{t+\Delta t}\leftarrow R_{t}\exp([\hat{v}_{\theta}\Delta t]_{\times}),$$

with uniform step size $\Delta t=1/N$ . Sampling begins from the $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ Riemannian-Gaussian prior and proceeds for $N=50$ steps with classifier-free guidance weight $w=2.0$ . For fair comparison, all reported Euclidean and Riemannian results use identical NFE budgets unless stated otherwise. Further optimization settings and numerical-stability measures (mixed-precision handling and clamping in the logarithm and Gram-Schmidt maps) are provided in the supplementary material.

#### 4.1 Experimental Setup

We train and evaluate on PoseScript-A2H2, the union of the PoseScript automatic (A2) and human-written (H2) caption splits, which pairs SMPL-H poses from AMASS with natural-language descriptions. Our default models are trained on PoseScript-A2H2 alone, matching the data available to the PoseScript VAE. A training-data ablation (Section 4.4 ) additionally trains on a 300k-pose subset of Motion-X++ [ 51 ] , a whole-body motion dataset whose frame-level captions provide detailed hand articulation that PoseScript does not emphasise. Baseline results use the authors’ publicly released pretrained models. All methods are evaluated identically on the test split ( $N=19990$ ).

All generated and reference poses are first rotated about the vertical axis to a common heading. We measure text-pose alignment with the PoseScript retrieval model [ 8 ] and report R@1 in a 32-candidate gallery, the correct item plus 31 seeded distractors, with identical galleries for all methods, a chance level of $3.125\,\%$ , and exact ties counted as misses, in both the text-to-pose (T2P) and pose-to-text (P2T) directions, together with Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) in the retrieval model’s pose-embedding space as a measure of distributional realism. We report FID in the whole-body setting (Section 4.3 ), where it is computed on complete 52-joint poses. In the body-only projection, zeroing the hand joints degenerates the retrieval feature covariance and renders the FID estimate unstable, so we omit it there. We additionally report per-joint kinematic error against the reference pose: Procrustes-aligned mean per-joint position error (PA-MPJPE) in millimetres and mean geodesic rotation error (Geo) in degrees, where $\mathrm{Geo}_{\setminus 0}$ denotes the same error with the global orientation excluded. In the whole-body setting we further report Geo restricted to the 30 finger joints and fingertip PA-MPJPE summed over the ten fingertips (TipPA). Stochastic methods are sampled with ten seeds per caption and reported as mean $\pm$ std, and their diversity (Div) is the mean pairwise PA-MPJPE between multiple samples, averaged over the test set.

We compare against the PoseScript conditional VAE [ 8 ] , the recent LLM-based generators ChatPose [ 15 ] , PoseLLaVA [ 14 ] , and UniPose [ 23 ] . PoseRieFlow is reported in two variants that share architecture, parameter count, and training budget and differ only in the flow formulation: Ours (Riem.) , the Riemannian model with geodesic interpolation and tangent-space targets, and Ours (Eucl.) , the Euclidean ablation that interpolates and regresses in $6$ D. This pairing isolates the contribution of the manifold geometry at matched compute. Recent text-to-pose systems increasingly build upon large vision–language models containing several billion parameters ( $13.43$ B for ChatPose, $8.23$ B for UniPose, and $7.64$ B for PoseLLaVA), whereas PoseRieFlow uses a standalone 21.6M-parameter generator. We therefore compare not only generation quality but also the effectiveness of geometry-aware whole-body modeling at substantially smaller scale.

A central objective of PoseRieFlow is the generation of full SMPL-H articulation, including the 30 finger joints omitted by most existing text-to-pose methods. To evaluate this setting, all retrieval-based metrics are recomputed for all 52 joints. To our knowledge, PoseScript is the only prior text-to-pose method that produces SMPL-H output and provides a directly comparable baseline in this setting, as existing LLM-based approaches operate exclusively on body joints. Table 2 therefore evaluates the methods applicable to whole-body generation. Across the full 52-joint skeleton, PoseRieFlow attains the lowest PA-MPJPE among all applicable methods, with the Riemannian variant reaching $111.5$ mm compared to $116.4$ mm for the Euclidean variant and $206.5$ mm for the PoseScript VAE. Against its matched Euclidean counterpart, the Riemannian variant improves both kinematic metrics (PA-MPJPE $111.5$ versus $116.4$ mm, Geo $10.32^{\circ}$ versus $10.65^{\circ}$ ), and the advantage remains consistent across all practical inference budgets (Section 4.4 ), suggesting that the gain is systematic rather than an artifact of a particular discretization. On the hands, both flow variants improve over the VAE by an order of magnitude or more (hand Geo $0.26^{\circ}$ versus $23.4^{\circ}$ , TipPA $13.4$ to $13.9$ versus $207$ mm), while between the variants fingertip PA-MPJPE improves from $13.85$ to $13.38$ mm and hand geodesic error is statistically tied (Table 2 ). A2H2 ground-truth hands are nearly identical across items, so these metrics chiefly reward producing plausible hands, which the VAE frequently fails to do. Distributional and retrieval metrics do not follow the kinematic trend: the Euclidean variant is marginally better on FID ( $0.904$ versus $0.944$ ) and in both retrieval directions ( $27.8$ versus $27.2\,\%$ T2P), while both flow variants improve upon the PoseScript VAE on every metric by a wide margin, with whole-body T2P retrieval roughly eight times higher ( $27.2\,\%$ versus $3.3\,\%$ ). Geometry-aware transport therefore buys kinematic fidelity at matched compute rather than uniformly improving every axis.

##### 4.4.1 Riemannian vs. Euclidean Flow

Our Riemannian and Euclidean variants share architecture, parameter count, training schedule, and inference budget, differing only in the interpolation path and regression target (Section 3.2 ). This makes the comparison the most direct test of the proposed contribution, isolating the effect of respecting rotational geometry from other factors. At the default budget of $N=50$ steps the Riemannian variant already attains lower geodesic rotation error than its Euclidean counterpart in both the body-only (Table 1 ) and whole-body (Table 2 ) settings, confirming that aligning the training signal with the geodesic structure of $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ improves rotational fidelity rather than changing the parameterisation. We further examine how this advantage depends on the inference budget by sweeping the number of function evaluations $N$ for both variants (Table 3 ). Across the practical range $N\in[5,100]$ , the Riemannian variant achieves lower geodesic error by $0.17^{\circ}$ to $0.36^{\circ}$ . In the one- and two-step regime ( $N\leq 2$ ), performance degrades sharply for both variants, and the ordering reverses: the geodesic prior’s wide angular support makes a single Euler step on $\mathrm{SO}(3)^{52}$ a poor geodesic approximation. Thus, PoseRieFlow yields rotational gains at moderate-to-low NFE ( $N\geq 5$ ) rather than in few-step settings. These gains are far outside sampling noise. Over ten seeds, per-seed standard deviations (Tables 1 and 2 ) are at most $\pm 0.02^{\circ}$ Geo, $\pm 0.19$ mm PA-MPJPE, $\pm 0.002$ FID, and $\pm 0.2$ pp recall, one to two orders of magnitude below the Riemannian-Euclidean kinematic gaps. A Euclidean variant with per-step renormalization to the rotation manifold does not close the gap either, and instead destabilizes integration ( $>44^{\circ}$ Geo for $N\geq 20$ ).

##### 4.4.3 Architecture, Conditioning, and Training Data

We ablate the remaining design choices (part tokenization, text conditioning, and Motion-X++ supervision) by varying one axis at a time from the default model (Tables 5 and 6 ). Replacing the eight part tokens with per-joint tokenization at matched data yields a trade-off rather than a uniform loss: part tokenization wins both kinematic metrics (PA-MPJPE $111.6$ vs. $114.1$ mm, Geo $10.31^{\circ}$ vs. $10.60^{\circ}$ ), while per-joint attention is slightly better on FID and retrieval at $6.5\times$ the sequence length. The batch-matched pair (the PS+MX++ rows) shows the same kinematic pattern ( $112.6$ vs. $117.9$ mm, $10.54^{\circ}$ vs. $10.85^{\circ}$ ). Part tokenization therefore improves the kinematic fidelity targeted by our objective while reducing the attention sequence length from $52$ to $8$ tokens. Replacing all-token cross-attention with a pooled text embedding substantially degrades alignment and reconstruction regardless of encoder capacity (Table 6 ). Swapping the $4$ B embedding encoder for the 250M Flan-T5-Base under pooled conditioning barely changes any metric, while enabling cross-attention at the same encoder lifts T2P recall from $17.5$ to $27.4\,\%$ and reduces PA-MPJPE by $53$ mm. The conditioning pathway, rather than encoder capacity, is the bottleneck. Adding Motion-X++ at fixed architecture does not improve reconstruction: it costs PA-MPJPE and geodesic error at both architectures ( $112.6$ vs. $111.6$ mm and $10.54^{\circ}$ vs. $10.31^{\circ}$ at the part architecture) and improves only the retrieval directions there. PoseScript contains roughly 100k poses, small relative to the scale typical of flow-based generative models, and Motion-X++ supplies additional whole-body coverage. Its automatically generated captions differ stylistically from the PoseScript descriptions our retrieval metrics are built on, so we cap its contribution at 300k poses. The absence of a kinematic gain indicates that the primary benefit of PoseRieFlow arises from the model formulation rather than increased training data, and we keep PoseScript-only training as the default.


### 5 Conclusion

We presented PoseRieFlow , a Riemannian flow matching model for text-conditioned whole-body pose generation. By formulating the flow on the product manifold $\mathrm{SO}(3)^{52}$ with constant-velocity geodesic targets, training a compact Diffusion Transformer with AdaLN time conditioning and cross-attention to Flan-T5-Base features, we outperform far larger LLM-based baselines on kinematic fidelity while modelling the full SMPL-H articulation. Ablations show that the Riemannian formulation consistently improves rotational and joint-position fidelity over Euclidean flow matching at matched compute across inference budgets and guidance weights, that token-level cross-attention rather than encoder capacity drives text alignment, and that part tokenization improves kinematic fidelity while cutting attention cost relative to per-joint tokenization.

Our captions and evaluation depend on PoseScript-style descriptions and Motion-X++’s automatically generated whole-body pose descriptions. Even PoseScript’s human-written captions are detailed, body-part-relative descriptions written to discriminate the pose from distractors, so performance on free-form colloquial requests is not characterised, and dataset biases in AMASS and Motion-X++ may propagate to generated poses. We do not explore learned or adaptive groupings, nor granularities between eight parts and per-joint tokenization. We do not model body shape, facial expression, or contact constraints. As with any generative pose system, misuse risks arise if outputs are linked to identity or surveillance applications. Natural next steps include extending the flow to body shape, integrating contact priors as gradient guidance, and combining PoseRieFlow with a multimodal LLM front-end [ 23 ] for caption generality at compact compute.
