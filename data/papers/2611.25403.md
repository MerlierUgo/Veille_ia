We report the development of Antom 3-in-1 Transformer , a payment foundation model that integrates sequence , tabular , and graph representations for payment risk detection. The model contains approximately 7B parameters, with approximately 1.7B activated per task route. Antom 3-in-1 Transformer is pretrained to learn reusable behavioral and relational representations, primarily on Ant International’s internal payment data. In a cross-business transfer experiment, pretraining improves the target business metric by 34.5% over the same architecture trained from scratch. With only 25% of the training labels, the pretrained model outperforms the full-label scratch baseline in Average Precision (AP) and Recall@0.5% on different merchant domains. ### 1. Introduction

Large-scale payment platforms need to continuously detect fraud across different markets and business scenarios. Ant International has accumulated substantial transaction data and experience in risk prevention as its business has expanded. Antom , a brand of Ant International, is a unified merchant payment and digitalisation platform serving businesses of all sizes across Asia and worldwide. User behavior, transaction processes, and data distributions differ across scenarios, while fraudsters continually adjust their tactics and create new forms of risk ( He et al., 2026 ) . These changes increase the work required to maintain effective risk systems across markets. They motivate a transaction foundation model that generalizes across scenarios and tactics and supports the continued prevention of complex payment risks.

Research on transaction foundation models has made substantial progress ( Zuo et al., 2026 ; Han et al., 2026 ) . TransactionGPT learns transaction trajectories through autoregressive modeling and combines transaction metadata with downstream features ( Dou et al., 2025 ) . PRAGMA uses masked reconstruction to learn transferable representations from heterogeneous banking events and user attributes. PANTHER combines generative pretraining with contrastive learning and applies behavioral representations to business decisions ( Li et al., 2025 ) . These approaches develop rich representations of individual histories. Payment risk also depends on relationships across accounts and payment objects. In its anti-money-laundering analysis, PRAGMA identifies the difficulty of capturing highly relational patterns through independent history encoding ( Ostroukhov et al., 2026 ) . The same transaction can also present different signals from different perspectives: an ordinary-looking user history may coexist with an associated network location or device that carries high-risk context. Jointly modeling these perspectives gives risk assessment a more complete transaction context ( Jiang et al., 2026 ) .

Based on these observations, we propose Antom 3-in-1 Transformer, a foundation model for card-payment risk that integrates transaction sequences, scenario-specific tabular features, and entity relationships. It has approximately 7B total parameters, with approximately 1.7B activated for a single task route. The model combines behavioral histories, transaction fields, and user relations that specialized risk models often process separately. Large-scale pretraining learns transaction behavior and entity relations; business adaptation connects this knowledge to the objectives and decision rules of each business.

The resulting foundation supports cross-scenario reuse, learning with limited labels, and behavioral analysis. In a transfer experiment, adapting an existing source-domain foundation to a new business improves the business metric by 34.5% over the same architecture trained from scratch. With only 25% of the training labels, the pretrained model outperforms the full-label scratch baseline in Average Precision (AP) and Recall@0.5% on both merchant domains. A shared-checkpoint study evaluates adaptation across Antom-Merchant A, Antom-Merchant B and AlipayHK. Pretrained representations also distinguish evasion and account-nurturing behavior. These studies examine the reusable behavioral capabilities contributed by pretraining. Joint-model comparisons then show how behavioral and relational information improves risk detection when combined with scenario features. In production, Antom 3-in-1 Transformer reduces overall loss rate by 37.88% relative to XGB-Online at the same 0.5% intervention budget.

The model is pretrained through sequence contrastive learning and self-supervised learning for graph relationship, respectively, primarily on Ant International’s internal payment data. Subsequent supervised fine-tuning adapts the model to scenario-specific risk objectives across Antom and other Ant International businesses.

### 3. Methodology Overview

We introduce Antom 3-in-1 Transformer , a payment foundation model that integrates sequence, tabular, and graph representations for payment risk detection. Figure 1 presents the architecture of Antom 3-in-1 Transformer. The sequence modality learns behavioral patterns from transaction histories. The tabular modality models interactions among transaction attributes, business states, and engineered features through self-attention. The graph modality captures relationships among users by aggregating their profile and connected neighborhoods . Each modality has a corresponding encoder. Task-conditioned fusion combines sequence, tabular and graph representations for each prediction task.

Sequence Encoder. The sequence encoder models the transaction histories associated with the current payment. Event tokenizer embeds each event through its amount, categorical fields, and elapsed time, then uses a transformer to summarize the history. User, card, and device histories follow this procedure with separate encoders. The sequence encoders are pretrained by prefix-future contrast , which matches each historical prefix with a statistical summary of its subsequent activity. Contrastive candidates with similar coarse activity profiles encourage the encoder to learn finer behavioral differences without risk labels.

Tabular Encoder. The tabular encoder embeds features commonly used in payment-risk assessment, including current-transaction attributes and business statistics. The modeling procedure of tabular modality includes two stages: field tokenization embeds each value, and field interaction uses self-attention to combine information across fields. The resulting representations describe both individual fields and the transaction as a whole.

Graph Encoder. The graph encoder models users through their account profiles and their connections to other users. We construct rolling user graphs from transactions in dynamic time interval. Each user node initializes from an embedding of its account. The graph encoder is pretrained by combining contrastive agreement between edge-perturbed views of the same user with reconstruction of relation counts. The objectives preserve neighborhood consistency and relational information without supervision.

Task-Conditioned Fusion. For task-conditioned fusion, the model is optimized for a payment-risk prediction task together with a set of auxiliary business tasks. We associate each task with a learnable vector, referred to as a task query. Learnable task queries aggregate information through shared attention, and the resulting task representations are used for expert routing. Downstream fine-tuning adapts Antom 3-in-1 Transformer to supervised risk and auxiliary tasks. It updates the sequence encoder and trains the tabular encoder, task-conditioned fusion module, experts, and prediction heads. The graph encoder is frozen and supplies user representations aligned with each transaction’s time.


### 4. Experiments

We evaluate Antom 3-in-1 Transformer through overall risk-prediction performance, production deployment, and reuse across businesses and tasks. We then examine sequence pretraining, graph pretraining, task-conditioned fusion, followed by modality ablations and behavioral case studies.


#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

Datasets. We use five groups of datasets as follows.

- • Industrial datasets. We use sampled card-payment transactions from multiple business scenarios at Ant International. Each transaction has tabular features, user/card/device sequences, and user graph information.

- • IBM dataset. IBM’s synthetic credit-card dataset ( Padhi et al., 2021 ) contains 24,386,900 transactions.

- • Low-label and cross-scenario datasets. These card-payment transaction datasets are sampled from core business of Ant International, including Antom-Merchant A, Antom-Merchant B, and AlipayHK (HK). Antom-Merchant A represents a global e-marketplace, while Antom-Merchant B is a pooled cohort of merchants spanning e-commerce, digital entertainment, travel and aviation, AI services, and other industries. AlipayHK is another important business for Ant International. Table 10 summarizes the three evaluation domains.

- • Graph datasets. Amazon and YelpChi are multi-relation review graphs ( Dou et al., 2020 ) , T-Finance is a financial transaction graph ( Tang et al., 2022 ) , and Elliptic is a Bitcoin transaction-flow graph ( Weber et al., 2019 ) . Their statistics appear in Table 11 .

- • Fusion datasets. We use Sampled Industrial, TransXion, IBM CCT, and MBD. Appendix B.1 describes their inputs and splits (Table 12 ).

Baselines. Online risk prediction compares Antom 3-in-1 Transformer with XGB-Online ( Chen and Guestrin, 2016 ) and the SAINT-style production model ( Somepalli et al., 2021 ) . Public transaction baselines include FATA-Trans, TabBERT-LSTM, PANTHER, TransactionGPT, and PRAGMA ( Zhang et al., 2023b ; Padhi et al., 2021 ; Dou et al., 2025 ; Ostroukhov et al., 2026 ; Li et al., 2025 ) .

Sequence pretraining compares PFC with CoLES, BYB, and CPC ( Babaev et al., 2022 ; Wu et al., 2025 ; van den Oord et al., 2018 ) , alongside training from scratch.

Graph augmentation comparisons include tabular-only and graph-only models, concatenation, graph-token fusion, logit stacking, and residual predictors.

Fusion comparisons use a fixed model reference (denoted as B1), score-only fusion (denoted as B2), concatenation, sum-pooling, and learned fusion methods: CE, MMT, UMI, $I^{2}M^{2}$ , MMPareto, $I^{2}$ MoE, and BALM ( Liu et al., 2019 ; Gabeur et al., 2020 ; Zhang et al., 2023a ; Madaan et al., 2024 ; Wei and Hu, 2024 ; Xin et al., 2025 ; Nguyen et al., 2026 ) .

Model and comparison settings. The industrial configuration contains approximately 6.92B parameters, with approximately 1.74B activated per task route. Sequence pretraining objectives are compared using a common encoder, pretraining data, and training budget. Graph and fusion comparisons follow their respective matched evaluation protocols.

Metrics. We evaluate ranking quality offline with three metrics and business impact online with two loss rates. Average precision (AP) summarizes precision over the risk ranking. AUROC measures class separation across score thresholds. Top-budget recall R@ $q$ is the share of risk events captured among the highest-scoring $q$ fraction of transactions; we emphasize R@0.5%, the production intervention budget. Graph benchmarks report AUPRC ( Tang et al., 2023 ) . For online assessment, the amount-based chargeback (CB) rate divides chargeback amount by successful transaction amount. The overall loss rate uses the same denominator and counts the realized financial loss after chargeback-dispute recoveries. Appendix A defines supplementary metrics.


#### 4.2. Performance Overview

We assess overall performance in two settings: Industrial online A/B test against the previous risk models; Open-source comparison with other pretrained models and transaction foundation models.

Industrial Online A/B test. From June 8 to October 14, 2026, the online evaluation covered 30% of platform transactions. Transactions were randomly assigned to Antom 3-in-1 Transformer and two production models: XGB-Online, which periodically refits gradient-boosted trees, and the SAINT-style model. The three arms used the same business policies and intervention budget. Across the evaluated payment businesses, Antom 3-in-1 Transformer reduces the pooled CB rate by 37.74% and the overall loss rate by 37.88% relative to XGB-Online (Table 1 ). Relative to SAINT-style, the reductions are 28.26% and 24.07%.

#### 4.3. Industrial Implementation

Antom 3-in-1 Transformer runs inside Ant International’s real-time risk-control workflow. Every card payment triggers one scoring request: the feature platform assembles the model inputs, a GPU model service computes the risk score, and the strategy platform turns the score into a decision. The sequence and tabular encoders, task-conditioned fusion, and experts run online for each request, whereas graph embeddings are computed offline and only looked up at scoring time.

Online system. A payment event first enters the risk-control system. The feature platform computes the tabular features of the current transaction, retrieves the preceding user, card, and device histories, and looks up the latest eligible graph embedding of the user. History retrieval typically takes 50–60 ms. The model service encodes the assembled inputs and returns a risk score. The strategy platform combines the score with the active rules and intervention budget and returns the risk decision. The complete workflow must finish within a 250 ms end-to-end budget, which covers feature access, model-service communication and inference, and the final decision (Figure 2 ).

A horizontal time axis from the payment event to the risk decision, with the feature platform, the model service, and the strategy platform in sequence, their monitored latencies, and the 120 ms and 250 ms budgets.

Inference performance. The model service runs on an NVIDIA A10 GPU under a 120 ms service budget. Online monitoring records a mean latency of 46.66 ms, a P95 of 55.86 ms, and a P99 of 80.16 ms.


#### 4.4. Foundation Model Capabilities

We examine how a pretrained foundation reduces the labels needed for a new business, transfers to other business scenarios and explore how the foundation model could benefit other tasks.

Low-label adaptation. To evaluate label efficiency, we fine-tune the pretrained model and the same architecture trained from scratch with 1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, and 100% of the training labels, keeping the validation set fixed. With 25% of the labels, the pretrained model already exceeds scratch training with all labels on both Antom-Merchant A and Antom-Merchant B (Figure 3 ). On Antom-Merchant A, it reaches 0.5246% AP and 8.33% R@0.5%, against 0.4205% and 7.81% for full-label scratch; on Antom-Merchant B, it reaches 0.6928% and 10.16%, against 0.6370% and 8.11%. Pretraining also flattens the label curve: with 25% of the labels, the pretrained model retains 87% of its full-label recall on Antom-Merchant A and 71% on Antom-Merchant B, compared with 73% and 62% for scratch training.

> Figure 3. Label efficiency on Antom-Merchant A and Antom-Merchant B. Points and error bars show the mean and standard deviation over three fine-tuning runs. Four plots show AP and recall at a 0.5 percent intervention budget for the pretrained model and scratch training across five training-label budgets on AliExpress and ANTOM, with a dashed line for full-label scratch.

HK is the most label-scarce business, with 732 positive training examples, so we evaluate it at 10% and 100% of the labels only. With 10% of the labels, the pretrained model reaches 0.8214% AP and 27.75% R@0.5%, well above scratch training with all labels (0.1148% and 10.41%). Across the evaluated budgets, pretraining improves both metrics over same-budget scratch on HK and Antom-Merchant B.

Cross-scenario application. To evaluate the cross-scenario capability, we first fine-tune a pretrained model on a target business whose histories were excluded from pretraining. The pretrained model and its scratch counterpart share the architecture, target labels, fine-tuning budget, and selection rule. Pretraining improves the target business metric by 34.5% and AUROC by 0.0518 over scratch. The gain does not depend on a few risk-dense accounts: after removing the accounts with the highest concentration of risk events, the business-metric gain remains 34.2%.

We then test how the pretraining data source affects transfer. Three checkpoints of the same size and budget are pretrained on Antom-Merchant A histories only, on Antom-Merchant B histories only, and on an equal mixture of both. Each checkpoint is fine-tuned on each target business with 10% or 25% of its labels (Table 4 ). Pretraining only on the other business already improves AP and R@0.5% over scratch in all four target–budget settings. For Antom-Merchant B with 10% labels, Antom-Merchant A histories help even more than Antom-Merchant B’s own (0.5791% vs. 0.4857% AP; 8.67% vs. 6.13% R@0.5%). Histories in the Antom-Merchant B cohort are shorter on average, with 7.8 events compared with 29.5 for Antom-Merchant A. Thus, richer histories from another scenario can partly substitute for in-domain ones. With 25% labels, mixing the two sources beats target-only pretraining on both metrics for both targets.

### 5. Related Work


#### 5.1. Transaction Risk Modeling

Transaction risk models have moved from hand-crafted features toward sequential and graph-based learning. Early systems feed manually designed statistics, such as velocity features, into tabular learners such as gradient-boosted trees ( Chen and Guestrin, 2016 ; Prokhorenkova et al., 2018 ) . Later work models transaction histories directly with sequential networks ( Jurgovsky et al., 2018 ; Zhang et al., 2023b ) , or connects accounts, devices, and merchants into graphs and applies graph neural networks ( Liu et al., 2018 ; Wang et al., 2019 ; Dou et al., 2020 ; Liu et al., 2021 ; Tang et al., 2022 ) . Recent advances in large language models have spurred the development of transaction foundation models ( Dou et al., 2025 ; Ostroukhov et al., 2026 ) .


#### 5.2. Pretrained Transaction Foundation Models

Transaction pretraining uses two main forms of supervision: event reconstruction learns dependencies among heterogeneous fields through masking or next-event prediction, as in TabBERT, FATA-Trans, TransactionGPT, and PRAGMA ( Padhi et al., 2021 ; Zhang et al., 2023b ; Dou et al., 2025 ; Ostroukhov et al., 2026 ) ; temporal learning instead relates segments of a history: CoLES contrasts subsequences, while CPC and BYB predict future representations ( Babaev et al., 2022 ; van den Oord et al., 2018 ; Wu et al., 2025 ) . Transaction foundation models extend these objectives with profile information and reusable representations for multiple business tasks ( Li et al., 2025 ; Zhao et al., 2026 ) .

Despite their achievements in transaction modeling, they mainly capture transaction features from a single perspective. Antom 3-in-1 Transformer uses three modalities to provide a more comprehensive view of transactions.


### 6. Conclusion

We presented Antom 3-in-1 Transformer, a payment foundation model applied on billion-scale industrial payment systems, which combines transaction sequences, scenario-specific tabular features, and entity relationships for card-payment risk. Sequence modality captures behavioral evolution, tabular modality models contextual feature interactions, and graph modality learns structure of user relationships. Antom 3-in-1 Transformer has been deployed on Ant International’s industrial system since June 2026 and reduces overall loss rate by 37.88% relative to current internal fraud detection system. Antom 3-in-1 Transformer demonstrates promising generalization capabilities across diverse scenarios and tasks. The technologies developed in this work have also strengthened Ant International’s overall risk management capabilities.

### Appendix A Metrics and Comparison Conventions


#### A.1. Risk Ranking at a Fixed Budget

Let $s_{i}$ be the risk score, $y_{i}\in\{0,1\}$ the confirmed label, and $w_{i}$ the population weight of payment $i$ . A complete evaluation population uses $w_{i}=1$ ; a sampled population uses inverse-inclusion weights. At intervention fraction $q$ , let $a_{i}(q)\in[0,1]$ indicate the fraction of payment $i$ selected by the score ranking. Nonboundary payments have $a_{i}(q)\in\{0,1\}$ . In the sampled evaluations, payments tied at the budget boundary share a fractional allocation satisfying

[Tableau] (1) $$\sum_{i}a_{i}(q)w_{i}=q\sum_{i}w_{i},\qquad\mathrm{R@}q=\frac{\sum_{i}a_{i}(q)w_{i}y_{i}}{\sum_{i}w_{i}y_{i}}.$$

This keeps the selected population weight fixed when identical histories receive identical scores.

Average precision integrates precision over the empirical recall increments:

[Tableau] (2) $$\mathrm{AP}=\sum_{j}(R_{j}-R_{j-1})P_{j},$$

where $P_{j}$ and $R_{j}$ are the weighted precision and recall after including the $j$ -th score group. AP is computed without interpolation using the weighted precision–recall curve. AUROC evaluates ranking over all positive–negative pairs; R@ $q$ evaluates the portion of that ranking used for intervention. The public benchmark’s F1 threshold is selected on validation data and then fixed.


#### A.2. Business Metrics and Normalized Results

Some business studies report an anonymized operating metric $M$ against a reference $M_{0}$ . We retain the metric and reference associated with each study. A relative gain and a normalized index are, respectively,

[Tableau] (3) $$\Delta_{\mathrm{rel}}=100\left(\frac{M}{M_{0}}-1\right)\%,\qquad I=100\frac{M}{M_{0}}.$$

An index of 120 therefore denotes a relative gain of 20%. A difference between percentage-valued rates is reported in percentage points. Absolute AUROC changes are labeled as absolute differences.

The source-domain transfer study compares a pretrained encoder with scratch training on the same target-business labels. Its reported business-metric changes are +34.5% for the full cohort and +34.2% after removing high-concentration accounts. The joint-model study compares models at the same intervention volume and reports gains over the baseline. These references remain separate throughout the report.

Business metrics are reported as within-study normalized indices or relative gains. The tables identify their reference models; the joint-model comparison additionally fixes the intervention volume. AP, fixed-budget recall, and AUROC retain their explicit definitions above.


#### A.3. Additional Captured Risk Events(AddCB)

For the joint-model comparison and modality ablation, let $A_{q}$ and $B_{q}$ be the payment sets selected by the developed model and existing baselines at the same volume. The share of captured risk events missed by the baseline is

[Tableau] (4) $$\mathrm{AdditionalCoverage}(q)=\frac{\sum_{i\in A_{q}\setminus B_{q}}y_{i}}{\sum_{i\in A_{q}}y_{i}}.$$

It describes the share of its captured risk events outside the baseline’s selection.
