## Video Generation Meets Agent Harness Agentic Video Generation, Agentic World Modeling and Recursive Self-Improvement

Abstract: Video generation is evolving from end-to-end synthesis toward agentic generation, in which planning, tool use, and feedback produce coherent multi-shot videos with greater control. Meanwhile, video world models provide action-conditioned observations and predicted futures and can serve as interactive environments for agents. We study this intersection through the agent harness : the executable runtime that constructs an agent’s context, exposes tools, maintains state, routes feedback, and enforces budgets around models such as video generators. The report is organized around three directions. First, we study agentic video generation, where harnesses coordinate script and shot planning, asset management, shot production, and editing, and we discuss what the evaluation of such systems must separate. Second, we examine agentic world modeling, where harnesses mediate observation, control, execution, and state, including designs in which a game engine or a program written by a coding agent holds the world state while the video model renders it, and where agents and world models test and improve one another. Third, we examine recursive self-improvement (RSI) for video model development: we separate revisions of a current output from updates to harness state, generator parameters, and evaluators, and from changes to the procedure that produces later updates, and we identify the channels through which an improvement could re-enter development. Across these directions, we analyze the agent capabilities that video imposes, from audiovisual perception, long-horizon planning, and memory to interaction through code and computer use, feedback-guided control, skill reuse, and multi-agent organization. We conclude with open challenges, including autonomous video production, lifelong memory, programmable hybrid control, autonomous model development, provenance and cost accounting, and evaluation that links video quality and task performance to sustained improvement of models and harnesses.

### 1 Introduction


#### 1.1 Motivation

Video generators produce increasingly realistic, controllable, and temporally coherent clips [ kong2024hunyuanvideo ] . A generator alone, however, does not organize the decisions needed to complete a video task. A creative brief must become coherent shots; references must persist across scenes; takes must be inspected; edits must preserve what is correct [ huang2026vimax , vista2025 ] . Interactive video must also support continuing actions grounded in observations, not just a finished clip [ gao2026lingbotworld2 ] . These requirements motivate a transition from video generation to agentic video generation: a goal-directed process in which generated results inform subsequent decisions. An agent can revise a plan, retrieve a reference, select a tool, or localize a failure in response to actual outcomes [ li2026videogenagent ] . Agentic video generation denotes a system-level perspective, not a new generator architecture: the video model provides synthesis or predicted transitions, while the agent makes consequential choices around those capabilities. Video couples these choices over time. References affect later shots [ chen2026cinecrew ] , actions change subsequent observations, and local repairs can disrupt temporal consistency elsewhere. Plausible footage may still violate a narrative requirement or fail to realize an intended action, so a quality score alone rarely specifies what to do next [ liu2024videogenerationevaluation , ding2026playworld ] . Effective control requires connecting observations to localized decisions while maintaining project or interaction state and accounting for generation cost.

The development from large language models (LLMs) to agents offers a foundation for this control. Rather than only generating a response, an LLM can operate within a cycle of reasoning, tool use, and observation, as demonstrated by ReAct [ yao2023react ] . Planning and memory extend this cycle to tasks requiring multiple decisions [ weng2023agents ] . Making these capabilities operational requires an agent harness: the executable runtime around the model that constructs its context, exposes tools, maintains state, routes feedback, and enforces permissions and budgets [ weng2026harness , ning2026code ] (Section ). For video generation, the harness is where a creative brief or an interactive goal becomes executable operations on generators, editors, and world models. The central question shifts from generating a good clip to reliably completing a video task through feedback. Agentic video generation and video world-model interaction are two concrete settings. A further question is whether experience from these tasks can improve the system that will perform future tasks.

Feedback changes different objects at different timescales. Prompt revision or take replacement targets the current artifact [ vista2025 ] ; retaining a skill targets subsequent tasks [ wei2026videoweaver ] ; updating a dataset, critic, or generator targets later model versions [ liu2025videoalign ] . These outcomes should not be conflated: effective repair of a current video does not by itself establish improvement in future capability. Beyond repairing individual outputs, context engineering considers how instructions, retrieved evidence, memory, and execution records are selected and organized for subsequent decisions [ weng2026harness ] . Agentic Context Engineering (ACE), for example, accumulates and revises a structured context from execution feedback rather than updating model weights [ zhang2025ace ] . Such work shifts attention from a current response toward reusable knowledge, while leaving open whether the mechanisms that construct and update that knowledge can themselves improve.

Recent work on automated research extends these ideas to model development. The AI Scientist links idea generation, coding, experiments, and reporting [ lu2024aiscientist ] ; autoresearch runs a bounded loop of training code modification, experimentation, and validation-based acceptance [ karpathy2026autoresearch ] . These workflows connect proposals to empirical feedback, but automating a fixed experiment loop is not the same as improving the loop itself. Research on self-improving agents, exemplified by the Darwin Gödel Machine and Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement (RRSI), explores changes to the agent’s own code or harness [ zhang2025dgm , xia2026rrsi ] . Such work motivates examining the processes that generate and validate subsequent improvements.

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) asks whether a system can improve the procedures that produce further improvements [ chen2026surveyr04 , duan2026surveyn21 ] . Video-specific targets include failure diagnosis, data collection, supervision construction, update selection, and candidate validation. Repeating a fixed collection-and-training pipeline is iterative improvement. The recursive direction additionally allows evidence to revise that pipeline, changing how a later round discovers and addresses weaknesses. An agent harness provides an executable surface for this direction. Its context construction, tool orchestration, memory, testing workflow, and update-selection rules can be inspected and, where authorized, revised [ weng2026harness ] . This does not make every harness self-improving: a revision must be retained and independently evaluated on later tasks or improvement rounds under stated resource constraints [ xia2026rrsi ] .

Existing video research supplies parts of this connection: feedback can revise reusable production skills [ wei2026videoweaver ] , uncertainty can guide data acquisition for adaptation [ savov2025autoexplore ] , and validation can govern critic renewal for later training [ yang2026dualipo ] . These are building blocks rather than evidence that one system has completed the full RSI loop. Our survey therefore connects three questions: how harnesses organize agentic video generation, how they support video world-model interaction, and how they may drive video-generation development toward RSI. The first two establish task execution and sources of experience; the third examines persistent capability and the procedures used to improve it. #### 1.2 Contributions and Positioning

The survey addresses three questions about the influence of the agent harness on video generation (Figure 1 ), each corresponding to one core chapter.

How does the agent harness enable agentic video generation? Agentic Video Generation (Section ) studies how harnesses coordinate planning, execution, tool use, memory, editing, and watermarking around shared project state, and what the evaluation of such systems must separate. We examine how feedback changes generation and editing decisions and how those changes affect video quality, consistency, and efficiency.

How can the agent harness be integrated into video world models? Agentic World Modeling (Section ) locates where a harness can intervene in observation, control, execution, event organization, and state management, and examines designs in which an engine holds the world state while the video model renders it. We examine how these interventions support sustained interaction, control fidelity, state continuity, and agent-driven evaluation. The focus of this question is how a harness enables reliable interaction with a video world model.

How can video generation systems move toward recursive self-improvement? Toward RSI in Video Generation (Section ) examines how advances in video generators and the agent harness can feed back into the process that produces subsequent model improvements. We review foundation model improvement, agent harness improvement, and evaluation, and identify the channels through which gains in these components can strengthen failure diagnosis, data curation, training, and validation in later development rounds. The goal is to identify mechanisms and evidence needed for recursively improving development processes that can support next-generation video generation models.

These questions connect through feedback: agentic video generation and world interaction can expose failures, while development can turn selected evidence into changes deployed in later tasks. Section compares the shared agent capabilities that support all three settings.

Differentiating Our Work from Existing Surveys

Existing surveys provide complementary accounts of video generation. Work on spatiotemporal consistency examines generation models, representations, training strategies, and evaluation, emphasizing the mechanisms that maintain coherent video sequences [ yin2025spatiotemporal ] . Post-training and alignment surveys organize methods for adapting video generators to human intent, including supervised fine-tuning, distillation, preference optimization, and inference-time adaptation [ li2026videoposttraining ] . Evaluation surveys examine perceptual quality, semantic fidelity, and alignment with user instructions [ liu2024videogenerationevaluation ] . Together, these accounts explain how video models are built, improved, and assessed.

More directly related to our system perspective, agentic visual generation surveys organize the conditioning, execution, and adaptation decisions available to a controller across visual modalities [ huang2026agenticvisual ] , or study autonomous multimodal content creation and the construction of stateful artifacts [ xu2026ags , wang2026artifactcreation ] . Wu et al. place agentic generation and world-modeling generation on a single capability ladder of visual generation [ wu2026visualnewera ] . World model surveys characterize predictive and simulation capabilities across domains, focus on action conditioning, memory, and responsiveness in sustained interaction [ chu2026agenticworldmodel , liu2026interactiveworldmodel ] , or treat world models as interactive proxies that supply feedback to continually improving agents [ yang2026quovadis ] . Harness surveys analyze the execution layer around text and coding agents [ ning2026code , guo2026harnessdesign ] . Surveys of self-evolving agents and RSI examine evolutionary targets, adaptation stages, and feedback loops across general AI systems [ gao2025surveyr02 , chen2026surveyr04 , wu2026rsisurvey ] . These perspectives share concepts such as feedback, memory, and agent control, but none of them takes the harness around a video generator as the unit of analysis across production, interaction, and model development.

Our survey complements these perspectives with a framework centered on video generation that covers three settings in which a harness meets a video model: as a synthesizer in production, as an interactive environment, and as the object of development. The settings are linked through the harness: agents organize generation and refinement, while video models provide interactive experience that can inform agent decisions and further model development. Where capability ladders treat agentic generation and world modeling as successive stages of the generator [ wu2026visualnewera ] , we take the agent harness around the generator as the unit of analysis, so that one framework covers production, interaction, and development.

Our contributions are threefold.

- • A harness-centered framework. We define a common agent–harness execution loop (Section ) and apply it to production, organized by workflow function (Section ), and to world-model interaction, organized by the interfaces through which a harness intervenes, including designs in which an engine or a program holds the world state (Section ).

- • An account of improvement. We separate revisions of the current output from updates to harness state, generator parameters, and evaluators, and from changes to the development procedure. We identify four channels through which an improvement could re-enter video development. No surveyed system yet revises the procedure that trains and validates the generator (Section ).

- • Evaluation requirements and open problems. We state how the agent harness supports active testing and reciprocal evaluation of agents and world models (Section ), what the evaluation of agentic production must separate (Section ), which capabilities video imposes on agents (Section ), and which challenges remain (Section ).
